## How sales are projected

Each format has a design day: its products' daily volumes times their prices. The bakery-café uses every product's `par_daily`; the other formats use the menus and volumes listed under them in `proforma.yaml`. A format with sales channels gives each channel its own menu and trading days and averages them over its working days. A site's `sales_index` scales its format's design day to the site, and each scenario scales it again. The order counts divide the items sold by an assumed items per order, so treat them as rough. Year 1 ramps from 55% to 100% of the steady state, averaging 80%; Year 3 adds 3% of price growth. Food cost is the menu's cost at distributor prices plus 5.5 points for waste beyond the recipes' allowances, comps, staff meals and price drift. The burger and bakery adds 9.5 points instead.

Break-even is the daily sales at which Year-2 cash flow after debt service is zero. Payback is the startup cost divided by Year-3 EBITDA. The most base rent a site can carry is the rent at which Year-2 cash after debt service falls to zero, or, for an owner-run format, to the owner's target pay, with its NNN unchanged; an assumed rent is a placeholder until a broker quotes one. The site scan's rent test is base rent at or under 8% of sales; NNN charges come on top. Year-1 cash after debt has to fit inside the working capital reserve. The owner would put in up to $500,000 without a loan. A site paid for in cash has no debt service, so its EBITDA and its cash after debt are the same. Sites outside Boston use their market's wages, payroll burden, utilities, insurance and prices: see Markets below.

## Other assumptions

- **Days and hours:** bakery-café 360 days a year, 6:30 am to 3:00 pm, seven days; roll bakery 307 days a year, 7:00 am to 2:00 pm, Tuesday to Sunday; pop-up 190 days a year, Markets on Saturday and Sunday; pre-order pickup on Saturday; office orders Wednesday to Friday; roll kitchen 296 days a year, Window 7:00 am to 1:00 pm Thursday to Sunday (Friday to Sunday June to September); office orders Tuesday to Friday; markets at weekends; burger and bakery 360 days a year, 7:00 am to 9:00 pm, seven days; breakfast until 10:30.
- **Variable costs, % of sales:** card processing 2.6%, repairs maintenance 1.5%, supplies cleaning 1.5%, marketing 2.0%, delivery net 0.5%, miscellaneous 1.0%. The pop-up sets its own: marketing 3.0%, delivery net 0%, repairs maintenance 0.5%. The roll kitchen sets its own: marketing 3.0%, delivery net 0%. The burger and bakery sets its own: marketing 2.5%, delivery net 0%, repairs maintenance 2.0%.
- **Channel costs:** commissions, the share a fundraising group keeps and market stall fees are their own P&L line, set by channel under each format in `proforma.yaml`.
- **Fixed and flexing hours:** bakery-café 60% of paid hours fixed; roll bakery 60% of paid hours fixed; pop-up 50% of paid hours fixed; roll kitchen 60% of paid hours fixed; burger and bakery 60% of paid hours fixed; the rest flex with sales.
- **Fixed costs a year:** insurance $8,500, software pos $5,000, accounting payroll legal $9,000, licenses music $2,500, waste pest $7,200, linens uniforms $2,400, internet phone $2,400. Totals by format: bakery-café $37,000; roll bakery $27,300; pop-up $21,300; roll kitchen $42,200; burger and bakery $55,900.
- **Utilities:** $20 a sq ft a year.
- **Payroll burden:** 12% on all pay.
- **Financing:** bakery-café 70% from a 10-year loan at 10.5%; roll bakery paid in cash, with no loan; pop-up paid in cash, with no loan; roll kitchen paid in cash, with no loan; burger and bakery paid in cash, with no loan. The owner would put in up to $500,000.
- **Inflation:** wages, NNN and fixed costs rise 3% a year; base rent rises at each site's escalation.
- **Before opening:** rent and NNN are paid from lease signing to opening, for each site's months to open; no free rent is assumed.
- **Not included:** income taxes, depreciation, owner's pay in an owner-run format, owner's draw beyond the manager line in a managed one, and any landlord build-out allowance or free rent, which would lower the startup cost.
- **Production-kitchen labor:** formats marked `production_scale` count prep minutes at the database's production batch sizes (a 60-quart mixer run of 132 rolls), not its 36-roll test batches. Paid hours are set by role and don't depend on this count.

## Markets

The inputs above are Boston's. A site in another market replaces them with that market's own:

**Miami-Dade.** Florida's minimum wage reached $15.00 on September 30, 2026, the same as Massachusetts', so counter pay converges with Boston's; skilled baking pay runs about 5–10% lower. Florida has no paid-leave or sick-leave mandate and a lighter unemployment tax. Power costs about half Boston's per kWh, but the air conditioning runs all year.
