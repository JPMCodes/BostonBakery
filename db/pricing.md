| Blueberry Muffin | Large tulip baking cup, #6 kraft bag, kraft napkin | $0.11–$0.19 |
| Both salads | Clear 32 oz PET bowl and lid, 2 oz dressing cup and lid, wooden fork, kraft napkin, grab-and-go label | $0.28–$0.63 |
| Chicken salad and hummus scoops | 4 oz cup and lid, grab-and-go label | $0.04–$0.14 |

- **Box only what needs it:** glazed, frosted or fragile items, multi-packs and gifts. Sturdy, dry pastries go in a bag. Each box swapped for a bag saves about $0.28; at 100 pastries a day that's about $10,000 a year.
- **The roll's box is its biggest cost.** At distributor prices the box ($0.32) costs more than the roll's food ($0.29), and packaging is over half the roll's cost. A 6×6 sugarcane-fiber clamshell (about $0.15 at WebstaurantStore) would halve the packaging, at some cost to presentation.
- **The sandwich label does a job:** the sandwiches look alike once wrapped, so the label names which is which and seals the wrap.
- **Salads go in clear PET** so the bowl sells itself from the cold case. A kraft bowl with a clear lid (Solia, about $0.62) looks more upscale but nearly triples the $0.22 bowl, and was out of stock when checked. The label isn't optional: food packed on site for self-service generally needs its name, ingredients, allergens and a date under the Food Code.
- **Not costed:** a box for the 4-pack, and bags for multi-item orders.

- **At par with Flour's scones,** and a quarter under the cinnamon roll, which stays the hero.

More butter and less buttermilk keep fat and liquid within about 2 g of the crème fraîche version. Total cost drops from 25–37% of the price to 19–32%.

- **Dried blueberries are the biggest ingredient line,** at $0.16–$0.31 a scone.
- **Packaging is a kraft bag, not a box:** a scone is sturdy and unglazed, so the bag ($0.05–$0.09 with a napkin) does the job of the $0.34–$0.47 window box and saves about $0.29–$0.38 a scone.

## Sour Cream Coffee Cake Slice: $4.50 (draft)

| Comparable | Price | Note |
|---|---|---|
| The Original Boston Cinnamon Walnut Coffee Cake, 24 oz | $7.99 | Price Rite grocery listing ($0.33/oz), about $1.25 for a slice our size; the benchmark |
| Tatte cake slice | $5.50–$6.75 | Market research |
| Flour cake slice | $6.30 | Lemon raspberry; market research |
| Santia's cake slice | $6.50–$7.25 | Aggregator listing |
| Our Blueberry Scone and Cinnamon Roll | $4.50 and $4.75 | |

- **Level with the scone, under the layer cakes:** Flour and Tatte's frosted layer-cake slices run $5.50–$6.75. An unfrosted coffee cake slice sits $1.00 or more under them, at the scone's $4.50 and a quarter under the cinnamon roll.
- **Same cake, better ingredients:** the grocery cake's label leads with sugar, bleached flour, sour cream, soybean oil, walnuts, eggs, water and palm oil. Ours keeps the sour cream crumb, the sweetness and the heavy walnut-cinnamon ribbon, but uses butter (with a little sunflower oil for softness), unbleached pastry and all-purpose flour, real vanilla extract and bean paste, toasted walnuts, Saigon cinnamon and a brown-butter topping.
- **About 3.6 times the grocery cake by weight,** a bit above the 2–3 times the research found for a premium croissant over grocery. The quality gap is wider here too: a fresh all-butter bake against an oil-based shelf cake.
- **Biggest lines at distributor prices:** sour cream ($0.11 a slice), walnuts ($0.09) and butter ($0.08). The whole cake's ingredients cost $7.40–$12.00.
- **Keeps well:** sour cream, the high sugar and a little oil hold the crumb soft. Keep cakes whole under a cover and cut to order; best within 3 days of baking.
- **Walnuts are the menu's first tree nut.** They define the benchmark, but they bring tree nuts into the kitchen: store and scoop them apart from everything else, and decide whether to post a kitchen-wide tree-nut notice. Leaving them out makes a nut-free cinnamon version about $0.09 a slice cheaper.
- **Option:** whole cakes on pre-order at about $40 for 16 slices. The research found 8-inch bundts at $18.50–$35 and Flour's 8-inch cake at $57; ours is a 12-cup cake. The box isn't costed.

## Blueberry Muffin: $4.50 (draft)

| Comparable | Price | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Flour muffins | $4.50–$5.00 | Market research |
| Dunkin' muffin | $1.69 | 2022 aggregator listing; the chain tier |
| Our Blueberry Scone and Coffee Cake Slice | $4.50 | |

- **At Flour's low end, level with the scone and the coffee cake:** that makes $4.50 the house pastry price, with the cinnamon roll a quarter above as the hero.
- **The Jordan Marsh muffin, kept and upgraded:** the same creamed-butter method, about 80% sugar to flour, about 1.4 times the flour's weight in blueberries with a fifth of them mashed into the batter for the purple-streaked crumb, and a sugar crust. The upgrades are sour cream and a little oil for a crumb that stays moist through the day, real vanilla, a little lemon zest, turbinado sugar for a crunchier crust (the original uses granulated), and an overnight rest for taller domes.
- **Blueberries are the biggest cost:** about $0.30 a muffin at distributor prices ($3.50/lb), and more out of season.
- **The tulip liner earns its place:** at $0.06 it gives the bakery-case look and room for a tall dome. With the bag and napkin, packaging is $0.11, about a sixth of the muffin's cost.
- **Overnight batter, morning bake:** scooped and chilled the day before, the muffins go straight from the fridge into the oven, so the early shift only bakes.

- **Below the bakery-café band on purpose:** Boston bakery breakfast sandwiches run $9.50–$11.00, but each carries meat, greens or halloumi. This one is egg, cheddar and harissa on a house roll, so it sits $1.35–$2.50 under them and above the kids' and chain-promo tier.
- **Ticket:** with a latte ($4.50 at Flour, $4.85 at Tatte) the check is about $13, near Flour's $14.50 egg-sandwich-plus-latte check.
- **Fennel sausage SKU at $10.25:** $1.75 above the base sandwich. With meat on it, it joins the $9.50–$11.00 band: a quarter above Flour's $10.00 ham-or-bacon egg sandwich and $0.75 under La Saison's $11.00 Egg Bun. The extra quarter over $10.00 is pure margin, and with Boston's 7% meals tax it still rings up under $11 ($10.97). The 2 oz patty costs $0.36–$0.69. One reviewer reported paying $3 extra for sausage at Pavement, the high end of add-on pricing.
- **Why it matters:** breakfast sandwiches are Tatte's two most-liked DoorDash items, and in the one Boston bakery-café sales mix the research found, pastry and desserts were only 27% of sales.

## Lunch sandwiches: Harissa Chicken $12.50, Ham, Cheddar & Olive Tapenade $11.50 (draft)

| Boston comparable | Price | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Tatte chicken pita | $12.50–$13.00 | Its lunch sandwich in the research: roast chicken breast with a tomato, cucumber and red-onion salad |
| Sofra chicken shawarma and sausage pitas | $14.00 | |
| La Saison ham & cheese brioche | $9.50 | Cambridge, Toast listing |
| Elephantine ham & cheese croissant | $7.50 | DoorDash-run storefront |
| Flour kids' ham & cheese | $6.50 | Ham and cheddar on soft white focaccia |
| Flour BLT | $16.00 | Named by Flour's owner as a signature; left alone so we don't copy it |

- **Why these two:** the research has no sandwich sales counts. Roast chicken anchors lunch at Tatte and Sofra, and ham and cheese is the most widespread savory format in the research: Flour, La Saison, Elephantine and Colette (Parisian sandwiches) all sell a version.
- **Prices:** the chicken at Tatte's Wellesley pita price; the ham, cheddar and tapenade $1.00 under it, and above La Saison's smaller $9.50 ham & cheese brioche.
- **One dough, three looks:** the breakfast sandwiches keep the round, glossy 90 g bun. The lunch sandwiches use 120 g ovals, nigella-seeded for the chicken and cheddar-crusted for the ham. Nigella rather than sesame, because sesame seeds scatter across pans and would add a major allergen to the rolls. Wrapped to go, the label tells the sandwiches apart; the look matters on the plate and in photos.
- **Shared prep:** the chicken roasts with the chicken salad batch, the harissa is the breakfast sandwich's, and the arugula and red onion come from the salads. The ham sandwich needs no cooking beyond the melt. The tapenade is a 10-minute food-processor batch that keeps 2 weeks, made without anchovy so it adds no fish allergen. New are ham, Kalamata olives, capers, nigella seeds and red wine vinegar.
- **Ham is the cost lever:** at $1.59 a sandwich it's more than half the food cost, which puts this sandwich at 25% of its price at distributor prices, the highest of the four. A $7.30/lb Black Forest ham in place of the $8.50/lb hardwood-smoked would save about $0.22.
- **Runner-up:** a vegetarian sandwich built on the house hummus. Tatte pairs its chicken pita with a vegetarian feta-pistachio pita.

## Salad bowls: $12.50 and $11.50 (draft)

| Boston comparable | Counter price | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Tatte pita | $12.50–$13.00 | Chicken pita $13.00 |
| Mamaleh's nova lox | $13.75–$15.00 | |
| Sofra pita | $14.00 | |
| Tatte salad | $14.50–$15.00 | Copley runs $0.50 above Wellesley |
| Flour BLT | $16.00 | Flour's signature lunch sandwich |

- **Under Tatte's salads and pitas:** both bowls are meatless grab-and-go, so they sit $2.00–$3.50 under Tatte's $14.50–$15.00 salads and at or under its $12.50–$13.00 pitas. Each was cut by $1.00 on 2026-11-07 so a bowl with a scoop of chicken salad or hummus lands at $15.00–$17.00. The research puts Boston bakery-café lunch at $13–$16 and names it the lever that lifts the check past a coffee and a pastry.
- **Fennel Herb Salad Bowl at $12.50:** a dollar over the quinoa salad. It has the dearer ingredients (arugula, Parmesan, citrus, three fresh herbs), the most knife work, and it sells the same day only, so its cost carries a 10% waste allowance for bowls discarded at close.
- **Cilantro Lime Quinoa Salad at $11.50:** bigger (450 g) and cheaper to make, and it holds 2 days because it has no greens. Made without wheat, but not certified gluten-free, since the kitchen handles flour.
- **Labor sets these prices, not food cost:** at distributor prices, food and packaging are 19–22% of the price. Salads are knife work. Rough estimates, not yet timed, put prep at 6–8 minutes a fennel bowl and about 6 a quinoa salad in batches of 10: about $2.10–$2.80 a bowl at $21 an hour (the research's $18.54 BLS baker wage plus payroll costs). Food, packaging and prep together come to about 37–44% of the price. The fennel bowl's labor is in the orange segments, the shaved fennel and the three picked herbs; those are the places to cut if prep runs long.
- **Buy produce wholesale:** at grocery prices the fennel bowl's food cost more than doubles ($2.44 to $5.39), because arugula and clamshell herbs cost 2–6 times the distributor price. The biggest lines at distributor prices are arugula ($0.55) in the fennel bowl and cherry tomatoes ($0.44) in the quinoa salad.
- **Dressing on the side** keeps the bowls crisp through lunch and makes the quinoa salad's 2-day hold possible. The cup and lid cost $0.02.
- **Pepitas, not nuts:** pumpkin seeds give the crunch without adding tree nuts to a menu that has none so far.

- **Chicken salad at $4.50:** 18% of its price at distributor prices. The nearest comparable is Tatte's last published chicken add-on, $3.00 on its 2022–23 menus.
- **Hummus at $3.50 replaced tuna (2026-11-07):** a scoop costs $0.59 at distributor prices, 17% of its price and less than half the tuna's $1.34. It keeps 5 days against the tuna salad's 3, gives the menu a meat-free add-on, and suits the Mediterranean fennel bowl. $3.50 sits about where a Boston-metro Cava side of hummus would. Per scoop it earns about $0.25 less than tuna did at $4.50 ($2.91 against $3.16), but at a dollar less to the customer it should sell more often.
- **Canned chickpeas keep the labor down:** a 10-scoop batch is about 20 minutes of work (drain, blend, portion), roughly 2 minutes a scoop, against about 4 for the chicken salad, which has to be roasted, cooled and diced. Dried chickpeas would save about $0.08 a scoop but need an overnight soak and an hour's simmer.
- **The scoop goes in its own cup, not in the bowl:** the bowls are sealed and labeled. Chicken salad adds egg and hummus adds sesame, so scooping either into a bowl would make the bowl's allergen label wrong. The cup also keeps mayonnaise off the arugula, and lets the scoops sit in the case beside the bowls. Cup, lid and label cost $0.04.
- **Sesame is new to the kitchen** with the tahini. Wash the food processor after each hummus batch and add sesame to the staff allergen list.
- **Totals:** with a scoop, a bowl comes to $15.00–$17.00. Only the fennel bowl with chicken salad tops Flour's $16 BLT, the top of the research's $13–$16 lunch band.
- **Few new ingredients:** the chicken salad uses the parsley, dill, shallot, lemon and Dijon already bought for the fennel bowl, and the hummus uses the lemon, garlic, cumin and olive oil already on hand. Only chicken, mayonnaise, celery, canned chickpeas and tahini are new.
- **No wheat added:** neither scoop contains wheat, so the quinoa salad stays wheat-free with either.

## Rules carried over from the research

- Benchmark counter prices only. Delivery apps run 10–40% above the counter in Boston. If these go on apps, list them about 20% higher (roll $5.70, mini scone $2.70, coffee cake and muffin $5.40, breakfast sandwiches $10.20 and $12.30, chicken sandwich $15.00, ham sandwich $13.80, salads $15.00 and $13.80, chicken salad $5.40, hummus $4.20) and stay on the lowest-commission plan.
- Plan to hold these prices through 2026–27. Butter (CME cash $1.28/lb on Nov 4, 2026) and eggs ($2.27 a dozen in Sept 2026) are well below their peaks.
- Revisit once real distributor quotes replace the estimates in `ingredient_prices.csv`, or after the first month of sales.
