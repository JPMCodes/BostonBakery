## 1. Cinnamon rolls in Miami-Dade: Knaus, Cinnabon/Cinnaholic, dedicated shops and pop-ups, bakeries known for a roll

### Takeaway
No dedicated cinnamon-roll shop, Cinnaholic or Cinnabon turned up in Coral Gables, South Miami or Pinecrest. The only roll found inside the three towns is Madruga Bakery's cream-cheese-topped bun in Coral Gables, and its price was not found. The county has two benchmarks:
- **Low end:** Knaus Berry Farm. It is seasonal (Oct 13, 2026 – May 9, 2027, Tue–Sun 8–5), now in Redland under new owners since 2025. Rolls cost about $1.45–$2 each in Dec 2025, lines last hours, and 2026–27 has a 4-dozen cap.
- **High end:** Keik Bakeshop's weekend-only brioche rolls in Kendall, Doral and Pembroke Pines, at $8.99–$9.99 in 2025 and about $9.25 in 2026.

### Inferences
- In the three towns, a cinnamon-roll-led shop would have no same-format specialist competitor. The specialists are either a seasonal destination far to the south (Knaus) or a weekend-only event (Keik in Kendall).
- Knaus is the strongest price anchor. At $1.45 a roll in Dec 2025, a dozen works out to about $17.40. That is my calculation, not a published price, and it is close to the client's $18 four-pack. Knaus's roll size was not found, so a per-ounce comparison is not possible.
- Knaus's 2026 changes point to demand above stand capacity at peak: the cap fell from 12 to 4 dozen, and pre-orders of 4–12 dozen and online ordering were added "to shorten lines". This is a seasonal pilgrimage, though, not evidence of daily neighbourhood demand.
- Keik sells rolls only on weekends, in rotating flavours, and "never on the menu all year long". Roll & Dough works as a pop-up. Together they suggest Miami operators treat cinnamon rolls as a scarcity or event product rather than an everyday staple.

**Florida cottage food law (2026)**
- **FDACS page [R], read Nov 19, 2026** — [FDACS Cottage Foods](https://www.fdacs.gov/Business-Services/Food/Food-Establishments/Cottage-Foods):
  - "Gross sales for a cottage food operation must not exceed $250,000 annually."
  - Operators can "produce and sell these products directly to consumers without obtaining a food permit" from FDACS.
  - Approved products include "Loaf breads, rolls, biscuits" and "Cakes, pastries and cookies".
  - Operators "may sell cottage food products on their website, by mail order, and direct to consumer (in person)".
  - Products "cannot be sold wholesale".
  - Products "must be properly packaged and labeled"; free samples must be prepackaged.
  - Statute cited: Florida Statutes s. 500.80.
- **Icings and glazes:** FDACS guidance, as quoted in search results, says cream-cheese and butter icings may not be produced under cottage food: "These items require refrigeration due to the high-water content (made from cow's milk) and are considered a TCS food". Icings made with margarine, shortening or vegan butter "would be considered permissible". Sources disagree on buttercream. — Result set including [cottagefoodlawlibrary.org](https://cottagefoodlawlibrary.org/florida), [floridacottagefoods.com](https://floridacottagefoods.com/florida-cottage-food-law) and [urbanaglaw.org](https://urbanaglaw.org/cottage-food-laws/florida/) [S; the page quoting FDACS was not isolated, and the FDACS original was not read].
- **Year the cap rose:** one blog says HB 663 ("Home Sweet Home Act", 2021) raised it "from $50,000 to $250,000 effective July 1, 2021" — [Keeper Tax](https://www.keepertax.com/posts/florida-cottage-food-law) [S]. ### Inferences
- **Glaze risk.** The client's cooked buttermilk glaze is dairy-based. FDACS's stated reasoning is that high-water dairy icings need refrigeration (TCS), so the glaze may not qualify under cottage food unless it is reformulated to a shelf-stable glaze or shown not to need temperature control. Confirm with FDACS before relying on a home-kitchen test; otherwise bake in a licensed commissary. The bread roll itself is an approved cottage product.
- **Best in-area test.** The Pinecrest Sunday market reaches Pinecrest and South Miami customers directly. The Coral Gables City Hall market runs only January to March, and Vizcaya is just outside the three towns.
- **Cost of a test.**
  - Market stall: about $50–$65 a week. The Vizcaya figure is from 2020 and the $65 co-op figure is unconfirmed.
  - Commissary: about $15–$45 an hour typical, or CityChef from $300 a month.
  - The cottage cap of $250,000 a year is far above anything a test would sell.

## 7. Product fit: heat, humidity and the hot/iced split

### Takeaway
No Miami-specific figure was found for the iced share of café sales, or for how heat affects demand for a warm glazed roll. National proxies suggest iced drinks are a majority of beverage sales: Starbucks puts cold drinks at 76% of US company-operated beverage sales (undated), and one market report says Florida has year-round cold-coffee demand. The seasonal habits of local roll sellers point to rolls skewing toward the cooler months: Knaus opens October–May, Keik sells its roll on fall weekends, and Roll & Dough paired rolls with frozen yogurt in summer.

### Cited Findings
- Starbucks: cold beverages "account for 76 per cent of beverage sales at company-operated locations" in the US — [Global Coffee Report](https://www.gcrmag.com/why-cold-brew-beverages-are-dominating-the-cafe-scene/) [S; undated].
- "Iced and cold drinks account for 38% of café orders even in winter" — [Roastworthy](https://roastworthy.coffee/coffee-shop-statistics/) [S; methodology unknown].
- Cold coffee is "accounting for more than half of all specialty coffee shop revenue" — a trade piece among the results, either [FreshCup](https://freshcup.com/beyond-cold-brew-elevating-your-cold-coffee-menu/) or [Canopy Point](https://www.canopypointcoffee.com/articles/the-cold-rush-how-iced-drinks-are-dominating-the-coffee-industry) [S; attribution unclear].
- "Warmer states such as Florida, Arizona, and Nevada exhibit consistent year-round demand for cold coffee beverages, while northern states experience more pronounced seasonal fluctuations" — a market-research report among the results, such as [market.us](https://market.us/report/iced-coffee-market/) [S; attribution unclear; qualitative only].
- A Miami menu aggregator lists 217 places selling iced lattes — [searchthemenu.com](https://searchthemenu.com/florida/miami/iced-latte/) [S].
- **Seasonality of local roll sellers:**
  - Knaus is open Oct 13, 2026 – May 9, 2027 and closed in summer — [CBS Miami](https://www.cbsnews.com/miami/news/knaus-berry-farm-online-ordering-cinnamon-rolls/) [S].
  - Keik's 2026 roll ran on weekends in October and November only — [Keik blog](https://www.keikrestaurant.com/blogs/news/treat-yourself-cinnamon-roll) [S].
  - Roll & Dough's summer 2026 pop-up paired rolls with frozen yogurt — [Miramar FL News](https://www.miramarflnews.com/news/local/article316285946.html) [S].

### Inferences
- Plan for iced drinks as the larger share of beverage sales in Miami, likely well above Boston. The client's iced latte at $5.00 and cold brew at $4.00 are the drinks to price and promote.
- Warm glazed rolls may sell best from October to May. Knaus closes in summer, but that is also a farm-season effect, so this is suggestive only. A summer plan (cold pairings, smaller sizes, boxes for indoor gatherings) is worth testing at the markets.

### Gaps
- Miami-specific iced versus hot share for café sales.
- Seasonal (summer versus winter) sales curves for Miami bakeries.
- Any evidence on demand for warm, glazed pastries in Miami summer heat and humidity.
