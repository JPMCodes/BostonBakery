## 1. People: population, households, income, age, Hispanic share, daytime employment, drive-time trade areas

### Takeaway
Pinecrest is by far the richest of the three towns: median household income about $206,000 for 2020–24, against about $131,000 in Coral Gables (2019–23) and about $88,000 in South Miami. All three are about half Hispanic. Cambridge, Somerville and Jamaica Plain have similar incomes (about $130,000), but they are 9–22% Hispanic and much denser.

Coral Gables is the only one of the three with a big daytime workforce: about 59,000 jobs in 2019, 94% of them held by people who live elsewhere. Pinecrest had about 5,900 jobs in 2018, and nearly all its working residents commute out. No current 5- or 10-minute drive-time household counts were found for Miracle Mile, Sunset Drive or Suniland. The only drive-time incomes found are from 2012 and are for US-1 at SW 124th St in Pinecrest.

### Inferences
- Judgment: Pinecrest and Coral Gables households can easily afford a premium $8–9 pastry-and-coffee ticket. In Brighton, by contrast, 30% of households earn under $50,000. The Miami limit on sales is how many people pass the door, not what they can spend.
- Judgment: Daytime population is what sets Harvard Square apart: about 30,000 workers and 27,000 students within walking distance. Of the Miami towns, only downtown Coral Gables has a daytime workforce on that scale (about 59,000 jobs citywide in 2019, plus the CBRE 1-mile daytime population of 58,873), and it lacks Harvard's student and transit flow. South Miami has no published daytime-employment figure. Pinecrest's daytime workforce is small (about 5,900 jobs in 2018).
- Judgment: All three towns are about 49–56% Hispanic, and Cuban-American bakery and café habits are part of the local market. The Boston districts are 9–22% Hispanic. This bears on product mix and competition more than on volume.

- Not found: official median incomes for Jamaica Plain and Brighton (the Boston dataset gives only income brackets).

## 2. Foot traffic: Miracle Mile, Giralda, Sunset Drive and Dorn Avenue, Merrick Park, and Harvard Square for comparison

### Takeaway
No current (2023+) pedestrian counts or Placer.ai visit counts were found for any Miami district. The Coral Gables Business Improvement District was not renewed in 2022, and the city's own Smart City counter data is not published. The best Miracle Mile/Giralda figures are an average of about 2,100 people a day at the west end of Giralda (around 2021) and event-day peaks of 18,000–22,000 (2018).

South Miami's own 2024 study says Sunset Drive visits fell in 2023 and 2024 and that weekends are busiest. Pinecrest's US-1 corridor data is from 2019–2020 Placer.ai. Harvard Square also has no published pedestrian count. Its Red Line station alone carries about 21,500 weekday riders, which is 4–8 times any Miami candidate's Metrorail station (section 4).

## 3. Car traffic and parking

### Takeaway
US-1 (South Dixie Highway) carries about 65,000–95,000 vehicles a day through Pinecrest by broker counts. A city report gives about 73,000 a day in front of the University of Miami (2021). Kendall Drive at US-1 carries about 55,000. Ponce de Leon by Merrick Park carried only about 11,600 (2021).

No official FDOT AADT was readable; FDOT and county GIS hosts are blocked and broker figures conflict. Coral Gables parking is paid but cheap, and its on-street meters are not enforced before 9 am. South Miami has a paid, app-based 400-space garage. Pinecrest centers have free surface lots.

## 5. Seasonality: monthly pattern, summer dip, school holidays, hurricane season, rain and heat

### Takeaway
Miami-area restaurant sales peak in January, at about 1.15 times an average month, and bottom in late summer and early fall (October 2025 at about 0.90 times), by an aggregator's reading of Florida Department of Revenue data. That is roughly a 28% swing from peak to trough.

Miami owners describe summer dips of 20–40%, and beach areas reportedly dip more. Coral Gables is described as less seasonal. The academic calendar is the sharpest measured swing: about −55% in University-station boardings in June. No quantified data was found on how rain or heat affects morning walk-in trade.

### Cited Findings
- **Monthly pattern (FDOR data, aggregator):** Miami metro restaurant sales: January busiest at 1.15 times the average month, October 2025 slowest at 0.90 times. Miami-Dade restaurant sales totaled $10.79B over April 2025–March 2026. The figures are from Florida Department of Revenue Form 10 gross sales, kind code 8 (restaurants, lunchrooms, catering). DOR records a month when the tax is filed, so figures trail actual sales by about a month [search text; aggregator, not verified against FDOR] — [menuspy.ai Florida report](https://menuspy.ai/reports/florida/); [menuspy.ai Miami report, Oct 2026](https://menuspy.ai/reports/miami-fl/).
- **Summer dips (2025–2026):**
  - One Miami owner reported summer 2025 sales about 40% below April–May levels; a west Kendall restaurant was down 20–25% that summer against the previous summer.
  - An industry guide (unnamed in the results) estimates cover counts drop 35–50% from peak in July–August in beach areas, and says Brickell and Coral Gables "serve more stable residential and corporate markets with lower seasonality variance."
  - Axios (May 2026) reported Miami restaurateurs bracing for "another brutal summer slowdown," and notable closures followed in summer 2026.
  - In Coral Gables, Caffe Vialetto closed in August 2025 after 26 years.

The UM-adjacent site should be modeled with a much deeper summer and December dip than the Pinecrest and South Miami sites. This is inference from the Metrorail and FDOR patterns, not a measured café figure.
- Judgment: The 7 am–2 pm hours avoid the hottest late afternoons and much of the typical afternoon storm window. No data found quantifies the benefit.

## 7. Weekday versus weekend: which days and hours carry sales

### Takeaway
The evidence points the same way across all three towns. South Miami's own study finds weekends busiest. Pinecrest's draw is family and residential, with no main street and 95% of working residents leaving town on weekdays. Downtown Coral Gables carries weekday office and morning pedestrian traffic on Miracle Mile, while Giralda leans to lunch, dinner and weekends. Rail-borne trade everywhere is weekday-heavy. No café-specific day-of-week sales data was found for any of the towns.

### Cited Findings
- **South Miami:** weekends are the busiest days in the downtown study area (2024) [search text] — [South Miami Existing Conditions Analysis](https://southmiamifl.gov/DocumentCenter/View/7889/1-Community-Characteristics?bidId=).
- **Coral Gables:**
  - Miracle Mile has slightly more pedestrian traffic in the morning; Giralda draws at lunch and dinner, and the gap between them narrows at weekends.
  - Streets are "activated throughout all hours of the day."

  [read] — [Competitive Assessment](https://www.coralgables.com/media/4086)
- **Coral Gables visitor guide (low reliability):** weekday mornings are the quietest; Saturdays about 11 am–4 pm are the busiest [search text] — [Nomado](https://www.nomadotravel.app/en/attractions/miracle-mile-coral-gables).
- **Pinecrest:**
  - 94.7% of working residents work outside the village (2018).
  - The village "lacks an engaging, main-street style destination."
  - US-1 corridor visits after 6 pm fell most in 2020.

  [read][OLD] — [Pinecrest Market Analysis 2021](https://www.pinecrest-fl.gov/files/sharedassets/village/v/2/government/building-and-planning/documents/211006-pinecrest-market-an.pdf)
- **Madruga Bakery (Coral Gables):** weekend lines "out the door"; busy weekend mornings [search text] — [Tripadvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Restaurant_Review-g34152-d12202145-Reviews-Madruga_Bakery-Coral_Gables_Florida.html).

## 8. Judgment: expected morning bakery trade at each Miami micro-location against the four Boston districts

### Takeaway
**Judgment (not measured).** None of the six Miami micro-locations has the transit, student or pedestrian density of Harvard Square or Davis Square. On measured transit, the best Miami station carries about a quarter of Harvard's riders, and no current pedestrian counts exist.

My ranking for morning bakery trade, against the client's Boston scale (Harvard 263, Davis 224, JP 214, Brighton 185):
1. Miracle Mile, around Brighton to JP.
2. Dadeland end, around Brighton to JP.
3. Suniland, around Brighton on the weekly average: below on weekdays, above on weekends.
4. Sunset Drive, at or slightly below Brighton.
5. Office core, below Brighton.
6. US-1 by UM, below Brighton on an annual basis.

### Cited Findings
Measured inputs behind the ratings, all cited in sections 1–7:
- **Harvard Square:** about 21,526 weekday T riders; 30,058 workers; 27,127 students; $109.4M of food and drink sales within 0.5 mile (2021).
- **Metrorail weekday boardings, October 2025:** Dadeland North 4,996, Douglas Road 3,325, University 2,618, South Miami 2,569 [read] — [DTPW](https://www.miamidade.gov/global/transportation/ridership-technical-reports.page).
- **Coral Gables:** 59,048 jobs (2019); 1-mile daytime population 58,873 at Miracle Mile (listing); Giralda west end about 2,145 people a day (c. 2021); office vacancy about 10.1% (Q3 2026).
- **South Miami:** downtown visits declined in 2023 and 2024; weekends busiest (2024).
- **Pinecrest:** median household income $206,417; Suniland visited by 33.4% of Pinecrest residents (2020–21); free parking; US-1 at 65,500–90,000+ vehicles a day (listings).
- **UM:** 19,852 students (fall 2024); University-station boardings −55% in June against October.

### Inferences
All ratings below are **judgment**.

**1. Miracle Mile and Giralda.** Rating: **between Brighton (185) and JP (214)**; closest analog JP center.
- **For:**
  - The only true walkable main street of the six.
  - Slightly more morning pedestrians on Miracle Mile than on Giralda, per city sensor data.
  - About 59,000 jobs citywide, with a 58,873 daytime population within 1 mile.
  - High incomes.
  - Free trolley, and free on-street parking before 9 am.
  - Weekend leisure trade.
- **Against:**
  - Ordinary-day counts in the low thousands, against Harvard's transit-fed crowds.
  - No rail station at the door.
  - Visible vacancies, and the BID lapsed in 2022.
  - Climate-driven summer dip.
- Choose the Miracle Mile or Ponce de Leon frontage over the Giralda plaza for a morning-only shop.

**2. Coral Gables office core (Ponce de Leon and Alhambra).** Rating: **below Brighton**. Weekdays could approach Brighton; Saturdays and Sundays will be thin.
- **For:** dense office employment, improving occupancy (about 10% vacancy, Q3 2026), and the trolley.
- **Against:** two of the six trading days are weekend days with little office population, and hybrid attendance is unmeasured.
- The weekly average is pulled below the client's Boston range.

**3. South Dixie Highway by the University of Miami.** Rating: **about Brighton during semesters, well below in summer and December; annual average below Brighton**.
- **For:**
  - About 19,900 students and a large staff.
  - Brighton is also a student-heavy, lower-income district, so it is the right Boston analog.
  - The Underline Phase 3 along US-1 should help walking and biking access once open.
- **Against:**
  - US-1 traffic (about 73,000 a day) is pass-by.
  - Nearby retail is oriented to limited-service and convenience.
  - University station shows −55% in June and −45% in December against October.

**4. South Miami Sunset Drive town center.** Rating: **at or slightly below Brighton (185) in the near term**.
- **For:**
  - A compact walkable core: east of US-1 carries about 82% of district visits.
  - South Miami station has steadier ridership (−20% in summer).
  - The hospital workforce (about 2,400 full-time equivalents).
  - Close to affluent Coral Gables and Pinecrest neighborhoods.
  - Weekend-busy.
- **Against:**
  - Visits declined in 2023 and 2024.
  - The Sunset Place redevelopment will mean years of construction before any lift.
  - Paid parking.
  - Small resident base (about 12,000) with a lower median income ($88,000).

**5. Pinecrest Suniland center on US-1.** Rating: **about Brighton on the weekly average**. Weekday mornings are likely below Brighton; Saturday and Sunday mornings could match or beat JP.
- **For:**
  - The richest households of all ten places compared.
  - A family-heavy population and free parking.
  - A grocery-anchored center visited by a third of Pinecrest residents.
  - US-1 visibility.
- **Against:**
  - No walk-in trade.
  - US-1 access and turning are hard, which the village itself flags.
  - Most residents commute out early.
  - A bakery here depends on destination and routine car trips. That suits a cinnamon-roll treat format on weekends more than a weekday coffee run.

**6. Dadeland end of Pinecrest and Kendall (US-1 and Kendall Drive).** Rating: **between Brighton and JP; the strongest Miami weekday-morning candidate after Miracle Mile**.
- **For:**
  - The busiest Metrorail stations of the set (Dadeland North about 5,000 and Dadeland South about 4,700 weekday boardings).
  - Dadeland Mall, the place most visited by Pinecrest residents (45.5%).
  - Heavy arterials: Kendall Drive at about 55,000 vehicles a day, US-1 at SW 104th St at about 95,000.
  - Both weekday and weekend demand.
- **Against:** still about a quarter of Harvard's transit flow, congestion, and structured or mall parking. No counts were found for Downtown Dadeland's residents or offices, so its upside is unmeasured.

**Overall judgment**
- On measured density, no Miami micro-location justifies the Harvard (263) or Davis (224) figures.
- The defensible range for the Miami sites is roughly the client's Brighton-to-JP band (185–214) at best (Miracle Mile, Dadeland, Suniland on weekends), and below Brighton for the office core and the UM strip.
- The 195-order baseline is plausible only at the strongest Miami sites, and it implies upper-half franchise performance (see section 6).

### Gaps
- These ratings rest on proxies: transit boardings, jobs, incomes, broker traffic counts and qualitative city studies. No measured pedestrian counts or bakery sales exist for any Miami site.
- Data that would most improve them:
  - A Placer.ai pull, or the City of Coral Gables' Smart City pedestrian counter data, for each micro-location.
  - FDOT AADT for the exact frontages.
  - Day-of-week sales from a comparable local bakery.
