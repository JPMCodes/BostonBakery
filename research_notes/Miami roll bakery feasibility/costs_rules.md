## 1. Wages: Florida minimum wage, Miami vs Boston pay (OEWS and 2026 job ads)

### Takeaway
Florida's minimum wage became **$15.00/hr on September 30, 2026** (tipped cash wage **$11.98**), the last step of the 2020 constitutional schedule; from September 30, 2027 it is indexed to inflation. That equals Massachusetts' $15.00 floor, so counter and barista base pay now converge with Boston. Massachusetts' tip credit is much bigger, though: its service rate is $6.75 against Florida's $11.98. Skilled baking pay in Miami runs roughly **5–10% below Boston**: Coral Gables bakery ads show $18–24/hr, against $18–30 in Boston.

Suggested Miami pro forma rates, against the client's Boston figures [EST]:

| Role | Boston pro forma | Miami pro forma |
|---|---|---|
| Lead baker | $25 | **$22–24** |
| Second baker | $20 | **$18–20** |
| Barista | $16.50 | **$15.50–16.50** |
| Counter staff | $15.50 | **$15.00–15.50** |

### Inferences
- **Entry-level floor has jumped since the OEWS survey.** The May 2025 OEWS reference period predates two Florida increases. The floor in May 2025 was $13.00 under the $1-a-year schedule. It is now $15.00, up 15.4% [EST]. Miami's $14.23 counter median is therefore stale; current Miami counter pay will sit at or just above $15.00. Boston's $17.45 median reflects a $15 floor in place since 2023.
- **Baker pay (OEWS).** Miami's median is about 4% below Boston's ($17.73 vs $18.54) [EST]. Coral Gables artisan-bakery ads ($18–24) sit at or slightly below Boston's artisan ladder ($18–22 for bakers; $20–30 for lead bakers).
- **Pro forma substitutions [EST]:**
  - Lead baker $22–24 (use $23, −8% vs Boston).
  - Second baker $18–20 (use $19, −5%).
  - Barista $15.50–16.50 (use $16, −3%).
  - Counter $15.00–15.50 (use $15.25, −2%).
  - Weighted, the wage bill falls roughly 3–6% vs Boston, depending on the hour mix.
- **Tip credit is a weaker lever in Miami.** It is worth $3.02/hr in Florida against $8.25/hr in Massachusetts. A Boston pro forma that leans on tipped pay for baristas or counter staff loses most of that saving in Miami. A no-tip-credit Miami counter costs about the same as Boston's $15.
- **No Miami OEWS percentiles are in hand.** Use the job-ad ranges as the operative 25th–75th band for skilled roles.

## 2. Employer payroll costs: reemployment/unemployment tax, workers' comp, leave and scheduling mandates, all-in percentage

### Takeaway
Florida's state payroll charges are lighter than Massachusetts'. The pro forma's 12% of wages for payroll taxes and insurance is about right for Boston and slightly high for Miami; use **about 11%** in Miami. For a small crew the two states land within about 1 point of each other.

| Item | Florida (Miami) | Massachusetts (Boston) |
|---|---|---|
| Reemployment / unemployment tax, new employer | 2.7% on the first $7,000 (max $189 per employee) | 2.42% on the first $15,000 ($363), plus 0.056% Workforce Training Fund |
| Paid family and medical leave | None | PFML 0.88% total; no employer share under 25 covered individuals |
| Earned sick time | No statewide law found | Up to 40 hours a year (paid at 11+ employees) |
| Local scheduling and living-wage rules | Preempted by state law | — |
| Statutory burden (FICA + FUTA + state charges) | **8.1–9.1%** of wages [EST] | **8.5–10.3%** [EST] |
| All-in with workers' comp | **About 10–12%** | **About 10.5–12.3%** |

Florida's bakery workers'-comp rate (class 2003, residual market) is **$2.778 per $100 of payroll** from Jan 1, 2026.

### Cited Findings

**Florida reemployment tax**
- New-employer rate **2.7% (0.0270)**, kept until the employer has reported for 10 quarters. — [SNIPPET] [Florida Dept. of Revenue, RT rate information](https://floridarevenue.com/taxes/taxesfees/Pages/rt_rate.aspx)
- 2026 wage base **$7,000** (unchanged); rate range **0.10%–5.40%**; maximum **$189 per employee** at 2.7%. — [SNIPPET] [Netchex Florida tax guide 2026](https://netchex.com/resources/tax-guide/florida/); [Warp, Florida payroll taxes 2026](https://www.warp.co/state-payroll-taxes/florida)

**Florida workers' comp**
- **Class 2003 Bakeries: rate 2.778 per $100 of payroll, minimum premium $2,660, effective 1/1/2026.** This is the Florida Workers' Compensation Joint Underwriting Association (residual market) rate; voluntary-market carriers may charge less. A second site gives $2.78 for "Bakery & Route Supervisors, Drivers." — [SNIPPET] [FWCJUA current rates](https://www.fwcjua.com/Rates); [WorkersCompCost, Florida](https://workerscompcost.com/states/florida/)
- NCCI recommended an average **6.9% decrease** in voluntary-market rates effective **Jan 1, 2026**. — [SNIPPET] [Bridgeway Insurance, Apr 13, 2026](https://bridgewayins.com/2026/04/13/workers-comp-requirements-florida/)
- **No verified Florida rate for restaurant class 9079.** One calculator shows a national-style $2.50. Statewide averages quoted online conflict ($1.40 to $6.15 per $100). — [SNIPPET] [WorkCompCalculator](https://www.workcompcalculator.com/workers-comp-calculator/florida/)

**Florida leave and scheduling mandates**
- **Preemption of local rules.** HB 433 (2024), "effective July 1, 2024":
  - It "preempts local living wage programs, including Miami-Dade County's living wage program." Implementation is delayed: the preemption "does not apply to any contract entered into before September 30, 2026." That program covered county contractors only.
  - It "also preempts local heat exposure regulation and local employee scheduling or predictive scheduling regulation."
  - [READ] [Miami-Dade County 2024 Final Legislative Report](https://www.miamidade.gov/commission/library/2024-final-legislative-report.pdf)
- **Statute.** The enrolled bill creates **s. 448.077, F.S., "Preemption of employee scheduling regulation."** — [SNIPPET] [HB 433 enrolled text](https://flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2024/433/BillText/er/PDF); [BakerHostetler summary](https://www.bakerlaw.com/insights/sunshine-state-update-new-florida-law-forbids-local-employment-regulation-in-three-areas)
- **No paid-leave law.** No Florida statewide paid-sick-leave or paid-family-leave law surfaced in any search.

**Massachusetts comparison**
- **Earned sick time.** Workers "earn at least one hour of earned sick leave for every 30 hours worked," up to **40 hours per year**. "Employers with 11 or more employees must provide paid sick time. Employers with fewer than 11 employees must provide earned sick time, but it does not need to be paid." — [READ] [Mass.gov, Earned Sick Time](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/earned-sick-time)
- **Payroll charges [SIBLING]:**
  - UI new-employer rate 2.42% on a $15,000 base ($363); Workforce Training Fund 0.056%.
  - EMAC $0 in years 1–3 (vendor claim).
  - PFML 0.88% total, with no employer share under 25 covered individuals. PFML 2027 keeps 0.88% total, with the employer share moved to family leave (announced Oct 2026; effective Jan 1, 2027).
  - No post-2008 Massachusetts class 2003 workers' comp rate was found (2008: 2.75).

### Inferences
**Burden by wage level [EST].** Assumptions: FICA 7.65%; FUTA 0.6% on $7,000 (statutory; not re-sourced); a new employer; no health benefits.

| Wage and hours | Florida, statutory only | Florida + WC at 2.778% | Florida + WC at an assumed 1.5% (voluntary) | Massachusetts, statutory | Massachusetts + WC at an assumed 2% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $15.00 full-time (2,080 h) | 8.39% | 11.17% | 9.89% | 8.98% | 10.98% |
| $15.00 part-time (1,040 h) | 9.13% | 11.91% | 10.63% | 10.30% | 12.30% |
| $20.00 full-time | 8.21% | 10.98% | 9.71% | 8.64% | 10.64% |
| $25.00 full-time | 8.09% | 10.87% | 9.59% | 8.45% | 10.44% |

- **Recommendation.** Use **11%** in Miami. Keep 12% if the line also carries general liability or employer health contributions.
- **Massachusetts sick time.** With 11+ employees, Massachusetts paid sick time adds up to 40 paid hours per worker, about 1.9% of a 2,080-hour year [EST]. Florida has no equivalent.
- **Workers' comp class is the swing factor.** If the insurer rates the shop as a restaurant (9079/9083) rather than a bakery (2003), the rate could differ materially. A Florida broker quote is needed.
- **Mandate stability.** Local add-on mandates (sick leave, scheduling) are effectively off the table in Florida, so labor-rule risk is lower than in Massachusetts.

### Gaps
- **Restaurant comp rate.** The Florida voluntary-market rate for classes 9079/9083, and which class an insurer would assign to a bakery-café with seats.
- **Local benefits preemption.** Whether Florida's older employment-benefits preemption (s. 218.077, F.S.) still bars local paid-sick-leave ordinances. My understanding is that it does, but this was not verified this session.
- **Massachusetts workers' comp.** The 2026 class 2003 or restaurant rate.

## 3. Utilities: electricity (FPL vs Eversource), natural gas, water and sewer, cost per sq ft

### Takeaway
**Electricity**
- Florida commercial power averages about **12.8¢/kWh** (EIA, Feb 2026), against a US commercial average of 14.4¢.
- FPL's small-business tariffs:

| FPL tariff | Base charges | Energy charge | Demand charge |
|---|---|---|---|
| GS-1 (non-demand, under 25 kW) | Base charge about $14.20 | About 8.0¢/kWh | None |
| GSD-1 (25–499 kW) | Customer charge $33.71/month | About 6.3¢/kWh with fuel and clauses | **$12.70/kW** |

- Eversource's Eastern Massachusetts small-business **supply** price alone was **15.03¢/kWh** (Feb–Jul 2026), before delivery charges. Boston power therefore likely costs very roughly twice Miami's per kWh [EST; Boston delivery charges not found].

**Water and sewer.** Miami-Dade water and sewer (WASD) rates are modest: a small bakery using 10–20 ccf a month would pay about **$1.5–3.4/sq ft/yr** at FY2026-27 levels [EST].

**Gas.** Natural-gas availability in these towns' retail streets could not be confirmed.

**Bottom line.** No Miami bakery or café utility benchmark was found. A planning figure of about **$11–16/sq ft/yr** for an all-electric bakery with year-round air conditioning is an estimate, below Boston's $20 [EST].

**Planning figure [EST].**

- The Boston figure plausibly carries roughly double the per-kWh electricity price plus winter gas heat. Miami trades that for summer cooling load.
- **Gas.** If gas is unavailable on a site, budget all-electric ovens. The equipment choice then drives demand charges.

### Inferences
- **Expected lost trading days [EST, low confidence].** About 0.3 events a year × 2–7 closed or impaired days per event ≈ **1–2 lost trading days a year on average**. The pro forma should carry a contingency of about 0.5–1% of sales, or business-interruption cover.
- **Windstorm pass-through.** Under a typical NNN lease, the landlord's building property insurance, including wind, passes through in the NNN/CAM charge. The tenant insures its own improvements, equipment and stock, often with a named-storm deductible. This is my understanding of standard practice, not verified this session. Read the lease's insurance and NNN clauses.

## 5. Taxes: commercial rent tax, sales tax on bakery goods and drinks, tangible personal property tax, local business tax

### Takeaway
**Commercial rent.** Florida **no longer taxes commercial rent.** HB 7031 repealed s. 212.031, F.S., for occupancy periods beginning **on or after October 1, 2025**. The repeal covers both the state rate and the local surtax.

**Sales tax with 8 seats.** The shop has "eating facilities," so it must **separately account** for sales:
- **Exempt:** bakery products sold for off-premises consumption, such as packaged or to-go rolls.
- **Taxable:** coffee and other drinks, heated or toasted items, and anything eaten on site.

The rate is presumably 7%: 6% state plus Miami-Dade's 1% surtax, not re-verified this session.

**Tangible personal property.** Business equipment is taxed annually above a **$25,000** per-return exemption; file form DR-405 by April 1.

**Local business tax.** Coral Gables' city local business tax is about **$194–207/yr**. A Miami-Dade County business tax is also required in all three towns (amount not found).

## 6. State licence: FDACS retail food permit vs DBPR food-service licence

### Takeaway
**Which agency.** A roll bakery with an espresso bar most likely needs a **Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services (FDACS) retail food establishment permit**, not a Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR) food-service licence:
- FDACS lists "coffee shops, bakeries" as its retail food permittees.
- DBPR's jurisdiction page says FDACS licenses bakeries, while DBPR licenses "free standing restaurants, fast food services (takeout and delivery)."
- Eight seats push the shop toward the line. No "51% of sales" test could be confirmed. **Get a written jurisdiction determination before signing the lease.**

**FDACS process and cost**
- Apply **21 days before opening**. An inspector calls within **3–5 business days**, and the inspection is "typically" within **two weeks**.
- Optional plan review: **$55.10** initial fee.
- Annual permit fee: about **$355 (retail bakery)** to **$440–490 (retail bakery with food service)**, per a rulemaking notice not yet confirmed as current.

**DBPR alternative.** About **$262** for 1–49 seats (rule text, date unverified), capped at $400 by statute; plan review is required.

### Inferences

## 8. County requirements: DERM grease and plan review, WASD connection charges, impact fees, fire review

### Takeaway
**DERM**
- Approves every municipal Certificate of Use first.
- Requires an annual **Fats, Oils and Grease (FOG) Discharge Control operating permit** for any food business discharging to sewer: **$150–168.75/yr** under 4,000 sq ft. Grease traps are required.
- A **sewer capacity certification** ($120 commercial) is needed for any change of use or added seats that increases flow.

**WASD connection charges.** **$1.39/gpd (water) + $5.60/gpd (sewer)** on the *increase* in rated flow.
- WASD's schedule rates fast-food restaurants at **65 gpd per 100 sq ft** and full-service or take-out at **100**, against **10** for retail.
- Converting 930 sq ft of retail to food use therefore costs about **$3,600–5,900** [EST].

**County mobility (road) impact fee.** The fee schedule effective Dec 31, 2023 – Dec 30, 2027 depends heavily on the land-use class:

| Class | Fee per sq ft |
|---|---|
| Strip plaza retail | $6.47–7.41 |
| **Fast-casual restaurant** | **$17.13–19.62** |
| **Fast-food without drive-through** | **$79.44–91.01** |

On the net increase, a 930 sq ft retail-to-café conversion could owe about **$10K–12K** if classed fast casual, but **$69K–79K** if classed fast food [EST].

**Fire review**
- Coral Gables has its own fire department (fire permit fee **0.55%** of job value).
- South Miami and Pinecrest are reviewed by Miami-Dade Fire Rescue (MDFR).

## 10. Overall: easier or harder than Boston and Cambridge, by how many months and dollars

### Takeaway
**Base case.** For a second-generation food space where the bakery-café is an allowed use (most plausibly Coral Gables or Pinecrest), opening in Miami-Dade is about **as fast as Boston (3–6 months) or slightly faster**:
- No hearing is needed for the state licence, and the FDACS permit takes about 2–4 weeks.
- Coral Gables' Certificate of Use takes 7–10 working days, and the Board of Architects meets weekly.
- Running costs are lower: about 3–6% lower wages, about 1 point lower payroll burden, and roughly half the electricity price per kWh. Commercial rent is no longer taxed.

**Harder or costlier cases**
- **Change of use from retail** triggers one-time county charges with no Boston equivalent found:
  - WASD connection charges: about **$3.6–5.9K**.
  - The County mobility impact fee: about **$10–12K** if classed fast casual, up to **$69–79K** if classed fast food.
  - A Coral Gables DRC cycle: about **+1–2 months**.
- **Hearings.** South Miami's Hometown District appears to require a **special-use hearing** for general restaurants, comparable to Boston's ZBA (+3–6 months). Coral Gables conditional use adds **3–5 months and about $12.2K**; Pinecrest conditional use about **3 months and $3.1K**.
- **Other Miami-specific risks:** parking compliance at change of use, sewer vs septic (Pinecrest), hurricane-code storefronts and storm downtime.

### Cited Findings
- **Boston fees and timelines [SIBLING]:**
  - CV licence $100 + $1/seat; ISD health permit $200–300.
  - ZBA 3–6 months where takeout is not as-of-right.
  - 3–6 months (second-generation space, as-of-right, CV transfer); 6–12 months (new build-out with CV hearing); worst cases about 18 months.
  - The Oct 27, 2026 Wu proposals (drop zoning review for takeout; ownership transfers under 2 weeks) are **proposals only**.

**Annual running-cost effect on $530K sales, Miami vs Boston [EST].** Assume about $150–170K of wages; the wage base is my assumption.
- Wages 3–6% lower: about −$4.5K to −$10K.
- Burden about 1 point lower: about −$1.5K.
- Utilities at $14–16 vs $20/sq ft: about −$3.7K to −$5.6K.
- Storm reserve (0.5–1% of sales): about +$2.7K to +$5.3K. A higher Miami BOP premium may add more; no Boston BOP figure was available to compare.
- **Net: roughly $5–15K a year cheaper to run in Miami**, before rent. Rent is outside this note.

**Time and one-off cost [EST]:**
- **Coral Gables, second-generation café space with a prior restaurant CU:** easier than Boston, with no CV hearing and no ZBA risk; about 0–2 months faster.
- **Retail-to-café conversion in any of the three towns:** about 1–2 months slower than an as-of-right Boston site, plus about **$14–18K** of county charges if classed fast casual (WASD $3.6–5.9K + mobility $10.1–11.6K + DERM/FOG about $0.4–0.6K), or about **$73–86K** if classed fast food.
- **South Miami Hometown District with a special-use hearing:** about as hard as a Boston ZBA case, about 3–6 months extra.
- **Cambridge's 2021 parking relief** has no equivalent in these towns.
