## 1. Best-sellers, item prices, coffee attach, average check, daypart and channel mix

### Takeaway
No POS provider or trade body publishes a free bakery-item best-seller ranking or a croissant or cookie price series that I could find, and Toast's Menu Price Monitor does not track bakery items. The usable velocity signals are national. Toast's 2025 data show espresso drinks gaining (lattes +4%, espresso shots +3.3%) while drip coffee fell 3.3%. Circana supermarket-bakery data show cakes as the largest dollar category and cookies as the fastest-growing by units (+7.6% in Sept 2026), with croissants and muffins posting unit gains. Guide-level sources put coffee attach at about 50–60% and bakery revenue at 60–75% before noon. No Boston-metro cut of any of these was found.

### Inferences
- **The beverage program is shifting toward espresso.** Lattes and espresso are growing while drip and cold brew decline. A bakery-café's beverage revenue therefore increasingly depends on espresso equipment and barista labor, and an espresso-led attach plausibly lifts average ticket ($6.60 Boston iced latte vs $4–5 generic coffee ticket).
- **Velocity proxies for ranking.** Cookies show the fastest unit growth, cakes are the largest dollar category, and croissants and muffins posted unit gains. This is supermarket data, so it transfers imperfectly to independent bakeries. It is directionally consistent with Datassential's finding that croissants, muffins and cinnamon rolls are the most ubiquitous menu pastries.
- **The morning daypart dominates, but food attach is weakest then** (38% early-morning attach vs 51% midday). The cheapest growth lever is attaching a pastry to the morning coffee transaction, through bundles and counter merchandising. The 2–4 pm lull argues for front-loaded staffing.
- **Price anchors.** An average croissant of about $6 in LA and a Boston latte of about $6.60 (delivery) suggest premium Boston pastry pricing in roughly the $5–7 range. That is an inference only; Boston menu prices were not surveyed here.

### Gaps
- No Toast, Square, Clover or Lightspeed bakery best-seller ranking was found, nor any 2022–2026 item-level price series for croissants, cookies or lattes. The Toast Menu Price Monitor excludes bakery items, and Toast's 2026 coffee median prices could not be retrieved.
- No Boston-metro cut of any POS dataset was found.
- None of these were found: authoritative average check for bakery-cafés; weekend vs weekday split; pre-order, catering or holiday share of sales. Technomic, ABA, Retail Bakers of America, IBISWorld and NRA figures were not retrieved because the search budget ran out and the trade-press domains were blocked for direct fetch.

## 2. Product margin ladder: ingredient cost, gross margin and labor intensity

### Takeaway
Only vendor- and blog-grade unit-cost data were found, but they agree that ingredients are a small share of retail price for the core items:
- Croissants: about 6–33% of a $6 price, with butter about 60% of ingredient cost.
- Sourdough: about 10–38% of an $8–15 loaf.
- Cookies: a 20–28% food-cost target.

Labor, waste and channel (retail vs wholesale) therefore decide product profitability. No source quantified labor minutes per unit, such as lamination or shaping, and no beverage cost per cup was retrieved.

  | Sourdough at $5–6 wholesale | 25–60% | about 40–75% |
  | Cookie (food-cost target) | 20–28% | about 72–80% |

  Wholesale roughly doubles the ingredient share. This supports retail-first or café models over wholesale bread for margin.
- **Butter sensitivity.** With butter at about 60% of croissant ingredient cost and CME butter down 29% year to date in 2026 (section 5), full pass-through would cut croissant ingredient cost by about 17% (0.60 × 0.29). Ingredients are only 6–33% of a $6 croissant, so that is worth about 1–6% of price. Butter swings matter far less than labor and waste at retail, and more in wholesale.

## 3. P&L benchmarks, unit volumes, capex and break-even

### Takeaway
Consultant- and vendor-grade benchmarks cluster around the following shares of sales:
- COGS: 25–35%
- Labor: 25–40%
- Occupancy: 5–10% (above 10% means the site is too expensive)
- Net margin: about 3–9% for retail storefronts; 2.5–7% for coffee shops

No audited or association figures, AUVs for independent bakery-cafés, sales per square foot, Boston capex or break-even timelines were retrieved.

### Inferences
- **Midpoint cost stack.** COGS of about 30%, labor of about 30–35% and occupancy of about 8% total about 68–73% of sales before utilities, packaging, card fees, insurance, repairs and marketing. That is consistent with the mid-single-digit net margins cited. A model that cannot hold labor near 30% or occupancy under 10% likely loses money.
- **Coffee shops vs bakeries.** The coffee-shop net range (2.5–7%) overlaps the retail-bakery range (3–9%). The data here cannot show that adding coffee changes net margin, only that it adds revenue (20–40% per one vendor estimate, section 1).
- The Boston rent and labor tests that apply these ratios to Boston inputs are in section 4.

### Gaps
- No audited or association benchmarks were retrieved (Retail Bakers of America, American Bakers Association, NRA, RMA/Risk Management Association, IBISWorld).
- No AUV for independent bakery-cafés, and no franchise disclosure (FDD Item 19) figures for bakery-café or cookie chains, which are the best public P&L proxies.
- No sales per square foot.
- No Boston build-out cost per sq ft.
- No equipment prices for deck, rack or convection ovens, sheeters, spiral or planetary mixers, proofers or hoods.
- No time-to-break-even data. All of these were in scope, but the search budget was exhausted before they could be searched.

## 4. Boston cost inputs, 2025–2026

### Takeaway
Retail rents vary by roughly 20× across Boston submarkets:
- About $25–31 per sq ft for metro and citywide average asking rents.
- About $96 per sq ft average asking on Newbury Street (CoStar, Apr 2026).
- $400–480 per sq ft for prime Newbury ground floor.
- 2024-era Seaport figures: second-floor food and beverage up to $80, ground-floor apparel about $150.

Bakers earn about $18.50–20 an hour against a $15.00 state minimum that is unchanged since 2023, and PFML adds at most 0.42% employer cost. In Boston, a Common Victualler license must precede the ISD food permit. - **2026 is a cost-relief year relative to 2025 peaks** for butter, eggs, coffee and, partly, cocoa.
