## 1. Retail asking rents by submarket (and reported bakery lease terms)

### Takeaway
Boston street retail is tight: Q2 2026 vacancy was 2.6% (Matthews), and Banker & Tradesman expects asking rents to rise again in 2026. Rents fall into roughly three tiers:
- **Prime streets: about $85–225.** Back Bay prime runs $125–225 and the Seaport $85–150; both figures are undated.
- **Downtown and inner-core squares: about $45–80.** Downtown Crossing and the Financial District averaged $56.59 (CoStar, Q1 2024). Listings run $36–80 in South Boston and Somerville, the aggregator average for Somerville is $51, and Coolidge Corner is about $47–55.
- **Neighborhood main streets: about $22–49.** This covers Jamaica Plain, Roslindale, Dorchester Ave and East Boston.

No current priced figure surfaced for Cambridge's squares, South End storefronts, Fenway or Back Bay off Newbury. No 2025–26 bakery lease disclosed rent, term or escalations; only sizes were found.

  A 1,200 SF shop in Tier A must out-sell a national-average Crumbl just to hold rent at 8% of sales. A 2,500 SF café in Tier A must out-sell an average Panera.
- **NNN load (EST).** Each extra $10/SF of taxes, CAM and insurance adds $12,000 a year on 1,200 SF, which needs about $150k more sales at 8% occupancy. On 2,500 SF it adds $25,000 a year, or about $312k more sales. Many South End, Davis and Assembly listings are explicitly NNN, and actual Boston NNN charge levels were not found.
- **Where small formats pencil.** In Tier C and the low end of Tier B (Somerville at $36–51), a 1,200 SF counter bakery stays under 8% occupancy at about $450–700k in sales. Seaport and Back Bay ground floor suit only proven high-volume brands, or second-floor and kiosk formats (the sibling file cites second-floor F&B at up to $80 in the Seaport).
- **Possible negotiating leverage (unconfirmed).** Central Square has elevated vacancy (19 storefronts) and "unusually large" units. A bakery willing to take or subdivide oversized space may have leverage there. No rent evidence confirms this.
- **Downtown Crossing is tightening.** Broker-reported vacancy is 2.4%, and Uniqlo took 25,000 SF in October 2025. The CoStar figures of about $54–58 from 2021–24 likely understate 2026 asking rents. This is inference only.

## 2. Wages: Boston-Cambridge-Newton OEWS, bakery job-ad pay and tip income

### Takeaway
May 2025 OEWS medians for the Boston metro, via a third-party aggregator because bls.gov was blocked:
- Fast-food and counter workers (the barista/counter proxy): **$17.45/hr**.
- Food-preparation workers: **$19.07/hr**.
- First-line food-service supervisors: **about $22.64/hr** ($47,090/yr).
- Bakers: $18.54/hr (in the sibling file).

2026 Boston bakery job ads:
- **Front of house:** base pay of **$11.75–$15** plus **$5–9/hr in tips**, for **$18–24/hr all-in**. Tatte's base is below $15 because it uses the tip credit; Flour's base is $15.
- **Bakers:** **$18–22** at chains, **$20–30** for Flour's opening bread baker, and **$26–27** at a Seaport contract-food employer.
- **Back of house:** no tips. Flour adds a sales-based payout of about $2/hr.

## 3. Employer payroll burdens: Massachusetts UI, PFML, workers' comp and health-insurance rules

### Takeaway
For a new Massachusetts employer in 2026, the state charges are small:

| Charge | 2026 rate | Approx. cost per employee per year |
|---|---|---|
| Unemployment insurance | 2.42% on the first $15,000 | $363 |
| Workforce Training Fund | 0.056% on the same base | $8 |
| EMAC (employer medical assistance contribution) | Reportedly 0% in years 1–3 | $0 |
| PFML (paid family and medical leave) | 0.88% total; no employer share under 25 covered individuals | $0 for a small employer |

PFML's total stays at 0.88% in 2027, with the employer share moved from medical to family leave.

With federal FICA and FUTA added at statutory rates (not re-sourced here), the statutory burden is about **8.5–9% of wages** before workers' comp. The current workers' comp rate for bakeries was not found. The bakery manual rate (class 2003) was 2.75 per $100 of payroll in 2008, and statewide rates have been cut since, by 14.6% in 2024.

The state's employer "Fair Share" health contribution was repealed in 2013. The small-employer health rules found are administrative: an annual HIRD filing at 6+ employees and a Section 125 premium plan at 11+ FTEs.

## 4. Permits, license fees and time-to-open (Boston, Cambridge, Somerville)

### Takeaway
License and permit fees are trivial, a few hundred dollars a year:
- **Boston:** the CV license costs $100 plus $1 per seat, or $210 for takeout only. The ISD health permit costs $300 flat for retail food with takeout, or a $200 first-year minimum for a restaurant.
- **Cambridge:** the CV license costs $100 plus $1 per occupant.
- **Somerville:** the CV license costs $300.

Time is the real cost. The City says going from idea to open restaurant takes "a few months to a year or more," and a Zoning Board of Appeal (ZBA) case adds 3–6 months. Two 2025 Boston bakeries took about 5 months (Fuful) and about 12 months (Elephantine) from first report to soft opening.

On Oct 27, 2026, Mayor Wu proposed dropping zoning review for takeout service and cutting ownership transfers of existing restaurants from about 4 months to under 2 weeks. These remain proposals, not rules in effect.

### Inferences
- **Fees are rounding errors (EST).**
  - A takeout-only bakery in Boston pays about **$510 a year**: $210 for the CV plus $300 for the ISD retail-with-takeout permit.
  - A 20-seat bakery-café pays about **$320 in year one**: $120 for the CV plus the $200 ISD minimum. After that, the ISD fee is based on sales on an unknown scale, and the plan-review fee is also unknown.
  - Cambridge's CV runs about $100–150 at small occupancies; Somerville's is $300.
- **Carrying costs during permitting dominate (EST).** Base rent only, assuming no free-rent period (free-rent norms were not found):

  | Space | Monthly rent | Cost of a 6-month delay | Cost of a 12-month delay |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1,200 SF at $40/SF | $4,000 | $24k | $48k |
  | 1,200 SF at $100/SF | $10,000 | $60k | $120k |
  | 2,500 SF at $55/SF | $11,458 | $69k | $138k |

  A 6-month slip costs roughly 50–200 times a year of license fees, and more than the older reported average of about $5.6k in city fees.
- **Site selection is the main timing lever.**
  - A space with existing food use and an existing CV license avoids the public hearing for a new CV (sibling file).
  - A site where takeout is allowed as-of-right avoids a 3–6-month ZBA case.
  - Buying an existing bakery or café would become the fastest route if Wu's ownership-transfer change passes.
- **Takeout-led bakeries gain most from the Oct 2026 proposal.** If the change dropping zoning review for takeout is adopted, the ZBA step should disappear for many counter bakeries. Until then, plan around current rules.
- **Planning ranges (EST, synthesized from the above).**
  - Second-generation food space with an as-of-right use and a CV transfer: about 3–6 months.
  - New build-out with a CV hearing: about 6–12 months.
  - A ZBA case adds 3–6 months.
  - Worst reported cases run about 18 months.

### Gaps
- **Missing fee schedules.** Not found:
  - Cambridge and Somerville health-department food-establishment permit fees and plan-review timelines.
  - Brookline CV and food-permit fees (not searched).
  - Boston's current ISD plan-review fee, and the sales-based ISD fee scale for restaurants with takeout.
  - Building and occupancy permit fees, and Boston Fire Department hood and suppression permit costs.
- **Somerville CV fee.** The $300 figure is tied to the instructions for alcohol-serving applicants and needs confirmation for a bakery without alcohol.
- **No permitting-blamed bakery delay.** No 2025–26 report explicitly blames permitting for a Boston bakery's delay. The Elephantine and Fuful timelines run from announcement to opening with no cause stated, and the 12-to-18-month anecdote concerns a restaurant.
- **Reform timing.** Whether and when Wu's zoning changes take effect is unknown; they were still proposals as of Oct 27–29, 2026.
