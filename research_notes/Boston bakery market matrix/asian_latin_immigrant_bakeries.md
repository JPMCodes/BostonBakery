# Asian and Latin American/Caribbean bakeries in Greater Boston: operator matrix, menus, prices, best-sellers, business models (as of 5 Nov 2026)

> **How this was researched (read first).** Direct page fetching was blocked by this session's network policy (whatnow.com, bostonglobe.com, universalhub.com, parisbaguette.com, 1851franchise.com, franchisechatter.com and others returned proxy policy denials). Partway through, the session's shared web-search cap was also used up. **Every figure below therefore comes from search-engine extracts of the cited pages, not from my reading those pages in full.** Where an extract gave a number, I cite the page it was attributed to. When several aggregator pages were returned together and I could not tell which one held a given number, I cite the group and say so. Source-type tags used throughout: **[FP]** first-party (owner website, Toast or order.online storefront, franchisor store page or press release); **[PRESS]** news or trade press; **[AGG]** third-party menu or franchise aggregator (unverified scrape); **[DEL]** delivery app (usually marked up 10–30% or more); **[REV]** review or user-generated content; **[EST]** my own calculation or estimate. Benchmark: the premium artisan plain butter croissant costs about **$4.50–$6.00**. The coordinator supplied this band; I did not research it.

## 1. Which operators make up the segment, where are they, and are they open (Nov 2026)?

### Takeaway
The segment has three layers:
- **Two Korean franchise chains.** Tous les Jours has six Massachusetts store pages, several of them inside H Mart. Paris Baguette has three open Massachusetts cafés (Cambridge, North Quincy, and Bellingham, which opened June 2026). A new multi-unit franchisee is now targeting about 20 Greater Boston sites.
- **A thinning Chinatown/Beach Street cluster of Cantonese-style bakeries.** Several closed in 2023.
- **Scattered, community-anchored Latin and Caribbean independents.** Brazilian bakeries are in Everett and Framingham, Haitian in Mattapan, Dominican in Jamaica Plain, and Colombian in East Boston and Chelsea. I found no 85°C in Massachusetts.

### Inferences
- In units, the Korean chain layer is small. As of Nov 2026, Tous les Jours (6 store pages) is about twice Paris Baguette (3 open cafés) in Massachusetts. That is the reverse of their national positions (see section 5).
- Asian chains are clustered in Asian-grocery trade areas. PB Cambridge and TLJ Burlington and Quincy-Falls Blvd sit in or at H Mart; TLJ Allston, Malden and Quincy-Billings sit in Asian-American residential corridors. Newer PB units are moving to suburban standalone or anchored sites (The Abby's 600-unit residential complex; Bellingham next to Whole Foods).
- The Chinatown core is consolidating on one Beach Street block (Ho Yuen at 54, Taiwan Bakery at 56, Mei Sum at 36, Great Taste at 61–63). Since 2023 there have been at least three closures and one filed new entrant.
- Latin and Caribbean bakeries are spread across immigrant neighborhoods, one or two anchors each. None appears to run a multi-unit chain in Greater Boston. Santia's (JP plus Lawrence) is the closest to a two-market operator.

### Gaps
- **Paris Baguette count conflict.** What Now Boston (Oct 2026) names only Cambridge and Quincy as existing MA cafés, plus the June 2026 Bellingham opening. An undated Worcester Patch piece says PB "has five locations in Massachusetts with a dozen more in the works" ([Patch Worcester](https://patch.com/massachusetts/worcester/beloved-bakery-eyeing-central-ma-opening)). I could not reconcile these, possibly because Patch counted coming-soon sites. PB's [Massachusetts location list](https://parisbaguette.com/location-list/ma/) could not be fetched.
- **TLJ count conflict.** Six MA store pages were indexed. But Quincy-Billings was called the *sixth* MA store in July 2023, and the H Mart Quincy unit followed by Nov 2024, which implies seven. Either one earlier store closed or a store page was missed. Unresolved.
- Ownership (franchised or corporate) of PB Cambridge and of the five other TLJ units was not found.
- No Quincy or Malden Chinese bakeries were identified (searches returned Hong Kong results only). The same goes for Salvadoran or Mexican panaderías in East Boston, Chelsea or Revere, and Dominican bakeries in Hyde Park or Roslindale.
- I did not verify the open status of Golden Crust, Golden Krust (Mattapan), Beard Papa's, Taiwan Bakery, the Allston and Somerville Brazilian listings, or Ba Lẹ's Quincy and Malden branches.

## 2. Menu: category mix, service model, beverage program, SKU counts

### Takeaway
Each cultural sub-segment has a distinct, high-variety, low-ticket core assortment:
- Chinatown: buns and tarts.
- Brazilian: salgados and cheese bread.
- Dominican: picaderas plus cakes by the slice.
- Haitian: pâté.
- Colombian: pan de bono and buñuelo.

The Korean chains and newer Chinatown entrants layer a café format (coffee, matcha, bubble tea, seating) over a bun, croissant and cream-cake assortment. Independents more often bundle the bakery with a second format: a restaurant, buffet, grocery or catering.

### Inferences
- **Savory share is highest in the Latin, Caribbean and Vietnamese independents**: salgados, pastelitos, pâté and bánh mì work as meal-replacement items. The Korean chains and the Chinatown bakeries lean sweet (buns, tarts, cream cakes). That changes ticket composition and daypart: breakfast and lunch at the independents, versus snack, coffee and afternoon at the café chains.
- **The beverage program separates the chains from the old-line independents.** Matcha, coffee and bubble tea appear at TLJ, Great Taste, Golden Crust and Japonaise. - **SKU counts** were not found for any operator.
- Prices for the PB and TLJ beverage programs (coffee, latte, bubble tea) in MA were not found.
- I found no menus for Haitian cake (gato), Mid-Autumn mooncakes, or quinceañera cakes in Boston.

## 3. Prices 2025–2026, and how they compare with the premium artisan croissant band ($4.50–$6.00)

### Takeaway
Most priced items fall well below the premium artisan band:
- **Korean chains:** croissants and sweet buns about $3.35–$4.40, roughly 10–40% below the band. These are aggregator data, not Massachusetts first-party prices.
- **Chinatown buns and egg tarts:** about $1.00–$2.40, roughly 20–50% of an artisan croissant.
- **Latin snack pastries:** pastelito, pan de bono and buñuelo about $2, roughly 33–44% of the band.

The exceptions price inside or above the band: Japonaise's specialty azuki creams ($5.50–$6.00, first-party Toast), large savory or meal items (coxinha $6.45; Ba Lẹ bánh mì $7.50–$8.50 in 2026), and cake slices ($6.50–$9.50).

### Inferences
- **Three-tier price ladder.** The extracts suggest these tiers:
  1. Sub-$2.50 "community" items: Chinatown buns and tarts, Latin pastries.
  2. A $3.35–$4.40 "Korean café-chain" tier.
  3. The $4.50–$6.00 artisan band, which only Japonaise's specialty items and some chain specialty croissants (TLJ strawberry, $5.25–$6.45) reach.

  For comparable sweet buns, the Korean chains charge about 2–4× Chinatown prices: PB's $3.40–$4.40 against Chinatown's "under $2" and $1.00 custard bun.
- **Prices have roughly doubled over the past decade at the independents, but stayed low in absolute terms.** Ba Lẹ went from $3.50–$4.50 (pre-2019) to $7.50–$8.50 (2026). Mei Sum went from ~$3 (2015) to ~$6. La Sultana went from $1.15 to $1.65 to $2.00. These are reported point estimates, not a controlled series.
- **Delivery-app markups observed:** +30% (Japonaise kinako azuki, $6.00 to $7.80 on DoorDash) and ≈+38% (Ba Lẹ BBQ beef, $7.48 aggregator to $10.29 on Uber Eats). This confirms DEL prices should not be used as shelf prices.
- **Celebration cakes.** Chain whole cream cakes cost about $36–$59 (TLJ) and $39–$48 (PB estimate), roughly 7–13 artisan croissants each. No independent whole-cake prices were found to compare.

### Gaps
- **No Massachusetts first-party prices for Paris Baguette or Tous les Jours.** All PB and TLJ figures are national aggregator, delivery, or other-state listings. MA shelf prices may differ.
- **Missing prices:** a 2025–26 Chinatown pineapple, cocktail or BBQ-pork bun price (only "under $2" ranges); milk-bread loaf price; any Haitian patty or Haitian cake price; mooncake box prices for Boston (Mid-Autumn fell on Oct 6, 2025 per [Time Out LA](https://www.timeout.com/los-angeles/restaurants/where-to-buy-mooncakes-in-los-angeles)); whole tres leches or bizcocho prices; and coffee, latte or bubble-tea prices at any operator, other than Great Taste's old $2.95 listing.
- Many aggregator rows (Allmenus, Sage Menu, Foodboss) are undated or old. They should be treated as lower bounds.

- Across sub-segments, best-sellers are **low-ticket, high-frequency signature items** (egg tart, pâté, pastelito, pan de bono, bánh mì). ## 5. The Asian bakery-café chain model: Paris Baguette and Tous les Jours franchise economics, and Massachusetts expansion 2023–2026

### Takeaway
Both chains run about 5% royalties plus a 0–3% ad fund, with $0.7–1.9M build-outs. Paris Baguette's 2026 FDD reports **franchised average 2025 sales of about $3.05M (162 cafés; median about $2.99M)**. Tous les Jours's reported unit volumes are roughly **$1.9–2.0M** (aggregator figures, not verified against the FDD).

Nationally, PB opened a record 77 cafés in 2025 and targets about 150 in 2026. In Massachusetts it has been slow: three cafés in about six years. That changed in 2026, when a new multi-unit franchisee (the Sahnis) moved from an 8-unit deal toward about 20 Greater Boston sites. TLJ grew steadily in MA from 2019 to 2024 (Natick, Malden, Quincy ×2, plus Allston and Burlington) through local franchisees and H Mart counters.

### Inferences
These are my own calculations ([EST]) from the cited figures.
- **PB fee load per average franchised café:**
  - Royalty: 5% × $3,050,434 ≈ **$152.5K a year**.
  - Ad fund: 2–3% ≈ $61.0K–$91.5K.
  - Total ≈ **$213.5K–$244.0K a year**, or about 7–8% of sales, before rent, labor, COGS and debt service.
- **TLJ fee load:** 5% × $1,984,809 ≈ $99.2K a year, plus an ad fund of $0–$59.5K (0–3%).
- **Sales-to-investment ratios:**
  - PB: $3.05M against $0.73M–$1.86M ≈ **1.6×–4.2×**.
  - TLJ: about $1.98M against $0.73M–$1.70M ≈ **1.2×–2.7×**.
  - On reported volumes, PB's average unit is about 1.5× TLJ's ($3.05M ÷ $1.98M). Both figures are unaudited restatements, and TLJ's H Mart counters are a smaller-box format.
- **Daily throughput (illustrative):** $3.05M a year ≈ $8,350 a day over 365 days. At an *assumed* $12–$15 average ticket (not sourced), that is about 560–700 transactions a day. That is far above any plausible independent-bakery volume at $1–$2 items, but no independent revenue data exists to test the comparison.
- **The PB franchised range is very wide** ($1.42M–$7.04M). The best units likely sit in dense Asian-American or urban trade areas. MA's lag (three cafés against 77 openings nationally in 2025 alone) looks like a franchisee-capacity constraint, not a demand ceiling: one Quincy franchisee took over three years from signing to opening. The 2026 Sahni development deal is the first multi-unit push. Its first sites are outer-suburban (Bellingham, Mansfield), not Boston proper.
- **TLJ's MA model is opportunistic:** single-unit local franchisees (Harry Eng in Quincy) plus H Mart in-store counters. That likely explains its larger MA unit count with lower volumes.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the primary FDDs (Wisconsin DFI, California DFPI, Minnesota CARDS) for either brand. Item 5, 6, 7 and 19 figures are third-party restatements and partly conflict (PB ad fund 2% vs 3%; PB 2026 average $3.03–3.05M vs "$2.9M"; TLJ investment ranges).
- TLJ's 2026 FDD Item 19 table (average, median, n, inclusion of company units) was not found.
- There is no MA-specific unit-level performance for either brand, and no Franchise Times or Restaurant Business coverage of MA deals surfaced.
- Ownership of PB Cambridge (corporate or franchise) and the total size of the Sahni development agreement (8 units contracted vs about 20 aspirational) were not confirmed.

## 6. The independent operator model (volume, price, hours, payment, formats, wholesale)

### Takeaway
Across the sources, independents show these traits:
- Very low price points ($1–$2 core items).
- Early or long hours (5 am openings).
- Cash-only or informal practices at some shops.
- Daily sell-outs that suggest production-capped volume.
- Long tenure (founded 1976, 1985, 1992).
- Frequent **format bundling**: bakery with a restaurant, buffet, pizzeria, grocery or catering.

Delivery apps are used widely, at a 30–38% markup. I found no sourced evidence on wholesale to restaurants or markets.

### Inferences
- The independent model is **high-frequency, low-ticket, production-capped**. Sell-outs, lines and 5 am openings point to demand above capacity at the anchors (Le Foyer, Au Beurre Chaud, Ho Yuen). For these shops, growth means more ovens and labor, or a second unit (Ba Lẹ, Santia's), not more marketing.
- Format bundling (restaurant, buffet, grocery, catering) is the independents' answer to low item prices. It raises ticket size and spreads fixed rent and labor over more dayparts.
- The 1976–1992 founding dates and community-anchor role suggest owner-occupied or long-lease economics and family labor. That fits the observation that closures cluster around retirement (Hing Shing) and lease events (the 2015 Chinatown corner bakery). This is inference; there is no direct ownership or lease data.

## 7. Performance signals: openings, closures, expansions, relocations (2023–2026)

### Takeaway
- **Asian chains:** net expansion (PB Quincy 2023, PB Bellingham 2026, TLJ Quincy ×2 in 2023–24, with a 20-unit PB pipeline announced Oct 2026).
- **Chinatown independents:** net contraction (Hing Shing and Eldo closed in 2023, Double Chin and Bao Bao gone), offset only by a filed newcomer (Golden Crust).
- **Latin and Caribbean independents:** stable at the anchors (Le Foyer, Santia's, La Sultana open in 2026), with some Allston and Somerville Brazilian listings flagged closed and selective branching (Ba Lẹ to Quincy and Malden).

### Inferences
- The clearest structural shift: **traditional Chinatown cake and pastry shops have closed** (2023 cluster: retirement plus lease or landlord exposure), **while Korean franchise cafés expand in the suburbs and in Asian-grocery trade areas** (Quincy, Malden, Burlington, Bellingham). Celebration-cake and pastry demand in the Asian-American market is plausibly migrating from Chinatown independents to suburban chain cafés. This is inference; I found no sales data.
- Within Chinatown, new entry is happening in hybrid formats (Golden Crust: bakery, waffles and coffee, open 8 am–10 pm). That mirrors the chains' café model more than the old-line tray bakeries.
- Latin and Caribbean anchors show resilience rather than growth. The one notable expansion is a Vietnamese bánh mì operator (Ba Lẹ) branching into Asian-American suburbs (Quincy, Malden). That is the same geography the Korean chains target.
