## 1. Boston: which bakery items sell the most, and what volumes have owners disclosed?

### Takeaway
No new unit-volume disclosure from 2025–26 surfaced for any Boston operator. The only volume figures are dated, conflicting or narrow:
- **Mike's Pastry:** 32–36k cannoli a week. A daily capacity figure ("more than 5,000" or "up to 6,000" a day) is consistent with that, but WBUR's "about 5,000 shells a week" (2023) is about 7× lower.
- **Flour:** about 220k sticky buns a year, or about 600 a day. The latest source still traces to about 2019.
- **Lakon:** about 2,000 croissants per weekend day across two shops (2023).
- **South End Buttery:** 148 Thanksgiving pie orders (2023).

Owner-named best-sellers are classic flavors:
- Rosenfeld's: plain, sesame and everything bagels, which tie for most popular.
- Union Square Donuts: salted toffee and triple chocolate (2018).
- Cheryl-Ann's: round challah at Rosh Hashanah (2012).

Platform rankings show savory items winning on delivery: Tatte's top "most liked" DoorDash items are its breakfast sandwiches, not pastries. The one Boston sales-mix data point is a for-sale listing for a Boston Irish bakery-café: morning pastries are 18% and desserts/cookies 9% of $2.15M in gross revenue.

### Inferences
- **Mike's reconciliation (Estimate).**
  - Chowhound's 32–36k a week works out to about 4,570–5,140 a day. That fits Food Network's "more than 5,000" a day, with "up to 6,000" read as peak-day capacity.
  - WBUR's "about 5,000 shells a week" is the outlier. It would mean about 714 a day across four shops plus online, under about 180 per shop per day, which is hard to square with Mike's lines.
  - WBUR's number is identical to Food Network's daily figure but per week, exactly a factor of 7. That points to a day/week slip in one source or in the search extract.

- **Food is a minority of revenue even in pastry-forward cafés.**
  - Starbucks, which is coffee-led, gets about 19% of revenue from food (unverified).
  - The Boston hybrid bakery-café gets 27% from morning pastries plus desserts and cookies, with sandwiches and breakfast on top.
  - The working model for a Boston bakery-café is therefore beverage plus savory breakfast and lunch as the revenue base, with pastry as the reason to visit (Estimate).
- **Midday matters more for bakery cafés than for coffee cafés.** The old Technomic-style finding that bakery cafés own midday is consistent with Tatte's and Flour's lunch menus and the 2–4 pm lull (prior). ### Inferences
- **Boston gets trends through three channels (Estimate):**
  - Immigrant- or Europe-trained independents carry the croissant-format and pistachio trends: Lakon's cube, PRB's crookie, Elephantine's and Michette's pistachio, Bakey's Broissant.
  - Asian dessert franchises and small chains carry mochi donuts: Mochinut, Pon De Joy.
  - National chains carry Dubai chocolate, matcha and the cookie croissant: Starbucks.
- **Trend formats sit at the top of the price ladder.** Pistachio, cube and Dubai-style croissants cluster at $7.50–$9.00, against $4.50–$5.50 for a plain croissant (prior). That is roughly a 35–100% premium for format and flavor innovation (Estimate).
- **Scarcity is the operating model for trend items.** A weekend-only run that sells out by noon (PRB), rotating monthly specials (Lakon), and periodic stock-outs (Japonaise matcha) cap production to limit waste on short-shelf-life, high-labor SKUs. The lines and sell-outs double as marketing (Estimate).
- **Pistachio is the durable flavor carrier in Boston; "Dubai chocolate" as such is not visible.** Either local coverage missed it or it is an unoccupied slot. Pistachio items already span every price tier, from a $2.75 Sofra shortbread to an $8.50 Elephantine croissant. A pistachio-kataifi item would sit on an existing flavor base (Estimate).
- **Mochi donuts are a maturing trend in Boston:** launches from 2021–22, then suburban roll-out (Andover, 2024). Basque cheesecake and focaccia are niche, single-operator signals. Neither has evidence of being a sales driver (Estimate).

### Gaps
- **No Boston operator has quantified any trend item** (share of sales or units) for pistachio, matcha, cube or crookie croissants, mochi donuts, Basque cheesecake or focaccia.
- **Not found at Boston bakeries:** "Dubai chocolate" items. Croissant "supreme" (round) was not searched. A dedicated matcha search was skipped because the prior notes already covered it.
- **Not verified for 2026:** PRB Boulangerie's current units, Mochi Dough's status, and Pon De Joy's Allston opening date.
- **No national 2025–26 penetration data** for these trend items, except cheesecake (Datassential) and cruffin (Tastewise).

## 4. Holiday and seasonal volume in Boston

### Takeaway
Thanksgiving is the only holiday with Boston order counts, and they span about two orders of magnitude:
- **South End Buttery:** 148 pie orders, its busiest year in five (2023).
- **Montilio's:** about 10,000 pies (undated; outside the core area).

For Flour, Petsi and Mike's, only program structure is documented:
- **Petsi (2025):** nine 10-inch pies, an order deadline of 20 Nov, and pickup on 25–26 Nov.
- **Flour:** pickup or delivery within 15 miles, a "Piefecta," and 2026 list prices of $30–$40 (prior).

Other holidays have qualitative evidence only:
- **Rosh Hashanah challah:** Cheryl-Ann's round challah is the "item in demand" (2012); Mamaleh's round challah costs $10 (2023–24); Bakey takes pre-orders.
- **Pączki:** Dorchester's Polish Triangle markets sell them, but some are trucked in from Worcester and New York bakeries.
- **Mooncakes:** Ho Yuen sells them year-round at about $5–$8 each (2024).

No Boston figures surfaced for Lunar New Year, Valentine's or holiday cookies.

### Inferences
- **Thanksgiving revenue scales with capacity, not price (Estimate).**
  - At Flour's 2026 list prices of $30–$40, every 1,000 pies is worth about $30k–$40k.
  - South End Buttery's 148 orders, assuming one pie per order at $30–$40 (the Boston band from the prior notes), come to about $4.4k–$5.9k.
  - Montilio's ~10,000 pies would be six figures in one week at almost any plausible price.
  - The structures operators use (order deadlines about a week ahead, two-day pickup windows, Petsi's walk-in-only sale week, Take 'n Bake/Thaw formats) exist to cap and schedule production.
- **Pączki are under-served inside the city (Estimate).** Dorchester's Polish markets import part of their supply from Worcester and New York. Out-of-state specialists report one-day volumes in the tens of thousands (Cops & Doughnuts, ~70,000), so a Boston donut or bakery operator could treat Fat Tuesday as a single-day spike product. This is low confidence: no Boston demand figure exists.
- **High Holiday challah is a pre-order, kosher-led niche** served by a few operators (Cheryl-Ann's, Mamaleh's, Bakey, Rosenfeld's, Kupel's) at about $10 a round loaf. Cheryl-Ann's fire closure (about 2021–24) shows how concentrated supply is.
- **Mooncakes are not only a Mid-Autumn spike** at the Chinatown anchor: Ho Yuen sells them year-round. The $5–$8 unit price makes them the highest-ticket single item in that segment (prior). No 2025 volume exists.

### Gaps
- **No Boston volume figures** for:
  - Flour, Petsi or Mike's Thanksgiving pies; Flour's December holiday program.
  - Holiday cookie boxes; Rosh Hashanah challah (any operator); Fat Tuesday pączki in Dorchester or South Boston.
  - Lunar New Year cakes or buns; Mid-Autumn mooncakes in 2025 (count or gift-box prices); Valentine's Day.
  - Lunar New Year and Valentine's had no dedicated search (budget).
- **No share of annual sales from holidays or pre-orders** for any Boston operator (also a gap in UE and the model-economics notes).
- **Unverified operating status:** Kupel's current holiday ordering and the 2026 status of DJ's Market and Baltic Deli.
