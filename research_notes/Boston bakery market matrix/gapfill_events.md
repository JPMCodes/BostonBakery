# Gap-fill: Dated Openings, Expansions, Ownership Changes and Closures, with Causes (Boston bakery market, 2019 – Nov 2026)

> **Read first.**
> - **What this file is.** It extends `openings_closures_2019_2026.md` and the Tatte material in `premium_artisan_bakery_cafes.md`. It records only new events or new detail. Items repeated from sibling notes for context are marked **(carried over; not re-verified)**.
> - **Method and limits.** All 28 allotted web searches were used. The one WebFetch attempt (universalhub.com) failed with a DNS block, so **universalhub.com must be allowed in the session's network settings** before its bakeries tag page can be read. No other fetches were tried, because sibling sessions had already logged 403s for bostonrestaurants.blogspot.com, nbcboston.com, boston.com, bostonglobe.com, thecrimson.com and others.
> - **Every finding is extract-based.** Findings come from search-engine summaries of the cited URLs, not from full article text.
> - **Dates** are publication or report dates unless an event date is given.
> - **Grades:**
>   - **HARD:** a first-party record, such as a company newsroom post or press release, a landlord or broker lease announcement, a law-firm deal note, or a company page.
>   - **OWNER:** an owner or company statement, relayed by press or posted on social media.
>   - **PRESS:** a fact or characterization from a reporter or food blog.
>   - **3P:** an aggregator, directory, event listing, wiki or user comment.
>   - **EST:** my own inference or arithmetic.
> - **Cause basis:** **S** = stated by the owner or company; **R** = characterized by the press; **I** = my inference; **NR** = not retrieved.
> - **Scope.** No menu or price data is collected here, by design.

## 1. Tatte: openings by year, closed units, the DC/VA/MD/NJ/NY/CT timeline, and Act III ownership

### Takeaway
- **Ownership.** Ron Shaich's Act III took majority control of Tatte from Panera **in 2018**, per Shaich's deal counsel. Shaich had carved Tatte out of JAB's 2017 Panera buyout. No source discloses the price or the stake size.
- **2019 lawsuit.** Act III sued Panera after Panera hired Tatte's president.
- **Out-of-state growth started in August 2020**, mid-pandemic, with DC's West End café. DC-area units numbered about 10 by August 2023. New Jersey followed in May 2025. A first New York City café was listed for a September 2026 grand opening (third-party listing only). A Darien, CT lease was announced in August 2026, with no opening confirmed.
- **Closures.** The only one found is **205 Broadway in Kendall Square (October 2019)**. No reason was retrieved, and two other Kendall Tattes stayed open.

### Inferences
- **[EST] Order of events.** Act III took control (2018), then opened the first out-of-state unit (Aug 2020), reached about 10 DC-area units within three years, then entered New Jersey (2025) and New York City and Connecticut (2026). Growth beyond Massachusetts started only after the Panera exit. No source states that one caused the other.
- **[EST] Counter-cyclical expansion.** Tatte opened in DC in August 2020, while Au Bon Pain was closing Boston units (see `openings_closures_2019_2026.md`). The likely reason is pandemic-era lease availability, but no source cites lease terms.
- **[EST] DC is now Tatte's second home market.** About 21 of about 50 units, roughly 40%, are in the DC area, using the Wikipedia DC count against the Axios total. That makes the Boston-area share well below the "half" Wikipedia states.
- **[EST] Pruning, not exit.** The one closure found (205 Broadway, 2019) sat in a cluster where two other Kendall units stayed open.
- **[EST] Competing camps.** The 2019 Kelley lawsuit shows that Panera/JAB and Act III became competitors for bakery-café talent after they split.

### Gaps
- Price, stake size and exact date of Act III's 2018 purchase. Outcome of the 2019 Delaware suit.
- Year-by-year Massachusetts unit counts (2019–2026). Opening dates for Burlington, Hingham and Newton Centre.
- Confirmation that the NYC (315 Park Ave S), Garden City, Darien and Burlington units actually opened.
- Any Tatte closure after October 2019. None surfaced, but that is not proof none happened.
- The stated reason for the 205 Broadway closure.

### Inferences
- **[EST] Real estate and retirement dominate the stated causes.** In this evidence, independents that closed in the window name the owner's life cycle (retirement) or real estate (lease non-renewal, rent, redevelopment) far more often than falling demand. Party Favors is the only closure that explicitly blames post-pandemic demand, together with costs.
- **[EST] Closures without a stated cause cluster in chain or multi-unit portfolios.** Tatte, Sweet, Georgetown, Mamaleh's and Swissbäkers fall here. Portfolio decisions are rarely explained in public, so cause data is biased toward independents.
- **[EST] Growth since 2020 has followed two routes:**
  1. **Capital-backed or franchised expansion outside Boston.** Tatte went from DC (2020) to NJ (2025) to NYC and CT (2026). PopUp signed a regional franchise deal (2026).
  2. **Local brands adding units at tourist, civic or captive-traffic sites.** Examples: Mike's at Logan, Flour on Boston Common, Bakey at Faneuil Hall, Brick Street on Charles St.
- **[EST] Space recycling points to site-level demand that outlasts individual operators.** At least seven bakery or café spaces were re-let to bakery concepts; the list is in section 6, Inferences.
- **[EST] Single-product dessert formats run in booms and busts.** Cupcake shops went through this between 2014 and 2023, and cookie chains are doing so nationally in 2026. In both cases expansion is fast, closures follow, and the brand falls back on shipping or online sales.
