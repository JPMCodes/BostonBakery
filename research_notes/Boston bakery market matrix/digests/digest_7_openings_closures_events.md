**Model inferences (all Inf.; OC strength scale: High = several independent reports covering several events; Moderate = consistent but limited; Weak = single case or indirect).**
- OC, ownership routes: (1) restaurant-industry capital (Tatte: Panera, then Act III); (2) founder-held, slower growth (Flour, USD); (3) franchise area development (PB, Crumbl). Routes 1–2 show growth with no surfaced closures; route 3 shows the most volatility (Crumbl nationally) and the slowest delivery against announced pipelines (PB).
- OC, models that don't work: (a) **high**: commuter-traffic national bakery-café chain (ABP: five confirmed closures plus one probable, 2019–2024, full exit; mall, transit squares, hospital corridor, FiDi, transit hub; decline began before COVID, then pandemic traffic loss hit core sites; no source states office vacancy or remote work); (b) **weak–moderate**: small independents' transit-hub satellites, cut first (Swissbäkers North Station); (c) **weak for Boston, moderate nationally**: trend-driven single-product dessert franchises (Crumbl), echoing the cupcake cycle (e.g., Todd English 2014); (d) **weak**: long-lived independent specialty bakeries under cost pressure (35-year Brookline bakery): tenure does not protect thin-margin, labor-heavy formats.
- OC, models that work: (a) **moderate**: chef-founder multi-unit bakery-café with central production and selective sites (Flour: 8 → 10 units 2018–2025, about two net units in seven years, incl. a city concession; caveat: no closure reports ≠ no closures); (b) **moderate on growth**: local bakery-café backed by restaurant-industry capital (Tatte; unit outcomes unknown); (c) **moderate**: specialty brand expanding into suburban retail centers (USD; matches a broader 2023–26 shift of growth away from the urban core); (d) **weak–moderate**: Asian bakery-café via area franchisees (PB: suburban, slower than its 12–20-unit pipeline). Crumbl entered GB mostly via suburbs (Methuen, Medford, Saugus) plus Central Sq and Dorchester: the most at-risk expansion group.
- OC, survival traits (weak): distinctive chef/founder product; central commissary; sites where residents, tourists and civic visitors sustain demand beyond commuters; patient slow growth or a well-funded capital partner.
- OC, labor: retail-bakery organizing came with the 2021–23 union wave at independent cafés in Somerville and Cambridge, not inside large chains; a union contract adds a fixed labor-cost structure for a small single-site bakery-café (effect on Forge unknown).
- GE, Tatte: growth beyond MA started only after the Panera exit (no source states causation); the Aug 2020 DC entry was counter-cyclical, likely using pandemic-era lease availability (no source cites lease terms); DC is now its second home market: about 21 of about 50 units (~40%, Wikipedia DC count vs Axios total), so the Boston-area share is well below the "half" Wikipedia states; the Kelley suit shows Panera/JAB and Act III became rivals for talent; 205 Broadway was pruning, not exit.
- GE, dessert formats: single-product dessert formats boom and bust (cupcakes 2014–2023; cookie chains nationally 2026: fast expansion, closures, fallback to shipping/online); the single-dessert slot passed to cookie chains (Crumbl from 2020, Levain from 2022, Insomnia); the GB cookie-chain build-out has plateaued (no new opening after Nov 2024; no core closure confirmed). Cupcake survivors moved to lower-fixed-cost channels (Sweet online/catering, Georgetown shipping, Kickass retail beside its bakery).
- GE, others: Mike's expands only into captive venues (university square, lifestyle center, arena, airport), not residential neighborhoods. Modern's Medford satellite follows its customers into inner suburbs (about 20 years without press-reported trouble if 2004 holds; low confidence). Bagels arrive by two routes: franchised NYC brand (PopUp: 3 units in about nine months; next wave outside GB) and local pop-up → storefront (Brick Street: about three permanent shops plus a market concept in about 18 months); local independents started the wave (called a wave in early 2024, a year before PopUp). Brick Street's espresso and seating should lengthen visits and raise the ticket ("second sites get bigger" pattern in the premium notes). Party Favors' hybrid model was squeezed on both halves: party goods vs online and mass retail (Party City's liquidation is context only) and celebration cakes vs specialists and chains (sibling notes: Nothing Bundt Cakes, the Korean chains' cream cakes). Swissbäkers is the clearest case of independent multi-unit distress (closed every shop, reopened some, dropped its transit satellite, filed Ch. 11).

### 6e. Event-pattern inferences (all Inf.)

- OC, ABP: the Boston unwinding began at least three to six months before COVID (Prudential Sept 2019, probable; Central Sq and Cambridge St Dec 2019) and ended almost four years after the March 2020 shutdowns (South Station Jan 2024), so the pandemic sped up a chain-level retreat. Closures covered every commuter location type (mall/office complex, neighborhood transit squares, hospital/West End corridor, FiDi, intercity hub); Davis Sq closed despite being "most popular", pointing to chain-level portfolio or lease decisions.
- OC, causes: independents' captured reasons point to cumulative cost pressure, not one shock; rent shocks are a long-running failure mode (Boschetto's 2013, Violette); recurring modes also include chain-level retrenchment (ABP) and pandemic traffic loss at transit/office sites (ABP State St, Swissbäkers North Station). OC confirmed no rent-driven 2019–26 closure (superseded, C19). Le Pain Quotidien should not be counted as a Boston exit.
- OC (weak), site turnover: local brands replacing national ones at high-visibility sites: HSq (ABP closed Mar 2016, Flour opened Oct 2016; same square, not confirmed same space; C9) and Boston Common (Earl of Sandwich → Flour, 2024–25). Central Sq "has turned over between chain formats" (ABP out; Crumbl and PB in).
- GE, neighborhoods: Chinatown and North End in-window closures followed the owners' life cycle (Maria's 2019, Hing Shing 2023), not falling demand; lease shocks are the older mode (Boschetto's 2013; the Chinatown corner bakery 2015). Kendall churn = redevelopment (Bailey & Sage, Rebecca's) + Tatte pruning; new bakeries keep arriving (Bakey 2025); no source links a Kendall closure to lab or office vacancy. Downtown shows a format shift, not a demand collapse: commuter grab-and-go closed 2019–2024 (ABP, Sweet, Panera Cares, Swissbäkers North Station); openings since 2024 sit at tourist and civic anchors (Boston Common, Faneuil Hall, Union St on the Freedom Trail, Charles St); the one 2026 closure is the office-atrium food-hall unit (Mamaleh's, the only in-window bagel closure; the hall started strongly in 2022, so any decline came later). Fits weaker office demand, but no source states it.
- GE, cupcakes: three waves: 2014 (Kickass, Todd English, as Crumbs collapsed after the fad peaked), 2019 (Sweet shrank to online and catering), 2023 (Georgetown left). By 2023 no stand-alone cupcake chain store was found in the core; GE dates the stand-alone cupcake retail cycle about 2008–2023.
- GE, causes: 2025–26 owner-stated causes are about leases (WPF, Party Favors; both long-running neighborhood stores; WPF kept selling through grocery and its remaining stores). Real estate and retirement dominate stated causes; closures without a stated cause cluster in chain or multi-unit portfolios (Tatte, Sweet, Georgetown, Mamaleh's, Swissbäkers), so cause data is biased toward independents.
