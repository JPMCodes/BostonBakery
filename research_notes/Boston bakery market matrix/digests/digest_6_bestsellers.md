Grades are the notes' own:
- **HD**: hard data (platform/POS data, first-party menus and ordering pages, SEC filings).

- **DEL**: 3P aggregator (delivery app). Delivery-app prices are typically marked up 10–30% (prior notes).
- **EST**: estimate, the researcher's arithmetic or inference.
- **IND**: industry (Datassential, Circana, Technomic).

**Inf. (EST):**
- **Three trend channels:**
  - Immigrant- or Europe-trained independents carry croissant formats and pistachio: Lakon's cube, PRB's crookie, Elephantine's and Michette's pistachio, Bakey's Broissant.
  - Asian dessert franchises and small chains carry mochi donuts: Mochinut, Pon De Joy.
  - National chains carry Dubai chocolate, matcha and the cookie croissant: Starbucks.
- **Scarcity is the operating model for trend items.** PRB's weekend-only run sells out by noon, Lakon rotates monthly specials, and Japonaise's matcha items periodically sell out. These limits cap production to limit waste on short-shelf-life, high-labor SKUs, and the lines and sell-outs double as marketing.
- **Pistachio is the durable flavor carrier; "Dubai chocolate" as such is not visible.** Either local coverage missed it or it is an unoccupied slot. Pistachio spans every tier, from a $2.75 Sofra shortbread to an $8.50 Elephantine croissant, so a pistachio-kataifi item would sit on an existing base.
- **Mochi donuts are maturing:** launches in 2021–22, then a roll-out to Andover in 2024. Basque cheesecake and focaccia are niche, single-operator signals, and none of the three is shown to drive sales.
- **Classic flavors carry bagels and donuts:** Rosenfeld's top three, PopUp's five flavors, USD's salted toffee, triple chocolate and maple bacon, and Kane's honey dip and "Best Sellers" pack. A narrow core plus rotating limited-time flavors is enough. Specialty SKUs (gluten-free, seasonal, collaborations) widen the customer base rather than drive volume.

### 4e. Researcher inferences (EST)

- **Mike's reconciliation.**
  - 32–36k a week works out to about 4,570–5,140 a day, which fits "more than 5,000" a day, with "up to 6,000" read as peak-day capacity.
  - WBUR's ~5,000 shells a week is the outlier. It would mean about 714 a day across four shops plus online, under about 180 per shop per day, which is "hard to square with Mike's lines."
  - It equals Food Network's daily figure but per week, exactly ×7, which points to a day/week slip in a source or extract.
  - **Working figure: about 4,500–6,000 cannoli a day chain-wide (about 32–42k a week), before the Logan unit opened (Sept 2026).** Verify against WBUR.
  - HER's revenue math (about $13–15M a year at $8) is unchanged and remains an order-of-magnitude bound.
- **Volume classes.**
  - Mike's daily cannoli ≈8× Flour's chain-wide sticky buns (about 5,000 vs about 600 a day), and Flour's figure covers several units.
  - Lakon in 2023 made about 1,000 croissants per shop on a peak weekend day.
  - So at the top independents, a hero line runs from hundreds to about 1,000 units per shop on a peak day. The North End icon is a different class.
- **Flour has added at least 2 units since 2019** (Boston Common 2025, Brigham Circle 2026; p), so its sticky-bun volume may exceed the 2019 figure. No source confirms it.
- **Delivery rewards savory and icon items:** Tatte's breakfast sandwiches, Ba Lẹ's bánh mì, Santia's cake and empanadas, Mike's cannoli. For all-day cafés the delivery best-seller is the savory breakfast item; pastry hero status shows in press and awards, not platform rankings.
- **Scarcity signal.** PRB sells out by noon, Lakon has lines, USD sold out (2013), and Ho Yuen and Rosenfeld's sell out (p). This supports the prior view that capacity, not demand, binds the top independents.
- **Thanksgiving counts** span about two orders of magnitude (148 orders vs about 10,000 pies).
- **Pączki are under-served in the city.**
  - Dorchester's markets import part of their supply from Worcester and New York.
  - Out-of-state specialists report one-day volumes in the tens of thousands (Cops & Doughnuts, about 70,000), so Fat Tuesday could be a single-day spike product.
  - Low confidence: no Boston demand figure exists.
- **High Holiday challah** is a pre-order, kosher-led niche (Cheryl-Ann's, Mamaleh's, Bakey, Rosenfeld's, Kupel's) at about $10 a round loaf. Cheryl-Ann's fire closure (about 2021–24) shows how concentrated supply is.
- **Mooncakes are not only a Mid-Autumn spike** at the Chinatown anchor: Ho Yuen sells them year-round. At $5–$8 they are the highest-ticket single item in that segment (p). No 2025 volume exists.
- **Provisional national bakery-café ranking.** It weights current multi-source signals over older ones, is consistent with the UE ladder, and does not change it.
  - **A:** cookies (fastest unit growth in 2026, plus Square's 2021 growth); croissants (ubiquity, 2025 unit gains, a PB top-3 slot); espresso drinks (Toast +3–4%).
  - **B:** muffins, cinnamon rolls, donuts (including mochi), cheesecake formats, brioche buns (basis: ubiquity, delivery dessert rankings, flavor growth).
  - **C (no current data):** scones, kouign-amann, focaccia, bagels, sourdough. Sourdough has only 2020–21 Square data and a 2026 trend-list mention.
