### 5k. Permits and regulation
**MA has no statewide cottage-food law.** Boston Inspectional Services Department (ISD), Health Division, **Retail Residential Kitchen Permit** (A; repeated in B as pn), with the MA baseline in parentheses:
- Who permits: ISD Health Division (retail elsewhere: local board of health; home-kitchen wholesale needs the DPH "License for Food Processing and/or Distribution at Wholesale") [a139–a141].
- Foods: only foods safely kept at room temperature, such as cookies, jams and other baked goods (MA: non-potentially hazardous foods: baked goods, jams, jellies) [a139–a141].
- Sales: online, mail, or venues such as farmers markets; "not directly from your home"; no sales outside MA (varies by town) [a139]. Labor: household members only, no outside employees [a139]. No brokers, wholesalers or warehouses (wholesale requires the DPH license) [a139][a140].
- Full-time on-site Food Manager Certification active during all production hours, plus food allergen awareness training [a139].
- Application: plan-review appointment with ISD Health (617-635-5326; ISDHealth@boston.gov); application, Kitchen Permit Plan Review worksheet, standardized recipes, planned operating hours, product label sample, pet disclosure, letter or email from the property owner (local forms vary: Athol, Arlington, Cohasset) [a139][a142–a144]. Renters: written landlord permission (ordinance text) [a145][a146].
- Fee **$100/year** (example: Athol $75) [a139][a142]. Annual ISD Health inspection plus possible random compliance inspections [a139]. ISD permit number on all advertising and listings; no exterior signage unless zoning or City Code ch. 16-33 allows [a139][a147]. Penalties up to $300/day [a139]. Must comply with 105 CMR 590 [a139].
- Labeling: sample required at application; a third-party guide says most boards require a statement that the food was made in a residential kitchen not subject to routine DPH inspection (confirm locally) [a139][a148]. Sales cap: none in the extracted permit text; no statewide cap found [a139].
- Pending: H.114, H.140, S.69 (194th General Court, 2025–26) would create a statewide cottage-food law; a third-party site says not passed as of 2026 (verify) [a149–a152].
- Other permits: a new Common Victualler license at an address triggers a public hearing (pn) [b63]; CV license and ISD food-service permit take "several months" [b183]. **Inf-A:** Cambridge, Somerville and Brookline permit through their own boards of health, so rules and fees may differ (not verified).

### 5l. Cross-model comparison (B §8; Est-B unless noted)
- **Est-B.** Scratch café: revenue ceiling $0.3M–$1.6M+ a unit on ~$0.4M–$1.0M+ capex (franchise Item 7 $0.67M–$1.86M). Finish-only café: similar revenue, less differentiation; convection oven + freezer (prices not found); competes on convenience against the scratch leaders. Wholesale: ~half the retail unit price; account value $7.8K–$46.8K/yr; production ovens, mixers, walk-ins, vehicles, industrial space (cost not found). Micro-bakery: ~$21K–$78K gross capped by household labor; costs = ingredients, packaging, platform fees (not found), owner labor; entry = $100 permit + Food Manager certification + home equipment. Stalls: cheap seasonal or percentage rent, traffic-limited; tent and display outdoors, BPM stall fit-out (cost not found); production must be licensed. Commissary: kitchen capex becomes ~$3K–$6K/month ($36K–$73K/yr at 20–40 hrs/week); close to zero build-out (smallwares, transport); limits: shift availability, storage, unknown CropCircle rates, tenant permitting unverified. Delivery: reach at 15–30% commission; capex = packaging, tablet integration, a delivery-oriented store (Insomnia Back Bay); fragile product. Entry cost, cheapest first: micro-bakery, stall, commissary, finish-only café, scratch café (≈ a wholesale plant); revenue ceilings rank about the same. Ladder: pop-up or stall → commissary + pre-orders → storefront + wholesale + pickup (Boston climbers: Brick Street, Nine Winters, Crisscross, Hearth & Hug, Tatte). Channel margin: retail full price; wholesale ~50–60%; delivery nets 70–85% of the app price (~84–102% of in-store after a 20% markup); stalls and BPM make occupancy seasonal or percentage-based; blended model = storefront + holiday pre-orders + selective wholesale + Basic/Lite-tier delivery. Risk: high rents, "several months" of permitting and ~50% five-year survival make the retail café highest-risk, highest-reward; Boston failure modes: lease terms (WPF), creeping costs (Brookline cake bakery), channel economics (commissions; frozen croissants capping wholesale).
- **Inf-A.** The Boston core is cookie-chain territory (3 Crumbl, ~5 Insomnia, 2 Levain) while ABP, Panera Cares and Le Pain Quotidien left or never came, so a full-line bakery-café faces less direct chain competition than a cookie concept. ABP's ~123 → 32–34 units after the 2021 sale plus Panera's high-rent urban closures suggest weak post-pandemic economics for downtown commuter-dependent bakery-cafés (consistent with "declining urban foot traffic" in ABP coverage). Crumbl's MA net growth is small (Cambridge was the 18th unit; the locator now shows 21, Hyannis added); with 55+ US franchises for sale, MA units are a plausible resale or closure source.
