- *Inf.* Savory share is highest at the Latin, Caribbean and Vietnamese independents (salgados, pastelitos, pâté, bánh mì as meal-replacement items); Korean chains and Chinatown lean sweet (buns, tarts, cream cakes). Dayparts: breakfast/lunch at independents vs snack/coffee/afternoon at café chains.
- *Inf.* The beverage program separates chains from old-line independents: matcha, coffee, bubble tea at TLJ, Great Taste, Golden Crust, Japonaise; little or none at traditional Haitian, Dominican, Brazilian counters (absence in extracts, not confirmed menus).

### 5e. Researcher calculations (EST, from the cited figures)
- PB fee load per average franchised café: 5% × $3,050,434 ≈ $152.5K/yr royalty, plus 2–3% ad fund ≈ $61.0K–$91.5K, total ≈ $213.5K–$244.0K/yr (~7–8% of sales) before rent, labor, COGS and debt service.
- TLJ fee load: 5% × $1,984,809 ≈ $99.2K/yr, plus ad fund $0–$59.5K (0–3%).
- Sales-to-investment: PB $3.05M vs $0.73M–$1.86M ≈ 1.6×–4.2×; TLJ ~$1.98M vs $0.73M–$1.70M ≈ 1.2×–2.7×; PB average unit ≈ 1.5× TLJ ($3.05M ÷ $1.98M). Both unaudited restatements; TLJ's H Mart counters are a smaller-box format.
- Illustrative: $3.05M/yr ≈ $8,350/day over 365 days; at an *assumed*, unsourced $12–$15 average ticket ≈ 560–700 transactions/day, far above any plausible independent volume at $1–$2 items (no independent revenue data to test).
- *Inf.* PB's franchised range is very wide ($1.42M–$7.04M); best units likely in dense Asian-American or urban trade areas. MA's lag (three cafés vs 77 national openings in 2025 alone) looks like franchisee capacity, not a demand ceiling (Quincy franchisee took >3 years from signing to opening). The Sahni deal is the first multi-unit push; its first sites are outer-suburban (Bellingham, Mansfield), not Boston proper.
- *Inf.* TLJ's MA model is opportunistic (single-unit local franchisees such as Harry Eng, plus H Mart in-store counters), likely explaining more MA units at lower volumes.

### 5f. Independent operator model
- Price points $1–$2 for core items (§3); 5 am openings (La Sultana, Padaria Brasil; §2).

- *Inf.* The independent model is high-frequency, low-ticket, production-capped: sell-outs, lines and 5 am openings show demand above capacity at the anchors (Le Foyer, Au Beurre Chaud, Ho Yuen); growth means more ovens and labor or a second unit (Ba Lẹ, Santia's), not more marketing.
- *Inf.* Format bundling answers low item prices: it raises ticket size and spreads fixed rent and labor over more dayparts.
- *Inf.* The 1976–1992 founding dates and community-anchor role suggest owner-occupied or long-lease economics and family labor, fitting closures around retirement (Hing Shing) and lease events (2015 corner bakery). No direct ownership or lease data.
