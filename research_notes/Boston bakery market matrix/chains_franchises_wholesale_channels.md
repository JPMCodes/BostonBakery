## Q1. What price floor do grocery and chain products set for each common item (croissant, loaf, baguette, cookie, muffin, cake, pie, donut, bagel, latte)?

### Takeaway
Supermarket multipacks set the croissant floor at about **$1.45–$2.25 per large croissant**. The sources are Stop & Shop, Star Market, Wegmans and Whole Foods; mini croissants run $0.43–$0.72 each and Trader Joe's frozen bake-at-home croissants about $1.25–$1.50 each. A supermarket baguette costs **$2.99**, while the local artisan benchmark, Iggy's, sells for **$4.25–$4.50** at Formaggio Kitchen. Among chains, a single cookie sets the main anchor: **about $4.50–$5.50 at Crumbl**, with 4-packs at about $16–$20, **about $4.50–$5 at Levain**, and **about $2–$3.70 at Insomnia**. A Nothing Bundt Cakes 8" cake costs about **$18.50–$35**. No verified Boston prices turned up for latte, pie, cakes at grocery bakeries, Dunkin', Starbucks or Panera.

### Inferences
- **Croissant floor ladder.** Grocery minis cost about $0.43–$0.72 each and large croissants in multipacks about $1.45–$2.25. Wegmans charges $3.19 for a single croissant via Instacart. A Boston independent pricing croissants at $4.50–$6 is therefore asking about 2–4x the supermarket per-unit price. The Whole Foods flyer dates (10/28–11/03) fall on a Wednesday-to-Tuesday sale week in 2026 but not in 2025, so the flyer prices are probably current.
- **Baguette.** Iggy's at Formaggio ($4.25–$4.50) costs about 42–50% more than Star Market's $2.99 store baguette. Iggy's was $4.00 in 2021, so the local artisan baguette has risen only about 6–12.5% in five years. That comparison is rough: the baguette types and retailers differ.
- **Cookie price band.** Insomnia sets a low anchor of about $2–$3.70. Crumbl ($4.49–$5.49 single; $4.00–$5.00 per cookie in a 4-pack) and Levain (about $4.50–$5) set the premium anchor near $5. A craft cookie in Boston above about $5 would be priced over both chains.
- **Cake.** Nothing Bundt Cakes' 8" cake at about $18.50–$35 is the closest franchise anchor for celebration cakes. No grocery cake prices were captured.
- **Same-item delivery markup.** Wegmans' 4-pack costs 14.9% more on Instacart than on wegmans.com ($9.19 vs $8.00), and Stop & Shop's 4-pack costs 17.3% more on its Express delivery service ($6.79 vs $5.79). Both fit the 10–30% markup range the brief anticipated.

### Gaps
- **Latte:** no Boston Starbucks or Dunkin' latte price was found.
- **Pie:** no grocery or chain pie price was found.
- **Grocery cakes:** Star Market custom-cake pages exist, but no prices were captured.
- **Market Basket:** no croissant or bread price was found; it is the likely absolute floor in Greater Boston.
- **Other prices not found:** Trader Joe's bread and baguette; Clear Flour shelf prices; Iggy's shelf prices at Whole Foods (store-specific); Eataly first-party prices; Panera menu prices; Boston-specific Starbucks and Dunkin' bakery-case prices.
- **Dunkin' and Insomnia** prices come only from low-reliability aggregators. Boston store or app checks are needed.
- **Whole Foods flyer store IDs** (10545, 10220) could not be matched to Boston stores.

## Q2. How do franchises perform in Boston (openings vs closures), and what do FDDs show for AUV and startup cost?

### Takeaway
In the Boston core, chain bakery presence today is mostly **cookies**: Crumbl in Brighton, Dorchester and Cambridge (21 Massachusetts stores in total), Insomnia with about 5 core stores, and Levain with 2. Bundt cakes are only in the suburbs (Newton, Burlington, Peabody). **Bakery-cafés have retreated**: Au Bon Pain was founded in Boston but left the city in January 2024, Le Pain Quotidien never opened here, Panera closed several urban cafés over the years, and its Franklin dough plant closes in March 2026. FDD summaries show Crumbl's **average unit volume (AUV) fell to about $1.09–$1.14M in 2025**, down from a $1.84M peak in 2022, with at least 57 U.S. closures in 2026. Nothing Bundt Cakes averages about **$1.5–$1.6M** on a $667K–$907K investment. Panera averages about **$2.6M** on $1.27M–$4.65M.

### Inferences
- **Commissary cost, illustrative math rather than a quote.** At $35/hour, 20 production hours a week costs about $700/week, or about **$3,030/month**, before storage and membership fees. Private kitchens at $80–$175/hour cost 2.3–5x the incubator rate, so incubators such as CommonWealth Kitchen and CropCircle are the realistic low-capex route to licensed production.
- **Residential permit as the cheapest test channel.** At $100/yr it costs the least, but Boston bars employees, brokers and wholesalers (see Q4). Volume is therefore capped by household labor, and the model suits pre-order "drops" sold online or at markets. Cafés and grocers require a move to a licensed kitchen.
- **Wholesale.** Iggy's appears at Whole Foods, Formaggio and on Instacart, and Nashoba has more than 275 accounts, so local artisan bread competes for shelf space with established suppliers. Shelf prices of $4.25–$4.50 per baguette leave a wholesale price somewhere below that, but **the wholesale price and margin cannot be backed out from public data**.
- **Subscriptions.** The 2021 Cambridge baguette subscription sold at the grocery shelf price ($4). Bread subscriptions in Boston therefore compete head-on with grocery availability of the same loaf unless the product is exclusive.
- **Delivery.** Same-item grocery platform markups were +15–17%. Crumbl app prices about 45–86% above pickup suggest that operators who set their own app menus mark up heavily to absorb commissions. This is an inference: the commission rates themselves were not verified.

## Q4. Which regulatory constraints shape low-capex entry (cottage food, commissary requirements)?

### Takeaway
**Massachusetts has no statewide cottage-food law.** Each town's board of health permits home kitchens that sell retail, and any home-kitchen wholesale needs a state DPH wholesale license. Boston's ISD **Retail Residential Kitchen Permit costs $100/yr** and is restrictive. It allows shelf-stable foods only and household members only, with no employees, brokers or wholesalers. It prohibits **direct sales from the home** and sales outside Massachusetts. It also requires a full-time Food Manager certification, allergen training, a plan review, landlord permission and annual inspections, plus the permit number on all advertising. **No sales cap appeared** in the extracted permit text.

### Inferences
- **The Boston home-bakery model is pre-order plus off-site handoff.** The ban on sales "directly from your home" and on employees points Boston home bakers toward online or mail orders and market stalls. Whether porch pickup of online orders counts as a "direct" home sale is unclear (see Gaps). This shapes the economics of Instagram "drops" in Boston.
- **The path to scale runs through a licensed kitchen.** Selling to cafés or grocers, or hiring a single employee, means leaving the residential permit. That means either a DPH wholesale license, if the town allows it, or a commissary or own licensed kitchen. CommonWealth Kitchen (about $35/hour, unverified) is the reference cost.
- **Neighboring towns may have different requirements.** Cambridge, Somerville and Brookline each permit through their own boards of health, so their requirements and fees may differ from Boston's. They were not verified.
- **Higher fixed burdens than many states.** Food Manager certification, plan review, annual inspection, landlord sign-off, and the permit number on every listing raise the fixed time cost of a Boston micro-bakery above what many state cottage laws require. The absence of a sales cap, if confirmed, partly offsets this.

### Gaps
- The boston.gov and mass.gov pages could not be fetched directly. All rules above come from search extracts, so confirm the exact text, especially for labeling and sales caps, against the live pages.
- Not covered: Cambridge, Somerville and Brookline residential-kitchen rules and fees.
- Unclear whether Boston ever permits **residential wholesale** under the DPH license, and whether online-order porch pickup is allowed.
- Not found: commissary-tenant licensing requirements, such as whether each tenant needs its own food-establishment permit in the kitchen's host city, and Boston temporary-event or pop-up food permits and fees.
- The status of the 2025–26 cottage-food bills comes from a low-reliability third-party site. Check it on malegislature.gov.
