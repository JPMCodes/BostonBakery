### Inferences
- **Openings vs closures:** in the target towns for 2024–26, the bakery and dessert openings found here and in earlier notes (Levain, Caracas Gables, Le Macaron, 16 Handles, MELI, Go Greek) roughly equal or exceed the bakery and dessert closures found (Misha's x2, Sundays, L'Artisane, Lien Bruno, Einstein, Green G, Pretty Sweet). That is about 6–7 against 7–8. This is a **search-coverage count, not a census**. Press covers closures of long-running places far more than quiet failures of new ones.
- **Long-running independents close when the lease ends,** not when sales slump. Lokal's 50% rent proposal and "landlords double the rent" when old leases expire fit the Einstein (28 years) and Misha's (21 years) pattern. **Newer shops close on sales:** Acme (9 months, no weekday trade) and Pretty Sweet are examples.
- **Who replaces them:** in the Gables and the Grove, the replacements are mostly funded brands (Levain, 16 Handles, Le Macaron, Carbone Vino). That fits landlords preferring credit tenants (section 5).

## 2. What named bakery owners say about money and work

### Takeaway
- **Miami owners rarely give figures in indexed interviews.** The usable facts are about scale, channel and expansion choices:
  - **Zak the Baker** has about 85 staff, a 7,000 sq ft site and a 125-seat café, and sells wholesale, including to Whole Foods.
  - **Madruga's** owner said she had no plans to wholesale or expand (2019, **OLD**).

### Inferences
- **Miami's lasting artisan bakeries took one of two paths:**
  1. Scale through wholesale with a big production staff (Zak, Sullivan Street).

  - Owner-chefs' restaurant spin-offs (Bachour's Tablé and Doral) failed where the core bakery-café survived.
- **Wholesale is described as steadier, not as more profitable.** The earlier notes put wholesale gross margin about 15–20 points below retail.

## 3. Survey, lender and trade data on owner pay, hours and who bakes

### Takeaway
- **No survey separates owner-bakers from bakeries run by a manager with hired bakers.** None was found from Retail Bakers of America, ABA or Bake.
- **What exists:**
  - Modelled owner-pay estimates: $74,497 (Salary.com) to $114,182 (another aggregator).
  - BizBuySell's median owner earnings of about $104.5k (earlier notes).
  - Toast's guidance: owner pay is usually under 50% of profits, more if the owner is lead baker.
  - General owner-hours data: over 70% of small-business owners work more than 40 hours.
  - A 2017 Bake study (**OLD**): 61% of bakery owners rate labor costs challenging.

### Cited Findings
- **Bake 2017 Retail Bakery Operations Study (OLD):** "61 percent of bakery owners said labor costs were challenging or very challenging" — [Bake](https://www.bakemag.com/articles/5621-in-search-of-skilled-labor).
- **Salary.com:** average bakery-owner salary $74,497 (modelled). Another site gives $114,182; the two conflict — [Salary.com](https://www.salary.com/research/salary/hiring/bakery-owner-salary); [Dojo Business](https://dojobusiness.com/blogs/news/bakery-owner-income).
- **Toast:**
  - "Usually, a business owner's salary is less than 50 percent of the profits."
  - "If you are the lead baker, accountant, and administrator, you can afford to pay yourself a higher percentage".
  - Bakery net margins of "between 3 percent and 5 percent, with some as high as 15 percent"; labor 24–40% of sales.
  - Source: [Toast](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/on-the-line/how-much-do-bakeries-make).
- **Better Baker Club (home and hobby bakers, not retail):** 51.9% "do not pay themselves" — [Better Baker Club](https://betterbakerclub.com/?p=4035).
- **Fundera (all small businesses):** more than 70% of owners work more than 40 hours a week — [Fundera](https://www.fundera.com/blog/study-finds-business-owners-earn-less).
- **SBA Office of Advocacy Issue Brief No. 22 (Aug 2025):** self-employed people are less likely to work a standard 40-hour week (American Time Use Survey) — [SBA Advocacy](https://advocacy.sba.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Issue-Brief-No.-22-How-Self-Employed-Use-Time-Differently.pdf).
- **Industry data on retail bakeries (NAICS)** exists at DataUSA; the figures were not captured — [DataUSA](https://datausa.io/profile/naics/retail-bakeries?redirect=true).
- **A model built on hired management** gives a "$60k salary" for a bakery-café owner. This is a template, not data — [FinancialModelsLab](https://financialmodelslab.com/blogs/how-much-makes/bakery-cafe).

### Inferences
- **The gap between owner-baker and hired-baker models is roughly one baker's wage.** BizBuySell's about-$105k median owner earnings (SDE) includes the owner's own labor. If the owner stops baking and hires a lead baker, SDE falls by that wage (often about $45–65k loaded in Miami; not sourced here). That leaves about $40–60k of true profit on a median bakery.
- **The $24.5k forum case** shows the low end: an owner-baker who cut staff from about 20 to 2.
- Whether hired-baker bakeries earn more in absolute dollars depends on whether the freed owner adds volume. No data was found on this.

### Gaps
- Share of owners who bake; hours worked by bakery owners specifically; any lender (RMA, SBA) bakery owner-comp data; any RBA or ABA survey since 2017. None surfaced.

## 4. Bakery-cafe economics by size ($0.7M, $1.5M, $3M) and break-even at $60–$95/sq ft

### Takeaway
- **No source gives P&Ls for bakery-cafés at these three sizes.**
- **Best anchors:**
  - National Restaurant Association 2024 medians: **occupancy 5.2% of sales (limited-service)** and 5.7% (full-service); 6.0% in urban centers.
  - The NRA also reports that **higher-volume restaurants had lower food-cost ratios** in 2024.
  - Brokers' rule: rent at no more than 10% of sales; average restaurant net margin about 3%.
- **Break-even arithmetic:** a 2,500–3,500 sq ft café at $60–$95/sq ft base rent pays **$150k–$333k a year in base rent alone**. At a 10% rent share that needs **$1.5M–$3.3M in sales**. At the NRA's 5–6% median it needs about $2.5–6.4M.
- **So:** a $700k shop cannot carry that space. A $1.5M shop can carry only the low end. A $3M shop is what the rent level assumes, which is about the Paris Baguette median.

### Cited Findings
- **NRA 2025 Operations Data Abstract (2024 data):**
  - Occupancy "a median of 5.7% of sales… among fullservice respondents" and "5.2%… among limited-service".
  - Urban full-service 6.0%; suburban limited-service 5.0%.
  - About 5.8% average in the 2010, 2013 and 2016 reports.
  - Source: [NRA](https://restaurant.org/research-and-media/research/restaurant-economic-insights/analysis-commentary/restaurant-occupancy-costs-were-more-than-5-of-sales-in-2024/).
- **NRA, food cost and volume:** "Higher volume restaurants reported lower food cost ratios in 2024" — [NRA](https://restaurant.org/research-and-media/research/restaurant-economic-insights/analysis-commentary/higher-volume-restaurants-reported-lower-food-cost-ratios-in-2024/) (title; figures not captured).
- **NRA, labor:** "Elevated labor costs had a significant impact on restaurant profitability in 2024"; labor "well above historical averages" — [NRA](https://restaurant.org/research-and-media/research/restaurant-economic-insights/analysis-commentary/elevated-labor-costs-had-a-significant-impact-on-restaurant-profitability-in-2024/) (titles).

### Inferences (calc; illustrative, not sourced P&Ls)

**Base rent alone for the target space**

| Size | Rent $60/sq ft | Rent $95/sq ft | Sales at 10% rent share | Sales at 6% rent share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2,500 sq ft | $150,000 | $237,500 | $1.50M–$2.38M | $2.50M–$3.96M |
| 3,500 sq ft | $210,000 | $332,500 | $2.10M–$3.33M | $3.50M–$5.54M |

NNN charges (taxes, insurance, common-area costs) would add to these. No Miami NNN figure was sourced here.

**Rent as a share of sales at each sales level (2,500 sq ft at $60–95)**

| Sales | Rent share | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **$700k** | 21–34% | Not viable. Food about 30% plus labor about 35% plus rent leaves nothing. A $700k shop needs about 1,000–1,200 sq ft or a lease near $30–40/sq ft. |
| **$1.5M** | 10–16% | At $60/sq ft, food 30% + labor 33% + rent 10% + other about 15% leaves about 12% (about $180k) before debt and owner pay. At $95/sq ft it falls to about 6% (about $90k). |
| **$3M** | 5–8% | At NRA-type ratios (rent 5–8%, food lower at volume), a 12–18% four-wall margin (about $360–540k) is plausible. This is the volume Paris Baguette (about $2.97M median) and Maman (about $1.8M calc) are built for. |

- **Break-even** for a 3,000 sq ft café at $75/sq ft ($225k rent) with about 65% variable costs (food plus hourly labor) and about $200–250k of other fixed costs (management salaries, utilities, insurance, NNN): about (225 + 225) ÷ 0.35, roughly **$1.3M a year, or about $3,500–3,600 a day**. Illustrative only.

## 6. Older leases vs today's asking rents

### Takeaway
- **No published rent for a long-standing bakery in the target area was found.**
- **Proxies:**
  - Coconut Grove restaurant rents average **$85–$100+/sq ft now**, against **$45.50/sq ft a decade ago** (more than double).
  - A city-owned Coral Gables restaurant lease ran **$27/sq ft in 2014** (+3% a year), reaching **about $35.24 in 2022–24**.
  - The city's own Miracle Mile marketing sheet shows **$45 NNN**.
  - Graziano's pays the city $41.31 (earlier notes).
  - Miami prime food-and-beverage space: close to $100.
- **A long-standing tenant on an old lease is likely paying half or less of the current asking rent.** When that lease ends, the reset is what closes it.

### Cited Findings
- **Coconut Grove:** restaurants pay "an average base rent of $85 to $100+ per square foot annually", "more than double rent prices of a decade ago, when the average was $45.50 per square foot" — search summary attributing the first figure to [Miami New Times](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/?p=40563529) and the decade comparison to [The Real Deal, Jul 16, 2026](https://therealdeal.com/miami/2026/07/16/coconut-groves-restaurant-scene-faces-1-million-rents/) [attribution between the two unclear].
- **Coral Gables city-owned lease (Fritz & Franz Bierhaus, a restaurant, not a bakery):**
  - 2014 renewal at base rent $27.00/sq ft, escalating 3% a year, with two five-year options.
  - About $35.24/sq ft in 2022–2024.
  - In 2024 the city sought to end the lease; the commission ordered a new extension "at market rates".
  - Sources: [WLRN, Feb 12, 2024](https://www.wlrn.org/government-politics/2024-02-12/fritz-and-franz-closure-coral-gables); [Miami New Times](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/restaurants/fritz-and-franz-bierhaus-fights-coral-gables-eviction-efforts-18931607).
- **City restaurant space, 278 Miracle Mile:** marketed at **$45.00/sq ft NNN** — [Coral Gables marketing sheet](https://www.coralgables.com/sites/default/files/mediaexport/Economic%20Development/278%20Miracle%20Mile.pdf) (date not shown).
- **Coral Gables city properties rent report** (dated Dec 5, 2025): lists each city tenant's term, options and rent per sq ft; the figures were not captured — [City Properties Rent Report](https://www.coralgables.com/department/city-managers-office/city-properties-rent-report). **This is the best next document for actual old-lease rents.**
- **Miami-wide:** asking rents for retail and restaurants are high $60s to low $80s per sq ft (CoStar via The Real Deal); close to $100 for prime food-and-beverage (Dec 2025) — [The Real Deal](https://therealdeal.com/miami/2025/12/10/cost-of-dining-out-in-miami-rises-alongside-retail-rents).
- **Lokal (Coconut Grove):** a proposed 50% increase at renewal preceded closure — [Miami New Times](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/food-drink/beloved-coconut-grove-burger-spot-closes-after-15-years-40563529/).

### Inferences
- **A bakery that signed in the Gables or the Grove 10–15 years ago** is probably paying about $27–45/sq ft. Today's asking rent is $60–100. On 2,500 sq ft, the reset adds about **$40k–$140k a year** of rent (calc). For a $700k–$1M bakery, that is 4–20 points of sales: the gap between surviving and closing.
- **The Coral Gables rent report** can turn this inference into fact for any city-owned site.
