## 1. In-depth profiles: Honey Veil, Madruga, Cécile Bakery + Café, Maman

### Takeaway
- **The four bakeries run on four different models:**
  - **Honey Veil:** a family business that started without premises (farmers' markets, then a trailer at UM) and moved into a South Miami café in Dec 2025. It is now listed at up to three points.
  - **Madruga (2017):** a single, owner-run bread bakery. The owner refuses wholesale and expansion, and the shop is open five days a week.
  - **Cécile:** a single pastry-chef-owned bakery-café on Sunset Drive with strong review volume.
  - **Maman:** a company-owned (not franchised) chain backed by private equity. It has opened 11 South Florida cafés since Feb 2024, including Coral Gables and South Miami, supplied from a regional pastry kitchen.
- **Sunset Drive in South Miami is now a cluster:** Honey Veil (5748), Cécile (5744), Maman (5801) and a planned PopUp Bagels (5828) all sit within about one block.

### Inferences
- **Honey Veil and Madruga show the two owner-baker extremes.** Honey Veil grows fast through matcha drinks and multiple points of sale. Madruga stays deliberately small (no wholesale, no second shop) and puts its limited capacity into bread.
- **Maman's model differs in kind.**
  - It is backed by private equity and company-owned.
  - A central kitchen supplies about 11 cafés in roughly 22 months, with 10-year leases.
  - It takes prime high-street sites (Miracle Mile, Sunset Dr) that independent operators compete for.
- **South Miami's Sunset Drive block is becoming a test of saturation.** Three bakery-cafés sit there (Cécile, Honey Veil, Maman), with PopUp Bagels listed as coming. Cécile's review counts (about 1,170 on Google) suggest it was established before the others arrived. How it trades now is unknown.

- Beer-and-wine licences for all four. DBPR licence lookup was not reachable.

## 3. Brief profiles of the other bakeries and the 2026 chains

### Takeaway
- **Most of the independent bakeries that opened in the area since 2017 are run by working pastry chefs or bakers:**
  - Madruga, Cécile, Façade, Casa Bake, Ophelia, Bachour and Dolce Isla.
  - Honey Veil is family-baked.
- **Several started without premises:** Honey Veil (market and trailer), Ophelia (an RV supplied by a commissary), Dolce Isla (home), and Casa Bake (a warehouse unit, not a high-street shop). Older precedents are Chocolate Fashion (home, about 2004) and Fireman Derek's (yard sales).
- **Investor- or franchisor-backed operators:**
  - Maman (private equity).
  - H&H Pinecrest (franchise).
  - Paris Baguette (franchise).
  - Call Your Mother and Levain (multi-unit chains).
  - Bachour's earlier ventures had investor partners, and those partnerships broke up.
- **Health is mixed:**
  - Growing: Ophelia (Fort Lauderdale), Caracas (Miracle Mile and Sunset Harbour), Façade (second site), Honey Veil.
  - Contracting: Bachour closed Tablé (2025) and Doral, but the Gables flagship is still open.
  - Unresolved: Sundays Croissanterie (closed, per the earlier note; unconfirmed here).

- **Owner bakes:** about 9 of 16 (Madruga, Bachour, Cécile, Casa Bake, Façade, Ophelia, Honey Veil, Dolce Isla, plus Madruga's partial).
- **Seats plus a kitchen menu (confirmed):** about 4–5 (Bachour, Façade, Maman, Paris Baguette, possibly Cécile).
- **Investor- or franchisor-backed:** 5–6 (Maman, H&H, Paris Baguette, Levain, Call Your Mother; Present via parent).
- **Started without premises:** 4 (Honey Veil, Ophelia, Dolce Isla, Casa Bake, the last partially), plus older precedents (Chocolate Fashion, Fireman Derek's).
- **The pattern:**
  - Chef-led independents that start small (RV, market, home, warehouse) are the ones generating lines and press in 2025–26.
  - Capital-backed chains are taking the high-street corners (Miracle Mile, Sunset Dr, US-1 in Pinecrest).
  - Owner-bakers who scale either step back from baking (Madruga) or split with investors (Bachour's Brickell venture).
