# Sales channels beyond the counter for a small south Miami-Dade bakery (as of Nov 19, 2026)

How to read these notes:
- **[R]** means I read the page itself with curl. Only www.coralgables.com, www.pinecrest-fl.gov, www.miamidade.gov and www.fdacs.gov were reachable.
- **[S]** means search-result text only. The proxy blocks every other host, so the page was not opened. The search tool often merges several results into one summary; where a figure could not be tied to a single URL I say "attribution unclear".
- Every fact carries a date or "undated". **OLD** marks anything from before 2025.
- "My arithmetic" marks a calculation from cited figures, not a published number.
- I did not repeat what the two earlier notes already cover: the Pinecrest and Coral Gables farmers markets, Florida's cottage food law, commissary rates, catering prices at Pinecrest Bakery, Panera and Einstein, and the pop-up origins of Miami's breakout bakeries. ## 1. Office and medical-office orders (ezCater, Fooda, direct; Coral Gables offices; hospitals; drug-company representatives)

### Takeaway
ezCater is the established route into Miami office catering. It takes a 15% marketplace commission on subtotal plus delivery fee, plus a 2.99% payment fee, and only 7% on orders that come through the caterer's own ezCater-powered ordering page; Uber agreed to buy ezCater in 2026. Fooda runs rotating lunch pop-ups in office buildings but publishes no partner fees and requires a commercial kitchen. Coral Gables says it has "more than 10,000 businesses". The three nearby Baptist-system and HCA hospitals employ roughly 1,100 (Doctors), 2,750–3,200 (South Miami) and 5,500–6,700 (Baptist Kendall) people by tax-filing and directory counts. No Miami order size or price per person, and no current evidence on how drug representatives buy breakfast for medical offices, was found.

**Drug-company representatives and medical offices**
- A trade article said pharma reps had "catering budgets of between $800 and $2,500 per month" and that food was the way to gain access to doctors; it advised restaurants to give reps one point of contact — [Pizza Today, "Prescription for Profits", Sept 2009](https://pizzatoday.com/?p=126986) [S; **OLD**, 2009; national, not Miami].
- No current Miami source on how reps order breakfast or snacks for medical offices was found.

### Inferences
- ezCater's effective take on a $150 order delivered by the bakery is roughly 15% of food plus delivery plus about 3% payment fee, i.e. about 18% (my arithmetic). Steering repeat office customers to the bakery's own ezOrdering link cuts the commission to 7%.
- The Uber–ezCater deal could make office catering visible to Uber Eats business users; fee terms after the deal are unknown.
- Hospital staffs of roughly 1,100 to 6,700 per campus are a large captive audience for department celebration boxes, nurse-appreciation orders and shift-change treats. Medical-office drug-rep breakfasts were historically a real catering channel (2009 budgets of $800–$2,500 a month per rep), but post-2010 industry meal rules (PhRMA Code, Sunshine Act reporting) may have tightened it. That last point is background knowledge, not sourced here.
- Fooda requires a commercial kitchen, so it is closed to a cottage-food operation.

## 2. The afternoon coffee break: 3:05 cafecito, the shared colada, merienda

### Takeaway
The 3:05 p.m. cafecito break is a real, branded Miami ritual named after the 305 area code; it began as a 2012 social-media campaign and was proclaimed official cafecito time by Miami's then mayor. The office custom is for one coworker to fetch a colada (a Styrofoam cup of several shots with thimble cups) and share it. No 2025–26 colada prices, office delivery services, or evidence of pastry buying at "merienda" were found.

### Cited Findings
- "3:05 becomes official cafecito time in Miami" — [NBC Miami](https://nbcmiami.com/local/305-becomes-official-cafecito-time-in-miami/2019907) [S; ~2012–13; **OLD**].
- The idea dates to "one sunny day in April 2012… It was 3:05 pm… The time was also the city's area code"; Mayor Tomás Regalado declared 3:05 p.m. the official cafecito break after a PR professional turned a hashtag into a movement — [Culture Trip: Why Miamians drink coffee at precisely 3:05 PM](https://theculturetrip.com/north-america/usa/florida/articles/why-miamians-drink-coffee-at-precisely-305-pm) [S; undated, **OLD** history; attribution between this and NBC unclear].
- "Every day at 3:05, many offices take a cafecito break… it is very conventional that someone from the office brings back a styrofoam cup filled with cafecito and shares it with the whole office" — same result set [S; attribution unclear between Culture Trip and Miami Culinary Tours].
- A colada is "a larger Styrofoam cup holding several cafecitos, which comes with small cups so you can share" — [Lonely Planet: Cuban coffee in Miami](https://www.lonelyplanet.com/articles/cuban-coffee-in-miami) [S, undated]; "Sharing a colada is an act of friendship and solidarity" — [Culture Trip: Hungerlust](https://theculturetrip.com/north-america/usa/florida/articles/hungerlust-cafe-cubano-is-a-social-ritual-for-miamis-cubans) [S, undated].
- A CBS Miami story on a "cafecito campaign sparking Miami pride" exists — [CBS Miami](https://www.cbsnews.com/miami/news/cafecito-campaign-sparking-miami-pride-among-coffee-lovers) [S; title only; date not captured].
- Vicky Bakery features in a TV segment on "the art of cafecito" — [WSFL Inside South Florida](https://www.wsfltv.com/inside-south-florida/brewing-tradition-vicky-bakery-and-the-art-of-cafecito) [S; title only].
- Merienda: one guide says "there is nothing like a pastelito for breakfast except for maybe a pastelito for an afternoon snack with some cafecito" — result set including [Time Out: Ricky Bakery](https://www.timeout.com/miami/restaurants/ricky-bakery) and [Perdiem reviews](https://www.tryperdiem.com/review/ricky-bakery---coral-way) [S; attribution unclear; undated]. No source used the word "merienda" or measured afternoon pastry buying.
- Colada prices and who delivers coladas to offices: not found (café con leche prices are in the earlier note).

### Inferences
- The 3:05 ritual is a ready-made hook for an afternoon office product (a tray of mini rolls "for the 3:05"), but the cultural pairing is with a cheap pastelito, so a mini-roll tray must be priced as a shareable treat.

### Gaps
- Colada prices in 2025–26; ventanitas or services that deliver coladas to offices.
- Any data on afternoon (merienda) pastry purchasing.

## 3. Gifts: real-estate agents, home sales, closing gifts, corporate holiday boxes

### Takeaway
Miami Realtors had about 56,000 members before merging with the Broward/Palm Beach/St. Lucie association on May 11, 2026; the combined body has about 93,000–94,000 members. Pinecrest recorded 192 closed single-family sales in 2025 (one broker's count); Coral Gables had 367 single-family sales in the 12 months to Oct 2026 at a $2.2 million median. Closing-gift guides put typical spend at $50–$300 (most often $100–$300). National corporate gift-box sellers ask $42–$189 a box with minimums of 6–12 boxes; no Miami bakery published holiday corporate-box prices or minimums.

### Cited Findings

Selling to agents (who close repeatedly) rather than to buyers is the lever.
- A local bakery box at $35–$60 sits at the low end of national corporate-gift pricing ($42–$189), with local delivery as the advantage over shipped boxes.

## 4. Fundraisers: Krispy Kreme, Butter Braid, local examples, schools and leagues

### Takeaway
Krispy Kreme's US programme sells dozens to groups at about half the suggested price (a pricing guide shows $4.50 cost against a $9.00 suggested price), with a 25-item minimum; its Digital Dozens (run with GroupRaise) let groups set $9–$30 a dozen. Butter Braid's dealer flyer lists a $15 price with the group keeping $3.50–$6.20 a pastry depending on volume; Butter Braid's own example is 400 pastries for $2,400. Local dine-out nights pay schools 15–33% of sales but raise small sums ($107–$206 per night in one Gables PTSA report). Gulliver (about 2,229 students), Ransom Everglades (~1,240), Belen (~1,360), Palmer Trinity (780+) and Columbus (862) are among the large private schools; Howard Palmetto calls itself Florida's largest recreational baseball league but publishes no total.

### Inferences

- Butter Braid's volume tiers imply successful groups sell 175–750+ items per campaign; the company's own example is 400.
- Dine-out nights yield little ($100–$200 per night); pre-sold product drives give schools more and give the bakery predictable volume.

## 6. Delivery apps and white-label courier delivery

### Takeaway
DoorDash's US plans charge 15% (Basic), 25% (Plus) and 30% (Premier) on delivered orders and 6% on pickup. Uber Eats raised its US Lite tier from 15% to 20% from March 11, 2026; Plus stays 25% (30% on Uber One member orders) and Premium 30%. Uber Direct (white-label courier) is a flat per-delivery fee "from $6.99" (one aggregator says $7.99). No Miami dessert ranking or average dessert order value was found; DoorDash publishes a national "Holiday Dessert 50" of small bakeries.

### Cited Findings
- DoorDash: "commission rates are 15% for the Basic plan, 25% for the Plus plan, and 30% for the Premier plan"; pickup "6% across all DoorDash plans"; orders through DoorDash's own Online Ordering are commission-free; plans available to restaurants with 75 or fewer US locations; switch plans any time — [DoorDash Merchant Pricing](https://merchants.doordash.com/en-us/pricing) [S, 2026 snapshot].
- Uber Eats: Lite "rising from 15% to 20%"; Plus 25% with Uber One member orders at 30%; Premium unchanged at 30% — [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/uber-eats-increases-marketplace-fees/814294/) [S, 2026]; effective date March 11, 2026 per [apis.io Uber Eats plans](https://apis.io/plans/uber-eats/uber-eats-plans-pricing/) [S; third-party].
- Uber Direct: "fee per delivery – no commissions", from $6.99 per delivery on Uber's US (Chinese-language) pricing page — [Uber Eats merchants pricing](https://merchants.ubereats.com/us/zh/pricing) [S, 2026]; $7.99 per delivery per [apis.io](https://apis.io/plans/uber-eats/uber-eats-plans-pricing/) [S]. Miami-specific price: not found.
- Uber online-ordering product: 2.5% + $0.29 per order — [Uber Eats merchants pricing](https://merchants.ubereats.com/us/en/pricing/) [S].
- DoorDash Drive per-drop price in Miami: not found.
- DoorDash "Rising Stars: The Holiday Dessert 50" used Jan–Nov 2025 data for bakeries with fewer than 10 stores, at least 500 reviews and top ratings — [DoorDash newsroom](https://about.doordash.com/en-us/news/doordash-holiday-dessert-rising-stars) [S, ~Dec 2025]. Whether Miami bakeries appear: not captured.
- Baked Bakery (Miami) lists a Cinnamon Roll among its most commonly ordered items on DoorDash — [DoorDash: Baked Bakery](https://www.doordash.com/store/baked-bakery-miami-23427753/) [S, undated].
- Average dessert order value in Miami: not found.

### Inferences
- On a $40 box delivered by app couriers, commission is $6 (Basic), $10 (Plus) or $12 (Premier) on DoorDash and $8–$12 on Uber Eats (my arithmetic). Uber Direct at ~$7 a drop is cheaper than a 20–30% commission once the order exceeds about $25–$35, but the bakery must generate the order itself.
- DoorDash Basic (15%) and pickup at 6% are the cheapest ways to be listed; the earlier note records Roll & Dough losing a 300-roll batch to an app driver, so app couriers are risky for bulk transfers.

### Gaps
