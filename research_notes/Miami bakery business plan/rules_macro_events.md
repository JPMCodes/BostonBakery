## 1. Sales tax on bakery products (Rule 12A-1.011, F.A.C.; s. 212.08, F.S.) and Miami-Dade's rate

### Takeaway
A walk-up window with **no eating facilities** is the most favourable sales-tax setup for a bakery in Florida:
- Unheated bakery products (rolls, boxes, cold or room-temperature items) are **exempt**.
- **Hot or toasted items, coffee and other drinks are taxable** at Miami-Dade's combined **7%** (6% state + 1% county surtax).
- **Catering is taxable** (food, set-up and delivery charges), per secondary sources.
- The treatment of **delivery charges** is disputed between sources.
- **Frozen unbaked dough is not addressed** by any source found.
- The rule's subsection numbering differs between sources (**(6)(d)** in a 2026 commentary, **(3)(d)** in older DOR rulings). Cite the rule by number until the current text is checked.

### Cited Findings
**The no-eating-facilities exemption**
- A DOR notice describes the exemption as covering "bakery items sold by bakeries, pastry shops, or similar establishments **without eating facilities**, excluding items sold as hot prepared food products." [SNIPPET] [flrules.org notice 9559949](https://flrules.org/gateway/notice_Files.asp?ID=9559949); [Cornell LII, Rule 12A-1.011](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/florida/Fla-Admin-Code-Ann-R-12A-1-011)
- **Subsection numbering:**
  - A law firm cites it as Rule 12A-1.011(6)(d). [SNIPPET] [FloridaSalesTax.com, "Florida sales tax issues facing bakeries", Apr 2026](https://www.floridasalestax.com/florida-tax-law-blog/2026/april/florida-sales-tax-issues-facing-bakeries/)
  - Department rulings cite 12A-1.011(3)(d). [SNIPPET] [TAA 20A-003 summary](https://www.floridasalestax.com/taas/taa-20a-003-sales-use-tax-restaurants-bakeries-c/); [Ezel, TAA 20A-003](https://ezel.ai/tax-rulings/fl/taa-20a-003-restaurants-bakeries-caterers)
- **What counts as "no eating facilities."** In one advisement the bakery supplied photos showing no eating facilities, inside or outside, and that "the counter space was only for ordering and carry out." Another source describes eating facilities as benches, chairs, stools, tables or counters that facilitate eating on the premises. [SNIPPET] [TAA 20A-003](https://www.floridasalestax.com/taas/taa-20a-003-sales-use-tax-restaurants-bakeries-c/); [TAA 23A-006](https://www.floridasalestax.com/taas/taa-23a-006-bakery-goods/)
- **Hot items.** "Cookies sold as hot prepared food products are not exempt" (TAA 23A-006, 2023). [SNIPPET] [FloridaSalesTax.com, TAA 23A-006](https://www.floridasalestax.com/taas/taa-23a-006-bakery-goods/)
- **Toasted items.** DOR guidance: if "a bakery establishment toasts a bagel for a customer… the sale of the bagel is subject to tax, **whether the bakery establishment has eating facilities or does not have eating facilities**." [SNIPPET] [DOR TIP 10A01-22](https://floridarevenue.com:443/taxes/tips/documents/TIP_10A01-22.pdf)
- **Containers.** The rule has separate guidance for exempt food sold in taxable containers, such as tins or decorative boxes. [SNIPPET] [Cornell LII, Rule 12A-1.011](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/florida/Fla-Admin-Code-Ann-R-12A-1-011)
- **Earlier TAAs** (already in costs_rules.md s. 5): TAA 12A-028 (sealed packaging shows off-premises intent) and TAA 23A-006 (chilled to-go cookies exempt). TAAs bind only the taxpayer that asked. [SNIPPET] (as cited there)

**Drinks, catering, delivery and frozen dough**
- **Catering:** "Food, setup, delivery, and equipment rental charges on catering invoices are all subject to sales tax unless specifically exempt." [SNIPPET] [Moffa Tax Law, Florida restaurant sales tax guide](https://moffataxlaw.com/restaurant-sales-tax-guide/); [VNV Events, catering gratuity tax](https://vnvevents.com/blog/florida-catering-gratuity-tax/) (attribution between the two unclear)
- **Delivery charges, sources conflict:**
  - One says delivery charges are taxable when bundled into the price and the buyer has no pick-up option.
  - Another says a separately stated delivery charge that the buyer could avoid by picking up is not taxable. [SNIPPET] [Commenda](https://www.commenda.io/usa/florida/is-food-taxable); [Zamp](https://zamp.com/guides/florida-sales-tax) (attribution unclear)
- **Frozen take-and-bake dough:** no source found.

**Rate**
- Miami-Dade's surtax is **1%** (two 0.5% levies, no sunset), for a combined **7%**. The surtax applies only to the first **$5,000** of a single item; the 6% state tax applies to the full amount. [SNIPPET] [FloridaSalesTax.com, 2026 county surtax rates (Aug 2026)](https://www.floridasalestax.com/florida-tax-law-blog/2026/august/florida-county-surtax-rates-for-2026-the-complet/); [Florida Sales Tax Rates by County 2026](https://ustax.tools/florida-sales-tax-rates/); [filefloridasalestax.com](https://filefloridasalestax.com/rates-and-surtax/)

### Inferences
- **Keep the window seat-free.** Under the no-eating-facilities rule, a pickup window with no benches, tables or ledges keeps every unheated roll and box exempt. No separate register keys for to-go and dine-in are needed. Adding even a ledge or a bench could turn the shop into one "with eating facilities," which brings the separate-accounting duty described in costs_rules.md s. 5. **Do not put seating or a counter ledge near the window.**
- **Do not warm rolls to order.** Warming makes them "hot prepared food" and taxable at 7%. Rolls held warm from the oven are a grey area; get advice before selling warm rolls as a feature.
- **Coffee and iced coffee are taxable at 7%** whatever the layout. Price drinks tax-inclusive or tax-exclusive consistently.
- **Frozen unbaked take-and-bake packs [BACKGROUND, unverified].** These are probably exempt as food for home consumption: they are not hot, not prepared for immediate eating, and are sold to go. Ask DOR for a Technical Assistance Advisement (TAA) if they will be a large line.
- **Office trays [inference].** Office platters delivered and set up are likely "catering" and taxable. Boxes of rolls simply sold and handed over are likely exempt bakery products. Invoice the two differently.
- **Delivery apps [BACKGROUND, unverified].** Under Florida's 2021 marketplace-facilitator law (effective July 1, 2021), the app collects and remits tax on taxable items sold through it. Check each app's handling of exempt bakery items.
- **Schools [BACKGROUND, unverified].** Sales to public schools are usually exempt with the buyer's exemption certificate. Keep the certificates on file.

### Gaps
- Current official text and subsection numbering of Rule 12A-1.011 (flrules.org not reachable).
- Treatment of frozen unbaked dough, and of rolls held warm but not heated to order.
- DOR guidance on delivery charges for food.
- Whether a stand at a farmers market counts as having "eating facilities" (for example, the market's shared tables).

## 2. State food permits, wholesale, schools, labelling, farmers markets and the espresso window

### Takeaway
**FDACS is the regulator.**
- FDACS permits bakeries **and coffee shops** as retail food establishments, so an espresso or iced-coffee window should not by itself move the shop to the Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR).
- Selling to grocers and offices on the side most likely stays under the **retail bakery** permit while retail is the business's "primary" function. FDACS's own wording is that businesses "whose primary function is to sell food products directly to the consumer are considered retail."
- If wholesale becomes the main business, a **wholesale bakery** permit, FDA facility registration and preventive-controls rules apply.

**Fees.** The 2026 fees could not be confirmed. Rule 5K-4.020 shows Retail Bakery **$355 → $325**, Retail Bakery with Food Service **$440 → $400** and Wholesale Bakery **$465 → $425** in a rulemaking notice; it is unclear which figure is in force.

**Farmers markets and temporary events.** An FDACS-permitted establishment may run **one free unit at 1–3-day temporary events** under its permit. FDACS also gives **one free mobile permit** to a holder of a brick-and-mortar permit, for a unit selling pastry, coffee or other non-TCS food. (TCS = time/temperature control for safety: food that must be kept hot or cold to stay safe.)

**Labels.** Labels need ingredients, allergens, net quantity, and name and place of business. The federal low-volume nutrition-panel exemption covers firms with **<100 FTEs selling <100,000 units of a product a year** (annual notice to FDA required).

### Cited Findings
**Which agency**
- FDACS "issues retail food permits to food establishments selling directly to customers… includ[ing] supermarkets, grocery stores, convenience stores, **coffee shops, bakeries**… juice and smoothie bars… and FDACS-regulated mobile food units selling only prepackaged foods or non-potentially hazardous food items." [READ] [FDACS, Retail Food Establishment Permit](https://www.fdacs.gov/Business-Services/Food/Food-Establishments/Retail-Food-Establishment-Permit)
- **Wholesale program** [READ] [FDACS, Wholesale/Manufactured Food Program](https://www.fdacs.gov/Business-Services/Food/Food-Establishments/Wholesale-Manufactured-Food-Program):
  - It covers establishments "manufacturing, processing, packing, holding or preparing of food for sale or distribution to other businesses… Businesses that typically fall into the wholesale category include: **wholesale bakeries**…"
  - "Businesses whose primary function is to sell food products directly to the consumer are considered retail food establishments."
  - Manufactured-food establishments must register with FDA (free, renewed every two years). They are subject to 21 CFR 117 (current Good Manufacturing Practices, adopted in Rule 5K-4.002), with some exemptions from the preventive-controls subparts.
- **Rule 5K-4.020 definitions:**
  - Wholesale bakery: a food establishment that bakes "breads, pastries or other similar baked goods, **primarily for wholesale distribution**."
  - Retail bakery: one that bakes "primarily for retail sale on the premises." [SNIPPET] [flrules.org 5K-4.020](https://flrules.org/gateway/readFile.asp?sid=2&tid=4505570&type=1&File=5K-4.020.htm); [Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/florida/Fla-Admin-Code-Ann-R-5K-4-020)

  - The rule also charges a prorated 1/12 of the fee per month "from January 1, 2024" for establishments operating before their initial permit. [SNIPPET] [flrules.org 5K-4.020](https://flrules.org/gateway/readFile.asp?sid=1&tid=5882970&type=1&File=5K-4.020.htm); [elaws 5K-4.020(2)](http://flrules.elaws.us/fac/5k-4.020(2)); [flrules.org Chapter 5K-4](https://flrules.org/gateway/readFile.asp?sid=0&tid=0&cno=5K-4&caid=1334058&type=4&file=5K-4.doc)

**Mobile units and farmers markets** [READ] [FDACS, Mobile Food Establishments](https://www.fdacs.gov/Business-Services/Food/Food-Establishments/Mobile-Food-Establishments)
- **Definition:** mobile food establishments sell food "from trucks, trailers or similar self-propelled conveyances, or **non-fixed structures such as tables, booths or kiosks**."
- **FDACS's scope:** FDACS regulates those "that sell only prepackaged foods or non-potentially hazardous food items, including: Foods that are not temperature controlled for safety, including ice confections, **coffee, tea**, soft drinks, **pastry products**, popcorn and candies…"
- **DBPR's scope:** "Mobile vendors with food service are permitted by… DBPR." Units that "process or prepare potentially hazardous foods… within the mobile unit should contact DBPR."
- **Free unit:** "Food establishments with an active permit for preparing or processing foods at a brick-and-mortar facility (such as a commissary) are currently allowed to have **one free mobile food permit**." An application and an initial inspection are still required.
- **Commissary:** a Commissary Letter of Agreement is required unless the unit sells "only prepackaged non-potentially hazardous food."
- **Schools:** units "that operate on institutional property that is licensed and regulated as a food service establishment by the Florida Department of Health (DOH), including **schools**, universities… should contact DOH."
- **Temporary events:**
  - A food establishment permitted by FDACS or DOH "may operate one free unit under their regular permit at temporary food service events of 1–3 days." [SNIPPET] [DBPR, Temporary Event guide](https://www2.myfloridalicense.com/hotels-restaurants/licensing/temporaryevent-guide/) (attribution between DBPR and a third-party guide unclear)
  - DBPR temporary-event fees per a third-party guide: 1–3 days **$91**; 4–30 days **$105**; annual temporary-event vendor **$456**. [SNIPPET] [FindHomegrown, farmers market vendor permit](https://findhomegrown.com/blog/farmers-market-vendor-permit-florida)
  - Department of Health rule: sponsors or vendors must notify the county health department **at least three days** before an event. [SNIPPET] [Temporary food service events, DOH 5030-034 form](https://Sopchoppy.org/uploads/5/4/8/4/5484400/temporary_food_service_events_5030_034.pdf)

**Federal nutrition-labelling exemptions**
- FDA's low-volume exemption (21 CFR 101.9(j)(18)): firms with "fewer than an average of **100 full-time equivalent employees**" selling under "**100,000 units** of a product in the U.S. in a 12-month period." "A notice must be filed annually with FDA."
  - It is lost if the label or advertising carries any nutrient content or health claim (for example "sugar free").
  - It covers only the Nutrition Facts panel. Ingredients, allergen declaration, net quantity and name/place of business are still required.
  - [SNIPPET] [FDA, Small Business Nutrition Labeling Exemption](https://www.fda.gov/food/labeling-nutrition-guidance-documents-regulatory-information/small-business-nutrition-labeling-exemption); [FDA guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/small-business-nutrition-labeling-exemption-guidance)
- A law-firm post says firms with fewer than 10 employees and under 10,000 units need not file a notice. The search tool found no FDA page confirming it. [SNIPPET] [NC State Extension, FDA exemptions](https://content.ces.ncsu.edu/food-labeling-fda-exemptions-for-small-business)

### Inferences
- **Espresso and iced coffee [inference from FDACS wording].** FDACS lists "coffee shops" among its retail permittees, so the drinks window does not change the regulator. What could push the shop to DBPR is selling **food for immediate consumption as a restaurant would** (for example sandwiches or hot plates), not coffee. A seatless window helps. **Ask FDACS in writing for a jurisdiction determination**, since Pinecrest's business-tax form maps "eating establishment" to DBPR (costs_rules.md s. 6).
- **Farmers-market tent [inference].** The cheapest route is the FDACS **one free mobile permit** (a "table, booth or kiosk" counts as a mobile unit), using the bakery kitchen as its commissary. Rolls with a shelf-stable glaze and black coffee fit "pastry products" and "coffee." **Cream-cheese-iced rolls or milk-based drinks are TCS foods.** They would need temperature control and could push the unit toward DBPR. Confirm with FDACS before the first market.
- **Schools [inference].** Selling frozen dough to a school's kitchen is a sale to a DOH-regulated buyer. The school's food service will want an approved, permitted source, and the FDACS permit is that proof. Selling **on** school grounds from a mobile unit needs DOH contact. Expect district vendor registration and possibly product specifications (school-nutrition rules); not researched here.
- **Labels for frozen packs [BACKGROUND, unverified].**
  - Packaged products need: product name, net weight, ingredients in descending order, the major allergens (wheat, milk, egg, soy, tree nuts), and the firm's name and address.
  - Frozen take-and-bake packs also need **safe-handling and baking instructions** and "Keep frozen."
  - **Two exemptions likely remove the Nutrition Facts panel; confirm before printing.** A small retailer selling bakery items made on site is generally exempt under 21 CFR 101.9(j)(1)/(j)(3) [BACKGROUND]. Otherwise, the low-volume exemption with an annual FDA notice applies.
  - Any "low sugar"-type claim removes the exemption.
- **FDA facility registration [BACKGROUND].** Probably not needed while most sales are direct to consumers (the FDA "retail food establishment" exemption). It becomes needed if wholesale to grocers grows past half of sales.

- FDACS's line between a "retail bakery" doing some wholesale and a "wholesale bakery" (no percentage found).
- Fee for a "retail bakery with food service" if espresso is classed as food service.
- FDACS guidance on whether cream-cheese-iced pastries at markets need DBPR.
- Miami-Dade public schools' vendor rules for bakery products.

## 3. Cottage food: s. 500.80, F.S., and FDACS guidance

### Takeaway
- Florida lets home kitchens sell non-TCS foods, **including "loaf breads, rolls, biscuits" and "cakes, pastries and cookies,"** with no permit:
  - Gross sales are capped at **$250,000 a year**.
  - Sales may be in person, online or **by mail order**.
  - **No wholesale.**
- Cottage goods **cannot be sold alongside, or associated with, an FDACS- or DBPR-permitted food business**. The statute does not apply to anyone holding a s. 500.12 food permit.
- So a cottage route can **test the roll before the shop opens**, but cannot run beside the shop or under its brand.
- **Icings that need refrigeration (cream cheese) are excluded.** Whether a lab-tested shelf-stable dairy glaze qualifies is **unconfirmed**.
- No source addressed whether a cottage operation may use employees.

### Cited Findings
**FDACS Cottage Foods page** [READ] [FDACS, Cottage Foods](https://www.fdacs.gov/Business-Services/Food/Food-Establishments/Cottage-Foods)
- "Florida law allows individuals to use their unlicensed home kitchens to produce for sale certain foods that present a low risk of foodborne illness… without obtaining a food permit… Gross sales for a cottage food operation must not exceed **$250,000 annually**."
- Approved products include "Loaf breads, rolls, biscuits; Cakes, pastries and cookies; Candies and confections; … Fruit pies…"
- "Cottage food operators may sell cottage food products **on their website, by mail order, and direct to consumer (in person)**. Cottage food products **cannot be sold wholesale**. Cottage foods must be properly packaged and labeled. Cottage food operators can serve free samples… but the samples must be prepackaged. A cottage food operation must comply with all state or federal tax laws…"

**Statute**
- s. 500.80 applies to annual gross sales of cottage food products "that do not exceed $250,000."
  - It "does not apply to a person operating under a food permit issued pursuant to s. 500.12."
  - "The regulation of cottage food operations is preempted to the state."
  - The cap counts sales of all cottage products combined.
  - [SNIPPET] [Florida Statutes 2025, s. 500.80](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/0500.80); [2026 Florida Statutes, s. 500.80](https://m.flsenate.gov/statutes/500.80); [FindLaw](https://codes.findlaw.com/fl/title-xxxiii-regulation-of-trade-commerce-investments-and-solicitations/fl-st-sect-500-80/)
- The cap was raised from $50,000 to $250,000 by the 2021 "Home Sweet Home Act" (HB 1521 / SB 1294 of 2021). [SNIPPET] [UF/IFAS Lee County blog, 2022](https://blogs.ifas.ufl.edu/leeco/2022/08/25/cottage-food-operations-home-sweet-home-act-2021-update/); [Senate bill analysis 2021 SB 1294](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2021/1294/Analyses/2021s01294.cm.PDF) (older than 2025, but it is the enacting history)
- A cottage food product is defined as food "that is not time or temperature controlled for safety and is not a potentially hazardous food." [SNIPPET] [cottagefoodlawlibrary.org](https://cottagefoodlawlibrary.org/florida) (secondary)
- **No mixing with a permitted business:** "Cottage food products may not be sold with or associated with a regulated food business under FDACS or… DBPR. All cottage food products must be sold separately from non-cottage food products." [SNIPPET] [UF/IFAS EDIS FS425](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/FS425/pdf); [pickyourown FL FAQs](https://pickyourown.org/CottageFoodLaws-Florida-FAQs.php)
- **Garnish:** baked goods "may not be decorated or garnished with fresh fruits or vegetables." [SNIPPET] same sources
- **Icings:**
  - Most secondary guides say **cream-cheese frosting is not allowed** because it needs refrigeration.
  - Sources **conflict on butter icings**: one says FDACS disallows icings made with real butter or cream cheese; another says butter-and-powdered-sugar buttercream is allowed.
  - One guide says FDACS treats the water in a dairy ingredient as making the icing TCS.
  - The FDACS FAQ itself (ccmedia.fdacs.gov) was not reachable.
  - [SNIPPET] [cottagefoodlawlibrary.org](https://cottagefoodlawlibrary.org/florida); [floridacottagefoods.com](https://floridacottagefoods.com/florida-cottage-food-law); [cakerybakeries.com](https://cakerybakeries.com/cottage-laws/florida); [Keeper Tax](https://www.keepertax.com/posts/florida-cottage-food-law)
- **Water activity:** no source addressed it. **Employees:** no source addressed them. [SNIPPET, absence]

### Inferences
- **Lab-tested glaze [inference + BACKGROUND].** The statutory test is "not TCS / not potentially hazardous." The FDA Food Code (which FDACS adopts by reference, 2022 edition per the FDACS page) generally treats foods with water activity ≤0.88 as non-TCS, so a glaze tested below 0.85 has a technical argument. But secondary sources say FDACS screens icings by **ingredient** (dairy = no) rather than by measurement. **Treat the glaze as unconfirmed until FDACS answers in writing.**
- **Employees [BACKGROUND, unverified].** s. 500.80 defines a cottage operation as "a natural person or his or her household" operating from the home. On that reading, hired non-household staff are outside the exemption. Not confirmed.
- **Cottage labels [BACKGROUND, unverified].** s. 500.80 requires the operator's name and address, product name, ingredients in descending order, net quantity, allergens, nutrition information if any claim is made, and the statement "Made in a cottage food operation that is not subject to Florida's food safety regulations" in at least 10-point type. Check the statute text.
- **Planning use.** A cottage phase can pre-sell and test boxes and mail-order before the lease. **It must stop, or be kept fully separate (different products, no shared brand or sales point), once the FDACS-permitted bakery opens.**

### Gaps
- Verbatim s. 500.80 text (flsenate.gov not reachable).
- FDACS cottage FAQ wording on icings and on water-activity testing.
- Whether employees are allowed.
- Whether a cottage operator may hold an LLC.

## 4. Mobile selling: truck or trailer licence, preemption, and where trucks may sell

### Takeaway
**State licence**
- A bakery trailer selling **only pastry, coffee, tea and prepackaged or non-TCS items** can sit under **FDACS**, and is **free as the one mobile permit** attached to a brick-and-mortar FDACS permit.
- Anything cooked or assembled to order from TCS ingredients needs a **DBPR Mobile Food Dispensing Vehicle (MFDV) licence: $347/yr** ($178.50 half-year) per DBPR's fee page (date not shown).

**Preemption.** State law (s. 509.102, F.S., first enacted in 2020 and extended in 2023) **preempts local licences, permits and fees for MFDVs** and bars any city or county from **banning them across its whole territory**. Local rules on *where* and *how* they operate survive.

**Unincorporated Miami-Dade.** Mobile operations need a county Certificate of Use and may sell only in:
- **business or industrial zones (BU, IU), urban-centre/urban-area districts and GU land trended for business or industrial use;**
- or at **schools, churches, hospitals or museums** in residential zones.

Limits: at most **6 days in any 30 on one site**, 7 a.m.–10 p.m. weekdays (11 p.m. weekends), never on the public right-of-way.

**Towns.** No official Coral Gables or Pinecrest rule was found; secondary sources say Coral Gables allows trucks only at approved special events or on private commercial property with the owner's written consent.

### Cited Findings
**State licensing**
- **FDACS mobile scope:** see section 2 (pastry products, coffee and tea allowed; one free mobile permit; commissary letter unless prepackaged only). [READ] [FDACS, Mobile Food Establishments](https://www.fdacs.gov/Business-Services/Food/Food-Establishments/Mobile-Food-Establishments)
- **DBPR MFDV fee:** "Mobile Food Dispensing Vehicles, Hot Dog Carts and Temporary Commercial Kitchens… **$347** for a full year and **$178.50** for the half-year." **Preemption, s. 509.102, F.S.**
- "Regulation of mobile food dispensing vehicles, and temporary commercial kitchens, involving licenses, registrations, permits, and fees is preempted to the state. A municipality, county, or other local governmental entity may not require a separate license, registration, or permit other than the license required under s. 509.241, or require the payment of any license, registration, or permit fee other than the fee required under s. 509.251…"
- "A municipality, county, or other local governmental entity **may not prohibit mobile food dispensing vehicles… from operating within the entirety of the entity's jurisdiction**."
- "This section may not be construed to affect a… local governmental entity's authority to regulate the operation of mobile food dispensing vehicles… other than the regulations described."
- [SNIPPET] [Florida Statutes 2024, s. 509.102](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/509.102); [FindLaw](https://codes.findlaw.com/fl/title-xxxiii-regulation-of-trade-commerce-investments-and-solicitations/fl-st-sect-509-102/)

  - The 2020 provision appears in a committee amendment to CS/SB 474 (2020). The enacted 2020 bill number (reported as HB 1193, the 2020 deregulation bill) was **not confirmed** by search.
  - The 2023 amendment (SB 752 / HB 415, Ch. 2023-48) extended the section to temporary commercial kitchens, **effective July 1, 2023**.

## 7. Staffing rules and market

### Takeaway
**Wages (from search snippets).** Florida's minimum wage rose to **$15.00 on Sept 30, 2026**, with a **$11.98 tipped cash wage** (the $3.02 tip credit is fixed). Wages are covered in costs_rules.md.

**Tip pooling.** Florida has **no tip-pooling statute**, so federal Fair Labor Standards Act rules govern:
- If the employer takes **no tip credit**, the pool can include back-of-house staff such as bakers.
- If it takes a tip credit, the pool must be limited to customarily tipped staff.
- Managers can never share.

**E-Verify.** It is mandatory for private employers with **25 or more employees** for new hires since **July 1, 2023** (s. 448.095). A 2025 bill to extend it to all employers was found, but its fate is unknown.

**Not found:** Miami-Dade or town leave or wage rules for private employers; counter tips per hour; baker hiring difficulty.

### Inferences
- **Demand [inference].** The resident base is flat to slightly shrinking: domestic out-migration, cushioned by immigration that is now slowing. Visitors are at records. For a neighbourhood bakery in Pinecrest, Palmetto Bay and Kendall, **resident households matter more than tourists**. Treat population as a headwind, not a growth driver, and lean on the visitor season (Dec–Apr) for markets and events.
- **Rent [inference].** Rent is the stated killer of 2025–26 closures. A seatless kitchen-plus-window needs far less frontage than a café, which is the plan's best defence. Ask about rent escalators (3%+ a year is common per one report).
- **Ingredient costs [inference].** Eggs and butter are much cheaper in 2026 than at the 2025 egg-price peak. The dairy outlook's butter price (~$1.24/lb wholesale, CME basis) looks low against typical 2024 levels (about $3/lb [BACKGROUND]), so butter is likely a **saving against 2024**. Coffee is the main rising input; price drinks accordingly.
