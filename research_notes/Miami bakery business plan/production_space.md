# Cheap production space for a roll bakery in south Miami-Dade (as of Nov 19, 2026)

Notes on method:
- Research date: Nov 19, 2026. 30 web searches were used, the maximum allowed.
- **[R]** means I read the page itself with curl. Only www.miamidade.gov and www.fdacs.gov pages could be read this way.
- **[S]** means the fact comes from search-result text only; I did not read the page. Every commissary, listing, equipment and news fact below is [S].
- Listing "age" figures (e.g. "~665 days old") are the search tool's estimate of how old the indexed page is. I turned them into approximate dates, and these dates are approximate.
- **OLD** marks anything dated before 2025.

### Inferences

  - At $21–$45 an hour, that is about **$1,400–$5,000 a month** before storage.

- **Questions for every kitchen:** mixer size, whether retarding racks can stay in the walk-in overnight, and storage fees.

## 3. Renting another business's kitchen off-hours; two permit holders in one kitchen

### Takeaway
No specific Miami bakery, restaurant, church or caterer renting its kitchen overnight was found with a price. Church fellowship-hall kitchens are marketed nationally at about $800+/month for one day a week. On permits:
- The UF/IFAS extension says each business in a shared kitchen must get its own permit.
- FDACS treats a commissary as a permitted brick-and-mortar food establishment.
- DBPR says any FDACS-permitted or H&R-licensed fixed establishment can act as a commissary.

So two permit holders in one kitchen is the normal shared-kitchen model, but the exact FDACS rule text was not found.

### Cited Findings
- **Food Web church-kitchen marketplace:** "renting once a week ≈ $800+/month"; listing is free and the platform takes "12% on completed bookings". National, not Miami — [foodweb.network/churches](https://foodweb.network/churches) [S].
- **Earlier evidence of borrowed rooms** (already in prior notes): Babette inside Clásica Victoria in South Miami. No new Miami off-hours examples were found.
- **UF/IFAS extension FS400:** "it is the responsibility of each business owner to obtain the necessary permit to operate within the facility" — [UF/IFAS EDIS FS400](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/FS400/pdf) [S; publication date not seen].
- **DBPR temporary commercial kitchen guide:** "any FDACS permitted or H&R licensed fixed food service establishment can provide commissary services" — [DBPR TECK guide](https://www2.myfloridalicense.com/hotels-restaurants/licensing/teck-guide/) [S].
- **FDACS mobile-vendor guidance** [S] — [FDACS Mobile Food Establishments](https://www.fdacs.gov/Business-Services/Food-Establishments/Mobile-Food-Vendors) and [FDACS form 14223 (Rev. 11/18, OLD)](https://forms.fdacs.gov/14223.pdf):
  - Mobile operators preparing food at a commissary need an FDACS food permit "to cover the processing/preparing of food".
  - Others sign a Commissary Letter of Agreement.
- **FDACS Food Establishments page** [R, read Nov 19, 2026] — [FDACS Food Establishments](https://www.fdacs.gov/Business-Services/Food/Food-Establishments):
  - "To open a food establishment in Florida, you must obtain a food establishment permit from FDACS."
  - Permit types offered: Retail Food Establishment Permit and Wholesale/Manufactured Food Establishment Permit.
- **FDACS Retail Food Establishment Permit page** [R]: it says renewal is annual ("Each year, the food establishment owner is required to submit a renewal application and permit fees"). I found no sentence on shared kitchens on it — [FDACS Retail Food Establishment Permit](https://www.fdacs.gov/Business-Services/Food/Food-Establishments/Retail-Food-Establishment-Permit).

### Inferences
- **Which agency.** A bakery selling mostly to the public and wholesale is FDACS-regulated. A host restaurant is usually DBPR-licensed. A bakery tenant in a DBPR restaurant kitchen would likely still need its own FDACS permit at that address. Confirm with FDACS before signing.
- **Typical price.** For a Miami off-hours restaurant kitchen, the Facebook "$13–$65/hr" range and the "$25/hr" SW 131 Ave listing are the best local proxies found.

### Gaps
- The exact FDACS rule (5K-4 F.A.C.) on multiple permit holders at one address. The text was not found on readable pages.
- Named Miami churches, bakeries or caterers renting overnight, with prices.

## 5. Zoning: bakery with retail or a walk-up window in industrial districts

### Takeaway
Unincorporated Miami-Dade IU-1 lists bakeries as "wholesale only with incidental retail uses" (search-result text). The county's own IU-1 page [R] allows a share of floor area to be retail if the item is made or stored on the premises. IU-C takes all IU-1 uses [R]. So a production bakery selling its own rolls at a counter or window is plausible as an incidental use in IU-1/IU-C, but a retail-led bakery-café is not clearly allowed. BU-3 is the county district that explicitly allows a "Bakery, retail & wholesale" [R]. A 2026 county case shows the pattern: a brewery in IU-1 got its taproom and retail approved only "as an accessory use". City of South Miami's light-industrial zone is a TODD (LI-4) sub-district; its bakery row was not found.

### Cited Findings
- **County IU-1 district page** [R, read Nov 19, 2026] — [Miami-Dade IU-1 page](https://www.miamidade.gov/global/economy/zoning/districts/iu-1-industrial-light-manufacturing.page):
  - Uses: "Light industrial manufacturing; Wholesale distribution facilities; Warehousing; Storage facilities; Offices".

## 6. Walk-up service windows (ventanitas)

### Takeaway
Unincorporated Miami-Dade's BU-1 rules allow "outside walk-up window svc ok if min. 7' sidewalk" for restaurants and coffee houses (county legend [R], dated 2002/2011). No walk-up window rule was found for IU-1. No ordinance text was found for Pinecrest, Palmetto Bay or South Miami. The one Pinecrest precedent found is Sergio's adding a ventanita at Suniland Plaza (11927 S Dixie Hwy). Coral Gables' conditional-use rule for walk-up counters is in earlier notes and is not repeated here.

### Cited Findings
- **County BU zoning legend, BU-1 uses** [R; OLD, PDF 2002, modified 2011]: "Restaurant, coffee house, dining room if kitchen screened or in enclosed bldg (alcoholic beverage incidental); outside walk-up window svc ok if min. 7' sidewalk. Outdoor table service if approved at public hearing." — [BU zoning legend](https://www.miamidade.gov/permits/library/forms/legend-bu-zoning.pdf). BU-1A, BU-2 and BU-3 include the "more restrictive BU uses", so the rule carries up through BU-3 (same PDF [R]).
- **Pinecrest:**
  - Sergio's opened at 11927 S Dixie Hwy (Suniland Plaza) with a ventanita; no permit details given — [Lynley Residential blog](https://lynleyresidential.com/blog/sergios-cuban-restaurant-opens-in-pinecrest) [S].
  - Miami New Times: "zoning challenges, limited parking availability, and shopping plazas built in an era that left little room for modern restaurant amenities" delayed the opening, and the site has one of Pinecrest's first approved outdoor terraces — [Miami New Times](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/food-drink/pinecrest-finally-lands-cuban-staple-with-new-sergios-location-40510665/) [S; date not shown].
- **Palmetto Bay, South Miami:** no walk-up window rule found.
- **Site searches:** the pinecrest-fl.gov site search returned 404 and the coralgables.com search returned an empty 202 when I tried them (Nov 19, 2026) [R attempt].

### Inferences
- **Unincorporated BU parcels.** Including the parts of Kendall and Perrine that are not in a city, a ventanita is likely an as-of-right feature where there is a 7 ft sidewalk. This rests on the old legend; confirm against current §33-150 et seq.
- **Pinecrest.** Sergio's shows a window can be approved, but the route is unknown.

### Gaps
- Current county code text on walk-up windows.
- The Pinecrest, Palmetto Bay, South Miami and Cutler Bay rules.
- Whether IU-1 "incidental retail" may be served through an exterior window.
