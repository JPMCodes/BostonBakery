# Acquisition route: buying a business that already earns about $500,000 (South Florida, late 2026)

Research date: 2026-11-25. Method note: every page fetch in this session failed (the hosts were outside the session's allowed network, e.g. ibba.org and mmcginvest.com), so **all findings below come from search-result text (snippets and search-engine summaries), not from reading the full pages.** Figures should be checked against the primary documents before being relied on. Several sources are broker, lender or SEO "guide" sites with a commercial interest. They are labelled where it matters. **Every listing figure is the seller's or broker's claim, not verified.** "Cash flow" on listing sites usually means SDE (seller's discretionary earnings, i.e. before any owner salary), not EBITDA.

## 1. What does $500,000 of annual earnings cost to buy in 2026? (multiples by size and sector)

### Takeaway
Small businesses sell for roughly 2-3x SDE, and the multiple rises with size. The IBBA Q2 2026 survey gives 2.0x SDE under $500K of price, 2.8x at $500K-1M, 3.1x at $1-2M and 4.0x EBITDA at $2-5M. A business with about $500K of EBITDA (perhaps $600-650K of SDE) would therefore be priced at roughly $1.5M-2.5M. Restaurants trade lowest (about 2.4x cash flow in Q3 2026) and bakeries at about 2.7x (2025). Wholesale or manufacturing bakeries and food distributors are quoted at 3-6x EBITDA, and laundromats and car washes trade highest (about 4-4.7x SDE).

### Inferences (mine)
- A business with $500K of EBITDA after paying a manager probably shows $600-650K of SDE. At 2.8-3.1x SDE or 3.5-4.5x EBITDA, that implies a price of about **$1.7M-2.25M** for a typical service or food business. A restaurant might be $1.2-1.5M (about 2.4x), and a wholesale bakery or distributor with contracts $2-3M.
- Moving from the $100-250K earnings band to the $500K-1M band adds roughly 0.8-1.1 turns to the multiple (about 2.0x to about 2.8-3.1x). The $1-2M earnings band moves into 4-6x EBITDA territory.
- Restaurant multiples are the lowest of all sectors studied. That is cheap per dollar of earnings but reflects the risk.

### Gaps

- No source gave multiples by **earnings** band for food businesses specifically. Wholesale bakery figures come from one broker with an interest.
- The BizBuySell Q3 2026 market-wide multiple level was not stated in the snippets.

## 2. SBA 7(a) acquisition loans in late 2026

### Takeaway
A change-of-ownership 7(a) loan can be up to $5M over 10 years (longer only if real estate is included), priced at up to prime + 3% (prime was 7.00% in November 2026, so about 10%). It needs at least 10% equity. A full-standby seller note can supply no more than half of that 10%. Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (loan numbers from October 1, 2026) it must cover debt 1.25x on **historical** earnings, not projections. Every 20%+ owner must give an unlimited personal guarantee. Lenders and default data both favour buying an existing business over a start-up.

### Cited Findings
- **Prime rate:** 7.00% on 2026-11-09 (daily) and 2026-11-11 (weekly). The Fed held rates at its October 2026 meeting (target range given as 3.75-4.0%, which conflicts with a 3.50-3.75% range reported after December 2025; not resolved). — [FRED DPRIME](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph?graph_id=1218081); [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/business/loans/news/fed-rate-cut-small-business-loans)
- **Rate cap:** variable-rate 7(a) loans over $350,000 are capped at prime + 3%, and the term does not change the cap (2026 tables). — [NerdWallet SBA rates Nov 2026](https://nerdwallet.com/business/loans/learn/sba-loan-rates); [Nav](https://www.nav.com/blog/sba-loan-rates-74401/)
- **Base rate:** from March 1, 2026, lenders may use other "widely adopted" base rates, but the cap stays at prime + the allowed spread (Federal Register, Feb 10, 2026). — [Federal Register 2026-02660](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-02-10/html/2026-02660.htm)
- **Maximum loan and term:** $5M maximum (one blog's $10M claim is unconfirmed). Without real estate the maximum term is 10 years, fully amortized, with no prepayment penalty. Goodwill and intangibles are limited to 10 years. If 51%+ of proceeds go to real estate, the term can reach 25 years. — [BizBuySell SBA acquisition guide](https://www.bizbuysell.com/learning-center/sba-7a-loan-business-acquisition/); [CVBBA SBA fact sheet, July 2025](https://www.cvbba.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/CVBBA-SBA-Fact-Sheet-v3-FINAL-JULY-2025.pdf); [LendingTree, July 2026](https://lendingtree.com/business/restaurant-loans/)
- **Equity injection:** at least 10% of total project cost for a complete change of ownership. — [SBA Procedural Notice 5000-846607](https://www.sba.gov/sites/sbagov/files/2023-08/2023.05.09%20Procedural%20Notice%205000-846607%20Implementation%20of%20Affiliation%20Rule-R.pdf) (rule since SOP 50 10 8, June 2025)
- **Seller notes under SOP 50 10 8 (June 2025):** a seller note counts toward equity only if it is on full standby (no principal or interest) for the whole loan term, and it may supply no more than 50% of the injection. — [Whiteford law client alert](https://www.whitefordlaw.com/news-events/client-alert-sba-issues-sop-50-10-8-key-changes-impacting-sba-7a-lending)
- **SOP 50 10 8.1 (loans numbered on or after October 1, 2026), per lender and consultant commentary:**
  - Seller standby debt, other standby debt and minority-investor equity *combined* can cover no more than half of the required injection.
  - A non-standby seller note must be current for 36 months (was 24) before it can be refinanced with SBA money.
  - The seller may stay on as a consultant for up to 24 months (was 12).
  - The 10% minimum is unchanged.
  
  Sources: [Pioneer Capital Advisory](https://www.pioneercapitaladvisory.com/post/sba-seller-note-requirements-sop-50-10-8-1); [Jaken Finance](https://jakenfinancegroup.com/blog/sba-sop-50-10-8-1-october-2026-changes/); [AcquisitionAce](https://www.acquisitionace.io/learn/sba-loans/sop-changes-2026); [Cantrell Firm](https://cantrellfirm.com/blog/new-sba-acquisition-rules-sop-50-10-8-1/)
- **Debt-service coverage under 8.1:** 1.25x for initial acquisitions and owner buyouts; 1.15x kept for expansions. The test uses historical or adjusted historical earnings (last fiscal year or a two-year average), and projections cannot be used for change-of-ownership loans. — [Matthews](https://www.matthews.com/insights/new-sba-lending-requirements-take-effect-october-first); [Starfield & Smith, Sept 2026](https://starfieldsmith.com/2026/09/best-practices-change-of-ownership-transactions-under-sop-50-10-8-1-what-sba-lenders-need-to-know/); [FastCasual](https://www.fastcasual.com/blogs/3-restaurant-growth-deals-and-what-the-new-sba-rules-change-about-each/)
- **Credit tightening:** American Banker reports that SBA started FY2027 "with an even tighter credit box." — [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/sba-starts-new-fiscal-year-with-even-tighter-credit-box) (headline only)
- **Personal guarantee:** anyone owning 20%+ after the sale gives an unlimited personal guarantee (13 CFR 120.160). A seller who keeps equity must guarantee the loan for 2 years (SOP 50 10 8). — [Prometai](https://prometai.app/blog/sba-business-acquisition-loan-updates); [Whiteford](https://www.whitefordlaw.com/news-events/client-alert-sba-issues-sop-50-10-8-key-changes-impacting-sba-7a-lending)
- **Collateral:** SBA expects loans to be fully secured but will not decline a loan for insufficient collateral alone. If collateral covers less than 1:1, the lender must take a lien on real estate owned by guarantors where the guarantor has 25%+ equity in it. — [BizBuySell](https://www.bizbuysell.com/learning-center/sba-7a-loan-business-acquisition/); [CVBBA fact sheet](https://www.cvbba.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/CVBBA-SBA-Fact-Sheet-v3-FINAL-JULY-2025.pdf)
- **Acquisition vs start-up:** SBA treats a business with revenue for one year or less as a start-up. Lenders call restaurants high-risk, and start-ups are underwritten on projections, whereas acquisitions are underwritten on 2-3 years of tax returns. — [Bay Street Lending / sba7a.loans / LendingTree summaries](https://www.sba7a.loans/sba-7a-loans-small-business-blog/sba-7a-loan-for-a-restaurant/)
- **Default by loan purpose (broker analysis of FOIA data, 2020-2025 loans):** change of ownership 0.71%, start-up 1.43%, businesses ≤ 2 years old 1.99%. The default definition is unclear, and young loans have had little time to default. — [GoSBALoans](https://gosbaloans.com/guide/sba-loan-default-rates-by-loan-use/)
- **Restaurant charge-off rates:** these vary by source and definition.
  - Full-service (NAICS 722511): 5.42% dollar-weighted (all years).
  - Limited-service (722513): 6.17%.
  - FY2010-2015 cohorts by loan count: 9.0% full-service and 9.1% limited-service.
  - One directory reports an 18.9% "default rate" for limited-service among resolved loans.
  
  Sources: [PeerSense 722511](https://peersense.com/sba-industry/fullservice-restaurants-722511); [PeerSense limited-service](https://peersense.com/industry-data/limited-service-restaurants-722); [StartBusinessByState](https://startbusinessbystate.com/sba-loan-default-rates/)
- **Franchise benchmarks:** the seasoned charge-off frequency for the whole SBA 7(a) franchise portfolio is 3.75% (Coleman, July 2026). FRANdata (18,000+ loans) finds about 15% charge-offs for high-risk brands and under 2% for minimal-risk brands. No bakery or dessert brand rates were found. — [Coleman CFR 2.0 methodology](https://colemanreport.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/260716-Coleman-CFR-2.0-Franchise-Methodology.pdf); [FRANdata](https://frandata.com/reports/fund-score-franchise-loan-performance-frandata/)
- **Lender type:** SBLC-made loans had a 14.97% portfolio default rate vs 9.79% for other lenders (as of March 31, 2025). — [SBA OIG Report 26-14, Sept 2026](https://www.oversight.gov/sites/default/files/documents/reports/2026-09/SBA%20OIG%20Report%2026-14%20-%20Small%20Business%20Lending%20Companies%E2%80%99%20Performance%20in%20the%207%28a%29%20Loan%20Program%20and%20SBA%E2%80%99s%20Oversight.pdf)

### Inferences (mine)
- In late 2026 an acquisition loan is about 10% (7.00% prime + 3%) on a 10-year amortization, which costs about 15.9% of the loan balance per year. For example, a $1.65M loan costs about $262K a year.
- Because 8.1 uses historical earnings only, the seller's last tax-return year is what sizes the loan. A 1.25x test means the business must show about $1.25 of earnings for every $1 of debt service.
- Restaurants default at several times the all-industry rate. An established business with a record of earnings is clearly easier to finance than a start-up bakery or restaurant.

### Gaps
- I did not read the SOP 50 10 8.1 text itself. The 8.1 details come from lender and consultant blogs.
- Typical spreads actually charged (versus the cap) and the SBA guarantee fee for FY2027 were not found.
- No published SBA charge-off rate for bakeries (NAICS 311811/445291) or bakery and dessert franchises was found.
- Whether a primary residence must be pledged was not confirmed.

## 4. How often do such purchases work?

### Takeaway
Funded search funds acquire a business in a little over half of searches, and those that do have earned high average returns (33.9% IRR in the US). Losses are still common, especially for solo searchers (27% capital-loss rate internationally). For SBA borrowers, change-of-ownership loans default at about half the rate of start-up loans in one broker's FOIA analysis. Probably only a minority of listed businesses ever sell (my rough estimate is about 17% a year). The usual failure points are overstated add-backs, customer concentration and dependence on the owner.

### Cited Findings
- **Stanford 2026 Search Fund Study** (data to December 31, 2025; published around July 2026): aggregate IRR 33.9% and ROI 4.75x. Since 1996, 58% of funds have acquired a company. About half of funds launched in 2021-2024 have acquired so far (48% per one blog; sample 862 funds). — [Stanford GSB, July 13, 2026](https://gsb.stanford.edu/insights/search-funds-keep-offering-proven-path-ownership); [Peony](https://www.peony.ink/blog/search-fund-guide)
- **Yale (July 2026):** the cumulative acquisition rate fell from about 71% historically to 58%. Yale estimates a trailing flow rate of about 41% and a terminal rate of about 30% for the latest cohort (the authors' own calculations). — [Yale SOM, "The pernicious effects of falling acquisition rates"](https://som.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2026-07/The%20Pernicious%20Effects%20of%20Falling%20Acquisition%20Rates%20in%20the%20ETA%20Ecosystem.pdf)
- **IESE 2026 international study** (8th edition, data to end-2025):
  - 492 funds in total; 73% of completed searches ended in an acquisition; aggregate ROI 2.8x and IRR 30%.
  - Of the 216 funds that acquired a company, 167 were still operating, 38 had exited with a gain, and 11 had exited or closed at a loss.
  - Capital-loss rate: 12% for partnered searches vs 27% for solo searches.
  
  Source: [IESE Insight, "Search funds record expansion"](https://www.iese.edu/insight/articles/search-funds-record-expansion/)
- **SBA by loan purpose:** change of ownership 0.71% vs start-up 1.43% default (broker FOIA analysis of 2020-2025 loans). — [GoSBALoans](https://gosbaloans.com/guide/sba-loan-default-rates-by-loan-use/)
- **Sell-through:** BizBuySell data covers about 50,000 listed or recently sold businesses, against about 8,500 closed sales a year (2,117 in Q2 2026; 9,093 in 2023). No published sell-through rate was found. — [BizBuySell Q2 2025 report](https://www.bizbuysell.com/news/bizbuysell-2025-second-quarter-insight-report); [Searchfunder thread (unanswered)](https://searchfunder.com/post/what-percentage-of-advertised-business-for-sale-dont-sell)
- **Common mistakes (broker and lender commentary):**
  - Lenders reject many seller add-backs, which lowers SDE and the loan size, so the deal ends up priced above what a lender will finance.
  - A single customer above 20% of revenue is a warning sign, and one lender calls 40% "existential."
  - Dependence on the owner makes customers, staff and vendors hard to keep.
  
  Sources: [BizBuySell, financing problems](https://www.bizbuysell.com/learning-center/article/financing-problems-business-acquisitions/); [GoSBALoans, why acquisitions fail](https://gosbaloans.com/blog/why-business-acquisitions-fail/); [Searchfunder, 19 ways](https://searchfunder.com/post/19-ways-weve-seen-small-business-acquisitions-fail)
- The often-repeated claim that "70-90% of acquisitions fail (HBR)" refers to large corporate M&A and could not be verified. Do not use it.

### Inferences (mine)
- Search-fund statistics describe professionally backed, MBA-led buyers of $1-5M EBITDA firms. They are an optimistic upper bound for an individual buyer of a $500K food business.
- The 0.71% vs 1.43% default gap comes from young loans and probably understates lifetime losses on both. The direction (acquisition safer than start-up) is consistent with the underwriting logic.
- With restaurant charge-offs at 5-9%, a food acquisition carries more risk than the average SBA acquisition.

### Gaps
- No direct data on outcomes for self-funded or individual SBA buyers (failure or loss rates) was found.
- The share of acquired search-fund companies that lost money in the Stanford study was not found.
- No sell-through rate from BizBuySell or IBBA was found.

## 5. Due-diligence norms for buying a food business in Florida

### Takeaway
Earnings should be proven by reconciling three to five years of federal tax returns with sales-tax returns, point-of-sale reports and 12 months or more of bank deposits. Under the 8.1 rules the SBA lender will size the loan on the last tax-return year anyway. In Florida the buyer can inherit the seller's unpaid state taxes (s. 213.758) unless the seller provides a Department of Revenue certificate of compliance or the price is escrowed. The new owner must get its own DBPR (restaurant) or FDACS (food establishment) licence. The lease needs landlord consent, and the seller may remain liable unless released.

### Cited Findings
- **Earnings documents:**
  - Review 3-5 years of tax returns, financial statements and P&Ls.
  - Compare federal returns and current-year sales-tax returns with reported sales.
  - Check 12 months of bank statements, split into cash and card sales.
  - Reconcile POS data against deposits and tax returns.
  - Recalculate cash flow yourself rather than accept the owner's estimate.
  
  Sources: [Toast, buying an existing restaurant checklist](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/on-the-line/buying-an-existing-restaurant-checklist); [Restaurant Realty](https://restaurantrealty.com/the-buyers-due-diligence-process-part-ii/); [Mondaq (NJ restaurant purchase)](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/contracts-and-commercial-law/1796550/buying-a-restaurant-in-new-jersey-what-you-need-to-know-before-you-sign); [SCORE](https://www.score.org/resource/blog-post/what-know-you-buy-a-restaurant)
- **Lender requirement:** 2-3 years of tax returns plus interim statements for existing restaurants. Under 8.1, debt coverage is tested on the last fiscal year or a two-year average. — [sba7a.loans](https://www.sba7a.loans/sba-7a-loans-small-business-blog/sba-7a-loan-for-a-restaurant/); [Starfield & Smith](https://starfieldsmith.com/2026/09/best-practices-change-of-ownership-transactions-under-sop-50-10-8-1-what-sba-lenders-need-to-know/)
- **Florida successor tax liability (s. 213.758, F.S.):**
  - A buyer of a business is liable for the seller's unpaid taxes administered by the Department of Revenue (excluding corporate income tax) unless the seller provides a receipt or certificate of compliance from the Department. The certificate must show that no audit notice is outstanding, all returns are filed and all tax is paid.
  - The buyer's liability is capped at the greater of fair market value or purchase price, net of liens owed to non-insiders.
  - Involuntary transfers (e.g. foreclosure by a non-insider) are excluded.
  - Section 213.758 (2010) replaced the older sales-tax rule in s. 212.10.
  
  Sources: [Fla. Stat. 213.758 (2024)](https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/213.758); [2012 bill analysis](https://flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2012/170/Analyses/2012s0170.bft.PDF)
- **Licences:**
  - DBPR's Division of Hotels and Restaurants requires new owners of existing establishments to obtain a licence before operating. A change-of-ownership application needs no inspection if the previous owner passed one within 120 days.
  - Each operation needs a separate licence.
  - Florida splits food regulation among DBPR, FDACS and DOH.
  - Alcohol licences transfer under a separate regime (s. 561.32, DABT transfer application).
  
  Sources: [DBPR licensing](https://www2.myfloridalicense.com/hotels-restaurants/licensing/general/); [Fla. Admin. Code 61C-1.002](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/florida/Fla-Admin-Code-Ann-R-61C-1-002); [DABT transfer package](https://www2.myfloridalicense.com/abt/forms/licensing/transfer_of_ownership_application_package.pdf)
- **Lease:**
  - Under Florida case law, a landlord cannot arbitrarily refuse consent where the lease sets no standard, because good faith and commercial reasonableness apply (Fernandez line of cases).
  - A landlord's consent to assignment does not release the original tenant unless the release is stated.
  - Releasing the tenant can also release its guarantor.
  - One law firm summarizes s. 83.06 as allowing assignment unless the lease prohibits it (unverified).
  
  Sources: [Shutts & Bowen](https://www.shutts.com/business-and-legal-insights/must-a-commercial-landlord-consent-to-a-lease-assignment); [GrayRobinson materials](https://www.gray-robinson.com/uploads/files/Commercial%20Landlord-Tenant%20Law%20in%20Florida.pdf); [Jimerson Firm](https://www.jimersonfirm.com/services/landlord-tenant-leasing-commercial/lease-assignment-and-subletting/)
- **Seller tax issues:** resolving unpaid sales, payroll or corporate taxes with the state can take 1-4 months after closing. — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/contracts-and-commercial-law/1796550/buying-a-restaurant-in-new-jersey-what-you-need-to-know-before-you-sign)

### Inferences (mine)
- For a cash-heavy bakery or restaurant, Florida DR-15 sales-tax filings are the best cross-check of revenue. Unreported cash cannot be paid for, because the lender will not count it.
- Hold back part of the price in escrow until the DOR certificate is issued.
- A bakery that mainly makes and wholesales goods probably falls under FDACS (food permit), while a seated restaurant falls under DBPR. The licensing agency needs confirming for each target.

### Gaps
- The current DOR form and process for the certificate of compliance were not found.
- FDACS change-of-ownership rules for bakeries were not confirmed.
- SBA's requirement that the lease term match the loan term was not sourced in this session. Estoppel practice and quality-of-earnings norms were not found.

## 6. How a buyer with $500K of equity structures the purchase of about $500K EBITDA

### Takeaway
A typical structure for a business with $500K EBITDA (about $600-650K SDE):
- Price $1.75-2.25M.
- 10-20% down from the buyer.
- An SBA 7(a) loan of $1.6-2.0M at about 10% over 10 years, costing $260-320K a year.
- Often a small full-standby seller note.

Before tax, capital spending and debt principal risk, the owner keeps roughly $180-240K a year in years 1-10 and about the full $500K after the loan is repaid. The $500K of cash covers the down payment and leaves a reserve.

### Cited Findings
- A common published structure is 10% buyer cash, a 5% full-standby seller note and an 85% SBA loan. On a $2M deal at 10.5% over 10 years, that is about $20,200 a month (about $242K a year) on the SBA piece. — [CT Acquisitions loan calculator / worked example](https://ctacquisitions.com/business-acquisition-loan-calculator/); [ProjectionHub seller-note guide](https://www.projectionhub.com/post/guide-to-seller-notes-and-sba-loans-for-buying-a-business)
- Required equity is 10% of total project cost, and seller-note credit is at most half of it, on full standby (rules effective October 1, 2026). — [Bennett Financials](https://bennettfinancials.com/sba-loan-to-buy-a-business); [Regalis Capital](https://learn.regaliscapital.com/guides/full-standby-seller-note-explained/)
- Owner take-home = adjusted SDE − total annual debt service (calculator convention). Lenders test cash flow after a market salary for the buyer. — [SearchEros SBA calculator guide](https://searcheros.ai/blog/sba-loan-calculator-guide)
- A worked financing example is published by [Silver Surf](https://www.silversurf.co/blog/posts/small-business-financing-worked-example.html) (contents not visible in the snippet).

### Inferences (mine): worked scenarios
Assumptions:
- EBITDA of $500K after paying a general manager, so the buyer manages but does not take a separate salary.
- Prime 7.00% + 3% = 10% SBA rate (cap), 10-year amortization.
- About $100-150K of closing costs and working capital added to the project.
- Seller note at 8% where it is not on standby.

Annual payments were calculated with a standard amortization formula.

| Scenario | Price | Project cost | Buyer cash in | Seller note | SBA loan | Annual SBA debt service | DSCR (on $500K) | Owner pre-tax cash, yrs 1-10 | Cash reserve left |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. 3.5x EBITDA | $1.75M | ~$1.9M | $250K | none | $1.65M | ~$262K | 1.91x | ~$238K | ~$250K |
| B. 4.5x EBITDA, minimum equity | $2.25M | ~$2.4M | $240K (10%) | none | $2.16M | ~$343K | 1.46x | ~$157K | ~$260K |
| C. 4.5x EBITDA, all cash in, 5% standby note | $2.25M | ~$2.4M | $380K | $120K (standby, $0 paid yrs 1-10) | $1.9M | ~$301K | 1.66x | ~$199K | ~$120K |
| D. 3.5x with $250K amortizing seller note | $1.75M | ~$1.9M | $250K | $250K at 8% (~$36K/yr) | $1.40M | ~$222K | 1.94x incl. note | ~$242K | ~$250K |

- Every scenario passes the 1.25x test, provided the $500K is supported by the **last tax return**.
- From year 11 the owner keeps about the full $500K, less capital spending.
- The owner's pre-tax cash before debt repayment is lower than the "income" figure, because about 40-60% of each payment is principal in the early years. Taxable income is therefore higher than the cash kept.
- Income tax, capital spending (ovens, vans) and working-capital swings must come out of the remaining $160-240K.
- If EBITDA falls 20% to $400K, scenario B coverage drops to 1.17x and owner cash to about $57K. That is the main downside risk.

### Gaps
- No lender or broker published a worked example sized exactly at $500K EBITDA or $500K equity.
- The SBA guarantee fee, closing-cost percentages and actual market spreads (versus the prime + 3% cap) were not sourced.

## What the evidence says

- **Without a loan**, $500K of cash buys a business priced at about $500K. At 2.0-2.8x SDE that means roughly $180-250K of claimed SDE, about what the Miami wholesale bakery listings show (for example $449K asking for $137K claimed CF, or $750K for $320K). That is no better than the $50-250K expected from building a shop from scratch, though it starts with a track record.
- **With an SBA 7(a) loan** (10% minimum equity; about 10% interest; 10-year term; 1.25x historical coverage under SOP 50 10 8.1 from October 1, 2026), $500K of equity can support a purchase of about **$1.75-2.5M**. That is enough for a business with about $500K EBITDA (about $600-650K SDE) at 2.8-4.5x, with $100-250K left as a reserve.
- **What the owner keeps:** after $260-345K a year of debt service, about **$155-240K a year pre-tax in years 1-10**, before capital spending and tax. That rises to about $500K after the loan is repaid. A 20% earnings drop could cut that to under $100K in the more leveraged structures.
- **Availability is the binding constraint:** only a handful of South Florida food businesses claim $400-800K of cash flow at under $3M. Several of those claims look inflated (e.g. a 67% margin). Food businesses also carry higher SBA charge-off rates (restaurants 5-9%) than the acquisition average.
- **Relative to building from scratch**, acquisition is easier to finance and defaults less often than start-up lending. It also delivers the $500K earnings level immediately, but the owner pockets only about 30-50% of it for the first decade.
