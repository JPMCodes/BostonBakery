Research date: November 25, 2026. About 36 web searches.

**Method note:** This environment blocked every page fetch. Attempts to reach the FDD PDF host (pub-...r2.dev) and restaurantbusinessonline.com both failed with DNS errors, so I did not retry any fetch. **Every finding below comes from search-result text, meaning snippets and search-engine summaries of the linked pages, not from reading the documents themselves.** Figures in this file should be checked against the source documents before anyone relies on them.

**Source labels used below:**
- **[AGG-LOW]** marks a franchise-data aggregator (sharpsheets, franchisechatter, fdd.ninja, franchiseinvestordata, lexpress-franchise, franchiseoverview, zeereport, peersense, pulserevops, franzy, franchisevs, franchisepayback, BizBuySell/BizQuest franchise profile pages). ## 1. Franchise systems that publish store-level sales and profit (2025/2026 Item 19)

### Takeaway
Only a few of the listed brands publish a store-level profit line:
- **Nothing Bundt Cakes** publishes four-wall EBITDA of about $317K, or 21.6% of sales (2024 data).
- **Crumbl** published average profit of about $252K and median profit of about $77K for 2024, then dropped profit figures from its 2026 FDD.
- **Shipley Do-Nuts** markets "20% EBITDA" on a $1.2M AUV for its top 50% of stores.

Paris Baguette, Dunkin', Einstein, Cinnabon/Auntie Anne's, Duck Donuts, Tous les Jours and Kolache Factory publish sales only, or sales plus some cost lines. PopUp Bagels, Insomnia, Vicky Bakery, Pinecrest Bakery and Sergio's have no usable Item 19 that I could find.

On the evidence, only two brands reach $250K+ EBITDA per store:
- **Nothing Bundt Cakes**, on average, before royalty.
- **Crumbl**, on average for 2024 only; the median was far below.

Paris Baguette probably also clears $250K on its roughly $3M sales, but it publishes no profit figure.

### Inferences (mine)
- **NBC four-wall EBITDA likely excludes the 6% royalty.** Franchise Chatter's line items list "advertising" but no royalty line. On that reading, NBC's average after-royalty store profit is about $316.6K − (6% × ~$1.47M ≈ $88K) ≈ **$228K**. If royalty is already included, it is about $317K. Payroll probably includes a store manager, but I could not verify this.
- **Crumbl's average profit is skewed by top stores.** The 2024 average of $252K sat against a median of $77K, and 2025 AUV fell 16% with profit no longer disclosed. A typical Crumbl store today is probably well below $250K.
- **Shipley's 20% EBITDA on $1.2M is about $240K,** but only for the top half of stores. On the ~$830K average it would be about $166K, if the margin held.
- **Paris Baguette** at about $3M AUV would need only an ~8–10% after-royalty margin to clear $250K. This is plausible, but no margin is disclosed.
- **Brands that clear $250K per store:** NBC (average, before royalty) and Crumbl (2024 average only). Paris Baguette probably does. Shipley's top half does only marginally.

### Gaps

## 2. Multi-unit math: stores needed for $500K EBITDA after royalty and manager

### Takeaway
On disclosed averages, NBC needs about **2–3 stores** to reach $500K EBITDA, costing roughly **$1.3M–$3.1M** to build new. Crumbl or Shipley would need **3–4+ stores**, given their weak medians. Both paths need debt (SBA or landlord tenant improvements, TI) on top of $500K cash, and roughly 1.5–3 years to open several units.

The only published examples of 2–3 unit franchise profit are broker listings, which show cash flow of $338K–$820K for 2–3 Crumbl stores in Florida.

### Cited Findings
- **NBC per-store inputs:** four-wall EBITDA $316,591 (21.6%, FY2024); investment $667K–$1.03M; 6% royalty. — see Q1 sources ([Franchise Chatter](https://www.franchisechatter.com/2025/12/17/nothing-bundt-cakes-franchise-review-2025-costs-fees-news-average-revenues-and-or-profits/) [AGG-LOW]; [NBC](https://www.nothingbundtcakes.com/franchise-opportunities/))
- **Crumbl 2-unit, Pinellas County** [LISTING]: asking $950,000; about $338.5K cash flow on about $1.85M revenue; 27 employees. — [BizQuest](https://www.bizquest.com/business-for-sale/two-location-crumbl-franchise-in-florida/BW2522403/)
- **Crumbl 3-unit, Tampa Bay / Central FL** [LISTING]: asking $1.85M; $819,826 cash flow on about $3.72M revenue (about $273K cash flow per store). — [Sunbelt](https://www.sunbeltnetwork.com/miami-fl/buy-a-business/listings/listing-details/crumbl-franchise-tampa-bay-59459/); [BizQuest](https://www.bizquest.com/business-for-sale/crumbl-franchise-portfolio-three-locations-tampa-bay-central-fl/BW2524666/)
- **Crumbl 2 stores, FL Panhandle and AL** [LISTING]: asking $1.5M; about $461K cash flow; semi-absentee. — [Sunbelt](https://www.sunbeltnetwork.com/miami-fl/buy-a-business/listings/listing-details/high-performing-crumbl-stores-alabama-florida-57664/)
- **Crumbl 2 stores, Sarasota County area** [LISTING]: asking $1.65M; about $446K cash flow; includes rights to another site. — [LoopNet](https://loopnet.ca/biz/Business-Opportunity/high-volume-crumbl-sarasota-county-fl-rights-to-an-2nd-a-location/2283840)
- **Unbranded multi-unit franchise package, Broward** [LISTING]: asking $1.7M; $500K cash flow. The brand was unconfirmed and may not be bakery. — [LoopNet](https://loopnet.ca/biz/Business-Opportunity/3-profitable-multi-unit-franchise-opportunity-in-prime-florida-markets/2294261)

### Inferences (mine)
- **NBC math (2–3 stores):**
  - After a 6% royalty, average store profit is about $228K. Three stores give about $685K.
  - A $90–100K multi-unit/area manager brings that to about **$590K**, so 3 stores clear $500K.
  - Two stores (about $456K before an area manager) fall just short.
  - Build cost for 3 new stores is about $2.0–3.1M, so $500K cash covers only about 16–25% equity. Expect SBA 7(a) loans; at the $5M limit, that probably means one loan per entity or phased openings.
  - Debt service on about $2M at roughly 10–11% over 10 years is about $320K a year, so pre-debt EBITDA would be largely consumed for years.
- **Buying existing Crumbl units is the cheapest documented capital-per-dollar-of-cash-flow** (about 2.3–3.7x cash flow).
  - The 3-unit Tampa package at $1.85M for about $820K cash flow would net about $650–700K after a GM/area manager, if the figures hold.
  - The risk is Crumbl's 16% AUV decline in 2025: buyers may be acquiring trailing peak earnings.
- **Timing:** NBC/Crumbl build-outs typically take 9–18 months per unit (general franchise norm, not sourced here). A 3-unit build would take about 2–3 years; buying existing units could close in 3–6 months.

### Gaps
- No published franchisee P&L for a 2–5 unit NBC, Paris Baguette or Dunkin' operator was found.
- No sourced build timelines were found.
- No SBA terms were sourced.
- Listing cash-flow definitions (SDE vs. EBITDA) are unverified.

- **Dessert/bakery franchise units:** about **1.5–3.7x seller cash flow**.

## 5. Wholesale and production bakeries: margins, multiples, shipping

### Takeaway
Published, audited EBITDA benchmarks for a $2–5M regional wholesale bakery were not found.

- **Advisory multiples (2026):**
  - About 4–6x EBITDA for pure wholesale operators with $1M+ EBITDA.
  - About 3–4.5x for hybrid retail-wholesale bakeries.
  - About 5–7x for regional commissary platforms.
- **Small-bakery deals:** close around 2.2–2.8x SDE.
- **Listing margins:** wholesale listings claim adjusted EBITDA margins of about 20–30%.

## What the evidence says: cheapest and fastest documented paths to $500K EBITDA

1. **Buy an existing multi-unit dessert franchise package (fastest, and the lowest price per dollar of cash flow).**
   - The 3-unit Crumbl Tampa/Central FL package asks $1.85M on about $820K seller cash flow from about $3.72M sales. After a GM/area manager, that is roughly $650–700K (my estimate).
   - Two-unit Crumbl packages ask $1.5–1.65M on $446–461K cash flow.
   - Capital: about $1.5–1.9M, i.e. $500K cash plus SBA debt. Timing: months, not years.
   - Main risk: Crumbl's 2025 AUV fell 16% to $1.14M and profit disclosure stopped, so trailing cash flow may overstate future earnings. [LISTING data]
2. **Buy an established Miami multi-site independent bakery with wholesale.**
   - The 1972 two-bakery-plus-wholesale Miami listing reportedly asks $2.2M on about $580K cash flow (about 3.8x). That is roughly $500K after a manager, if verified.
   - Capital: about $2.2M. It is the only local listing found that reaches the target on its own.
   - The cash-flow figure could not be tied to the listing with certainty.
3. **Build 3 Nothing Bundt Cakes bakeries (best-documented unit economics, slowest).**
   - About $1.47M average sales and about $317K four-wall EBITDA (21.6%) per bakery, about $228K after a 6% royalty if four-wall EBITDA is pre-royalty. Three bakeries give about $590K after an area manager.
   - Capital: about $2.0–3.1M ($667K–$1.03M each). Timing: probably 2–3 years.
4. **Combination path.**
   - Buy the about-$1.1M Miami ice cream/dessert brand (SDE $311–326K: trucks, 2 storefronts, commissary), then add units or wholesale.
   - Or buy one high-volume franchise unit (e.g., Palm Beach Gardens Crumbl at $299K for $194K cash flow) and add a second.
   - Capital: about $1.1–1.5M combined for about $450–500K SDE, before a manager.
5. **Not supported by evidence as a fast path:**
   - Organic independent brand-building: The Salty reached about $21M in revenue over about 22 shops in roughly a decade; Night Owl reached about $1.5M in sales after about 5 years.
   - A single wholesale or commissary plant at $2–5M sales: no audited margin data found.

**Bottom line:** No documented path reaches $500K EBITDA with $500K cash alone. Every path requires about $1.5–3M of total capital, so a 3–6x leverage on the cash. Acquiring existing multi-unit cash flow at about 2.3–3.8x is the cheapest and fastest. Building franchise units has the most reliable unit data but costs more and takes 2–3 years.
