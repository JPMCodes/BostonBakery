How to read these notes:
- **[S]** means I saw only the search-result text. The one page fetch I tried (1851franchise.com) failed: the host is outside this session's allowed network domains. Per instructions I did not retry or work around it, so **every figure below comes from search-result text**. The search summaries sometimes merge results; where a figure could not be tied to one URL, it says "attribution unclear".
- No FDD (franchise disclosure document) was opened directly. All Item 19 (sales and profit) and Item 7 (initial investment) figures come from third-party readings: Franchise Chatter, Franzy, Sharpsheets, VettedBiz, BizBuySell franchise pages, Lexpress, fdd.ninja, partial Item 19 PDFs, or trade press.
- "FDD year" and "data year" differ. A 2026 FDD normally reports fiscal 2025. Each row names the data year where the source gave it.
- **Reliability grades used in tables:**
  - **A:** trade press quoting company or FDD data (Restaurant Business, Franchise Times, QSR, NRN), or a statute or state agency.
  - **B:** a franchise-data aggregator citing a named FDD Item and year (Franchise Chatter, Franzy, 1851, Lexpress, BizBuySell franchise page).
  - **C:** an aggregator with no FDD year, a company marketing claim, or a vendor blog.
  - **D:** modelled estimates (Pulse RevOps, directory revenue estimates, ZoomInfo, Prospeo). Pulse RevOps pages carry "2027" framing and unsourced numbers; treat them as unreliable.
- "My arithmetic" marks calculations from cited figures.
- Not repeated from `Miami bakery business plan/competitors_benchmarks.md`: Nothing Bundt Cakes FY2021–FY2023 (incl. the $1,479,065 / 22.1% FY2023 figures), Crumbl 2022–2025 volumes and profit, Cinnaholic's $375,100 average and its earlier investment ranges, Paris Baguette's $3.05M average.

## 1. Celebration-cake and gift bakeries (Nothing Bundt Cakes, custom cake studios, cupcake shops)

### Takeaway
- **Nothing Bundt Cakes is still the strongest franchised celebration format.** The FY2025 average (2026 FDD) is about **$1,477,163 across 545 bakeries**, flat on FY2024's about $1.48M. The median is about **$1.336M**. Cost of goods runs about **23% of sales**.
  - The FY2024 four-wall EBITDA average was not visible in any search text. Press puts it at "more than 20%" (about $350k).
  - 2024 openings averaged only about $1.02M in their first full year.
- **Cupcake shops are a weak, shrinking category.**
  - Gigi's last reliable AUV (average unit volume) was about $400k (2013), and its unit count fell.
  - Smallcakes has no verified Item 19.
  - Sprinkles closed all company-owned bakeries and 25 cupcake ATMs around the turn of 2025–26, with no reason given.
- **Custom cake studios:** no unit-economics data was found.

- **Novelty cupcakes have failed twice at national scale** (Crumbs in 2014, Sprinkles in 2025–26). Gigi's peaked at about $400k AUV. A cupcake-led storefront should be benchmarked at $300–500k with low confidence.

## 5. Bagel shops (PopUp, Einstein, Bruegger's, H&H, Big Apple, Call Your Mother, independents)

### Takeaway
- **PopUp Bagels (2025 FDD):** franchised AUV of **$1.28M with a drive-thru** and **$984,569 without**. Investment is **$312,600–$884,150**. It was valued at $300M (Tiger Global round) and targets 100 stores by end-2027.
- **Einstein franchise:** about $1.08M average (2024 FDD, 51 units).
- **H&H Bagels:** reports about $1.9–2.8M.
- **Big Apple Bagels:** the last reliable AUV is the 2010 Technomic estimate of $380k.

## 9. Wholesale and commissary bakeries

### Takeaway
- **Price:** wholesale to cafes, restaurants and grocers usually runs **50–60% of retail** (keystone, so the buyer can double it), or about **1.7–2.5× fully loaded cost**.
- **Gross margin:** about **35–50%** on wholesale, against **50–65%** on retail.
- **No US data was found** on sales per employee or minimum efficient size.

### Cited Findings
- **Price as a share of retail:**
  - "Typically 50% to 60% of retail" — search summary [S; attribution unclear between Metrobi and Toast].
  - Toast cites University of Nebraska researchers on keystone pricing, where retail is double wholesale — [Toast](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/on-the-line/wholesale-vs-retail-price) [S].
- **Cost multiples and margins:** wholesale is "2–2.5 times the total product cost", retail "3–4 times". Retail gross margins are 50–65%, wholesale 35–50% — [Dojo Business](https://dojobusiness.com/blogs/news/bakery-food-cost) [S; C grade].
- **Target margin:** at least 40% gross margin on wholesale, about 1.67× fully loaded cost — [Metrobi](https://metrobi.com/?p=61219) [S; C grade].
- **European example:** restaurant deliveries earn a 35–45% margin against 60–70% for shop sales — [Kitchen NMBRS](https://kitchennmbrs.app/en/knowledge-base/specific-kitchen-types-concepts/how-do-i-calculate-the-margin-on-a-bakery-that-also-supplies-bread-to-other-restaurants) [S; non-US].
- **Channel mix:**
  - A Nebraska bakery-cafe gets 18% of gross revenue from wholesale accounts — [Bake, "The wholesale advantage"](https://bakemag.com/articles/364-the-wholesale-advantage) [S; date not shown].
  - A "2027 playbook" estimate of 22–32% wholesale share at 32–42% gross margin — [Pulse RevOps](https://pulserevops.com/knowledge/gp0192) [S; D grade].

### Inferences
- **Wholesale gives up about 15–20 points of gross margin** against retail. It is worth it mainly to fill oven and labor capacity in off-peak hours. Delivery cost must be loaded in.

### Gaps
- US wholesale bakery sales per employee; minimum efficient size; actual audited margins. Census (NAICS 311811) data was not reached.

## 13. Cottage-food and home bakeries (Florida law)

### Takeaway
- **Cap:** Florida (s. 500.80, as amended by the 2021 "Home Sweet Home Act") caps cottage-food gross sales at **$250,000 a year**, across all products and channels.
- **How it may be sold:**
  - Sales directly to consumers, including **online and mail order**.
  - Delivery in person, to an event venue, or by USPS or commercial carrier.
  - **No wholesale, consignment, restaurant or grocery sales.**
  - Products must be prepackaged and labelled with the cottage-food statement, net weight and allergens.
- **Typical revenues:** no source found.

### Cited Findings
- **Sales cap:** annual gross sales "include all sales of cottage food products, regardless of product type, sales location, sales platform, or the number of individuals involved"; records may be requested by FDACS — [FDACS Cottage Food Operations](https://ccmedia.fdacs.gov/content/download/70108/file/Cottage-Food-Operations.pdf), [Fla. Stat. 500.80 (2026)](https://m.flsenate.gov/statutes/500.80) [S; A grade].
- **Online sales and delivery:** operations may sell, offer for sale and accept payment over the internet or by mail order. Products may be delivered in person, to an event venue, or by USPS or commercial mail delivery — [National Agricultural Law Center](https://nationalaglawcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/assets/cottagefood/Florida.pdf), [UF/IFAS 2021 update](https://blogs.ifas.ufl.edu/leeco/2022/08/25/cottage-food-operations-home-sweet-home-act-2021-update/) [S].
- **Labels:** must show net weight or volume, allergens per federal rules, and that the product was made in a cottage food operation not subject to Florida's food safety regulations — [UF/IFAS FS425](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/FS425) [S].
- **Prohibited channels:** no sales to restaurants or grocery stores; no consignment or wholesale — [UF/IFAS IN918](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/IN918/pdf) [S].
- **Typical cottage-bakery revenue:** not found.

### Inferences
- **Cottage food fits a Florida test phase:** markets, pre-orders and shipping up to $250k. It **cannot supply cafés or grocers**, so wholesale needs a licensed kitchen.
- **Products:** non-potentially-hazardous items only. Cream-cheese frosting would likely fall outside cottage food; verify with FDACS.

### Gaps
- The exact list of allowed and prohibited items (e.g. cream-cheese frosting) was not captured in search text.
- Typical revenues.

## 15. Buying instead of building, and survival rates

### Takeaway
- **What bakeries sell for (BizBuySell):**
  - Median **asking** price about **$249k–300k**.
  - Median **owner earnings** about **$104.5k**.
  - Sold deals at a median of about **2.2–2.4× earnings**; listings at about 2.66–2.77×.
  - Median sale price "settling at $200k in 2025".
- **Survival:** independent full-service restaurants fail about **17% in year one**; about **51–56% survive five years** (BLS-based). No bakery-specific rate was found.
