## 1. Consumer and restaurant climate (US and South Florida), fall 2026, and 2027 forecasts

### Takeaway
US chain-restaurant sales are growing only through price; **traffic keeps falling**. Black Box puts 2026 same-store sales at about **+1.7%**, mostly from higher checks. Chains' comparable traffic is **down ~21% since 2018** (reported Nov 2026). The split by income is the "K-shape": high-income spending is still growing while low-income spending declines. But by mid-2026 even middle- and upper-income diners had begun to trim. Diners aged 25–34 are cutting back most. Forecasts for 2027 call for a slight recovery: Technomic sees **+1.3% real growth** in North America and **continued unit contraction**. No South Florida traffic series was found.

### Inferences
- **Price-led growth with fewer visits** means each customer visit has to carry a higher ticket. That favours products bought for an occasion or as a gift (boxes, cakes, holiday items) over a cheap daily impulse treat.
- **Who is still spending:** high-income and older (65+) households are the most resilient; 25–34-year-olds are cutting back. A bakery near affluent, older-skewing suburbs (see s. 7) is better placed than one that depends on young trend-chasers. That is also the Crumbl customer whose visits are collapsing (s. 3).
- **2027 is forecast to be a little better** (Technomic, NRA), but chains are still shrinking in unit count. Fewer competing units plus more vacated food space is mildly positive for a new independent.

## 2. GLP-1 drugs: how many people use them, measured effects on sweets and restaurants, and how operators are responding

### Takeaway
About **one in eight US adults is currently taking a GLP-1**: KFF found 12% (March 2026) and Gallup found **11% for weight loss** (July 2026, up from 3% two years earlier). About **23% of US households** have a user (Circana, Nov 2025). Cheap pills widen access: the **Wegovy pill launched January 2026 at $149/month cash** for starter doses.

The best-measured effect on sweets is from Cornell–Numerator transaction data (2025): households cut grocery spending about **5.5%** within six months, with **sweet bakery down ~7–9%** and **cookies −6.7%**. Survey evidence on restaurant visits conflicts:
- NRA (March 2026): users still buy from restaurants more often than non-users, though nearly half say they have cut back.
- William Blair (2026): users' monthly restaurant visits **halved**.

Industry evidence says **indulgence shifts rather than disappears**: toward small, naturally portioned, premium treats and toward protein and fibre. Hershey says chocolate holds up because it comes in small portions. Greggs and Bimbo have launched smaller portions and protein lines.

No data were found for Florida or Miami, or by sex. Income and age splits were not visible in the excerpts.

### Inferences
- **Size of the drag.** With ~12% of adults using and sweet bakery down ~7–9% in user households, a back-of-envelope **category-wide drag is roughly 1–2% of sweet-bakery spending a year** [my arithmetic: ~23% of households × ~8%]. It grows as pills spread. It is real but not existential. A **novelty, large-format sweets concept** (giant cookies, oversized cinnamon rolls eaten as a solo snack) is the most exposed.
- **What the moment favours:**
  - small, premium, giftable portions (minis, bites, half-dozen boxes of small rolls);
  - something to share at an occasion (whole cakes, roscón, office boxes), since GLP-1 users still go to gatherings;
  - one or two savoury or protein-forward items (pan de bono-type cheese breads, savoury pastries, bread);
  - quality cues that justify trading up.
- **Customer mix [inference].** GLP-1 use skews toward women and the 50–64 age group, and cash pills widen access to middle incomes [BACKGROUND, unverified this session]. A suburban, affluent, older customer base, otherwise attractive, may have **above-average GLP-1 penetration**. Plan portions accordingly.

**2026 is a cheap year for the two biggest bakery inputs, butter and eggs.**
- **CME butter averaged $1.57/lb in July 2026, −38% y/y.** It was ~$2.50 in early 2025 and $1.43 in Nov 2025. US production is at records.
- **Retail eggs are forecast −29% for 2026** (−20.6% y/y in Sept).

**Cocoa futures collapsed** from >$6,000/t (Dec 31, 2025) to ~$3,100 (Mar 2026), but chocolate prices to bakers lag because of hedging.

**Coffee:** arabica futures fell ~23% y/y by March 2026 after the US exempted Brazilian food from the 40% tariff (Nov 2025), yet **retail coffee is still +7.1% y/y (Sept 2026)**.

**Tariffs:** the main pressure is on **equipment and packaging** (Section 232 steel and aluminium; aluminium pans; EU chocolate ≥15%), not on most ingredients.

### Inferences
- **Tourism is a weak lever for a suburban bakery.** The World Cup lifted prices more than visitor numbers, and benefits clustered downtown and on the Beach. Plan on **resident demand**.
- **The southern suburbs are the right demand pocket for the moment:**
  - incomes 2–3× the county;
  - high home equity;
  - an older skew, matching the resilient 65+ segment (s. 1);
  - less exposure to immigration-enforcement fear.
- **But costs squeeze even affluent households** (insurance, HOA, property tax, private schools). Affluent does not mean price-insensitive. They will pay for **quality and occasions**, and are less likely to buy daily novelty treats.
- **Overall verdict for the report writer [inference]:**
  - **Cost side favourable:** cheap butter and eggs, easing cocoa and coffee, and more second-generation space coming free.
  - **Demand side flat to soft:** traffic falling, population shrinking, GLP-1 drag growing.
  - **Labor side tight:** immigration shocks plus the $15 minimum.
  - **The moment favours** a small-footprint, production-led bakery in the affluent south suburbs selling **giftable, occasion and everyday-staple items** (whole cakes and boxes, breads, small or mini sweet portions, a savoury or protein option, plus coffee), with a lean cross-trained team.
  - **It disfavours** a large-format, single-item novelty sweets shop (the Crumbl, Chip City, Sprinkles model) or a labor-heavy seated café.
