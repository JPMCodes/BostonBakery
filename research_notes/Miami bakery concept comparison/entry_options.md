## 1. Operating bakeries, cafés, cafeterias and wholesale bakeries for sale (late 2026); multiples; pitfalls

### Takeaway
Few new bakeries for sale were found in the target towns (Coral Gables, South Miami, Pinecrest, Palmetto Bay, Kendall, Westchester, Coconut Grove). Two listings stand out:
- **A relocatable Miami-Dade wholesale bakery** at **$129,000**. It claims $373,488 in sales and $114,811 cash flow, and pays $3,990 a month for about 1,000 sq ft of warehouse space.
- **A larger wholesale bakery and distributor (est. 2009)** at **$449,000**. It claims $491,250 in sales and $137,377 cash flow, and pays $3,600 a month for about 5,000 sq ft.

The wholesale bakeries priced at 1–3× earnings are the most direct way in. Retail bakery-cafés found in or near the target towns were either over budget (a 50-year South Miami café-bakery at about $599K) or thin earners (an Argentine bakery at $120–200K on about $15K a month in sales).

Florida bakeries sell at about **2–3× SDE** (seller's discretionary earnings), with a median sale price of **about $200K**. Restaurants in Q3 2026 sold at an average **2.39×** cash flow, and sellers got a record 94.2% of their asking price.

**Pitfalls (with sources)**

- **Permits do not transfer** [R]: "Permits are not transferrable from owner to owner or from one location to another, with the exception of mobile vendors… A new food permit application, inspection, and documentation to meet the requirements for approved water source and wastewater (sewage) disposal are required if you incur an ownership or location change." Source: [FDACS Mobile Food Establishments page](https://www.fdacs.gov/Business-Services/Food-Establishments/Mobile-Food-Vendors).

- **Buyer can inherit the seller's unpaid Florida taxes** [SRT]
  - Under Fla. Stat. 213.758, a buyer can be liable for the seller's unpaid DOR-administered taxes, including sales tax. Liability is capped at the fair market value of what was transferred or the purchase price paid; a 2012 analysis says the greater of the two.
  - Either party can ask DOR for an audit, for a possible fee.
  - Sources: [Fla. Stat. 213.758](https://m.flsenate.gov/Statutes/213.758); [2012 House analysis](https://flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2012/103/Analyses/h0103z1.CVJS.PDF).

- **The same business at several prices** [SRT]
  - The $449K wholesale bakery is also seen at $399K and $499K; the 50-year café at $160K–$949K; the Argentine bakery at $120–200K. Ask the broker for the price history before offering.
  - Sources: as cited for each listing above.

- **Customer concentration**: the Flowers route lost its main account (Winn-Dixie #0214, closed August 2026) while still listed for sale [SRT]. Source: [Negocios en Florida](https://negociosenflorida.com/en/listings/ruta-de-pan-flowers-bakery-miami-dade-en-venta/).

### Inferences
- **Wholesale bakery #1844547** has the strongest claimed return. At $129K for $114.8K cash flow (calc 1.12×), it is under half the market multiple. That suggests cash flow is overstated, includes the owner's own labour, or depends on a few accounts. Its rent of $3,990/mo is about 12.8% of sales (calc).
- **Wholesale bakery #1904322** is priced at 3.27× (calc), at the top of the range. Its rent is very low: $3,600/mo for 5,000 sq ft, calc ≈ $8.64/sq ft/yr. Both fit inside $500K with $50–350K left over for working capital.
- **The 50-year South Miami café** at $599K is over budget. At its $165.6K cash flow it would be 3.6× (calc). Without a loan it only works if the price falls to about $400K or below.
- **The Argentine bakery** sells about $180K a year (calc), which can support little more than one owner's wage.
- **Seller financing** cannot be relied on to stretch the budget, because no figures were found. With a record 94% sale-to-ask ratio, buyers have less room to negotiate in Q3 2026.
- **Diligence checklist (general practice, not sourced here):** check bank deposits and tax returns against the claimed cash flow, since Miami cafeteria sales are cash-heavy; check the lease term, the assignment clause and the renewal rent; ask whether the cash flow includes the owner working 60+ hours a week.

- No rent, size or lease term was published for the Argentine bakery, the 50-year café, the Kendall 30-year bakery or the Craigslist ads. Only the two wholesale bakeries show rent.
- Listing dates and current status (most listing pages could not be read).
- The typical share of a Florida restaurant or bakery price that sellers finance: not found in this session.
- Unreported cash and owner hours: no Miami-specific source was found. The diligence points above are general practice.

## 3. Hosts, shop-in-shop and wholesale supply

### Takeaway
- **Milam's Markets** buys pastry from local bakeries: it names Sullivan St. Bakery (delivered every morning) and Domaselo sourdough, plus unnamed "local bakers and artisans".
- **Joanna's Marketplace** (8247 S Dixie Hwy) bakes its own bread and pastry, so it is a less likely buyer.
- **Whole Foods** brings in small local suppliers through its Supplier Portal and LEAP accelerator, and has a Florida "Local Partnerships" application form.
- **Who supplies Miami cafés:** wholesale bakeries such as La Provence (French viennoiserie), Tracy Bakery (since 1981) and Flowers. Some independents use named local bakeries: Emissary in Coconut Grove buys from Flour & Weirdoughs, an unnamed Gables café from Caracas Bakery, and Pamela Wasabi keeps a list of Gables and Grove stockists.
- **Prices:** no Miami wholesale price list was found. Frozen ready-to-bake croissants cost about $0.92–$1.31 each by the case (calc), against about $3.50 retail in South Miami. That sets the ceiling for a fresh wholesale croissant.
- **Wholesale conventions:** retail is usually about twice wholesale ("keystone"), so wholesale is about 50% of retail. Delivery minimums run from $50 an order to $160 an order with five drops a week.

## 4. Mobile selling: licences, where trucks can trade, trailer cost, event pay

### Takeaway
- **Which agency:** a trailer selling **coffee and pastry** that are not temperature-controlled for safety falls under **FDACS**, not DBPR. FDACS's own page names coffee, tea and "pastry products".
- **Commissary:** every FDACS mobile unit except prepackaged-only ones needs a **Commissary Letter of Agreement**. A business that already holds an FDACS permit for a brick-and-mortar kitchen can get **one mobile permit free**, and mobile permits are the only food permits that transfer to a new owner.
- **DBPR:** a vehicle that prepares potentially hazardous food (dairy desserts, hot sandwiches) needs a DBPR Mobile Food Dispensing Vehicle (MFDV) licence. A blog puts the fee at about $397 a year.
- **Local rules:** Florida law (§509.102) stops cities and counties requiring their own licence for a mobile food vehicle, but they still control where it can operate through zoning.
- **Coral Gables:** the city's own FAQ says **"Food trucks are not permitted"**. Pinecrest and South Miami rules were not found.
- **Trailer cost:** fitted food trailers in Miami list at about **$20K–$75K**; a turnkey coffee trailer in Islamorada was $48K.
- **Event pay:** Miami coffee carts charge **$650–$1,050** for 2–3 hours ($200–$250 an hour). Dessert and churro trucks start at about **$500–$800** an event.

### Cited Findings
- **FDACS mobile page** [R, read Nov 19, 2026] — [FDACS Mobile Food Establishments](https://www.fdacs.gov/Business-Services/Food-Establishments/Mobile-Food-Vendors):
  - Scope: FDACS "regulates mobile food establishments that sell only prepackaged foods or non-potentially hazardous food items, including: Foods that are not temperature controlled for safety, including ice confections, coffee, tea, soft drinks, pastry products, popcorn and candies… Note: Mobile vendors with food service are permitted by the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR)."
  - Commissary: "If you are selling only prepackaged non-potentially hazardous food (cookies, crackers, potato chips, etc.)… a commissary may not be required. All other mobile food establishments must complete a Commissary Letter of Agreement."
  - Free permit: "Food establishments with an active permit for preparing or processing foods at a brick-and-mortar facility (such as a commissary) are currently allowed to have one free mobile food permit."
  - Process: apply 21 days before opening; an inspector makes contact within 3–5 business days and inspects within about two weeks; renewal is annual, with a late fee of up to $100.
  - Transfer: permits "are not transferrable… with the exception of mobile vendors who do not operate at a fixed location".
  - DBPR's remit: units that "process or prepare potentially hazardous foods… within the mobile unit should contact DBPR at (850) 487-1395". Units on DOH-licensed institutional property (schools, universities) go to DOH.
- **DBPR MFDV** [SRT; secondary blogs]:
  - Licence about "$50 to apply plus $347 for a full year" (≈ $397).
  - Commissary: under rule 61C-4.0161 a commissary is needed only if the vehicle lacks its own sinks, refrigeration, power, water and waste tanks. Other blogs say all MFDVs need one; the sources conflict.
  - §509.102 bars local governments from requiring a separate licence or fee to operate an MFDV, though a local business tax receipt may still apply.
  - Sources: [runpitstop MFDV fee](https://runpitstop.com/blog/food-truck-permits-florida); [runpitstop commissary](https://runpitstop.com/blog/food-truck-commissary-florida); [Food Truck Guides FL](https://foodtruckguides.com/florida/licenses-permits).
- **Where trucks can trade** [SRT]:

### Inferences
- **Licence cost:** a coffee-and-pastry trailer stocked from the bakery's own FDACS-permitted kitchen could run on a **free** second FDACS mobile permit. Milk-based espresso drinks may count as handling potentially hazardous food and push it into DBPR; confirm with FDACS before choosing equipment.
- **Coral Gables** rules out street or lot trading. Private events (homes, offices, the UM markets via their operators) are the realistic route. Pinecrest and South Miami need a call to each zoning office.
- **Revenue (illustrative):** 2–4 bookings a week at $700–$1,000 each is about $1.4K–$4K a week, or about $70K–$200K a year at 50 weeks (calc). No source gave actual bookings per week.
- **Capital:** a $20–50K trailer plus about $10–20K of espresso equipment is within budget. The constraint is event volume, not capital.
