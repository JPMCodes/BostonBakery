## 4. Rules and fees: DBPR licence, county grease permit, change of owner, impact fees and water-and-sewer charges, sales tax, town rules

### Takeaway
- **State licence:** a burger restaurant needs a DBPR **permanent food service licence**:
  - "With seating": **$262/yr for 1–49 seats**. "Non-seating": $242/yr.
  - Plus a **$50 application fee**.
  - **Plan review is free** and has been since May 2017.
  - **Change of owner:** no plan review is needed if nothing changes. No inspection is needed if the previous owner passed one within 120 days.
- **County grease permit:** the Miami-Dade **FOG (grease) operating permit** is annual (calendar year) and **non-transferable**. A new owner needs a new permit, and the city's certificate of use or occupational licence comes first.
- **Impact fees (the big cost risk):** if the space was retail, a change to "**fast-food restaurant without drive-thru**" carries a county mobility fee of **$79–91 per sq ft**, against $6.5–7.4 for a strip-plaza retail bay. That is a net of about **$109K–$125K for 1,500 sq ft** (calc). "**Fast casual**" is $17–20 per sq ft, a net of about $16K–$18K (calc). If the space was already a restaurant of the same category, the net is about zero.
- **Water-and-sewer connection charges (WASD):** $1.39 + $5.60 per gallon per day. Retail is rated at 10 gpd/100 sq ft and fast food at 65 gpd/100 sq ft. A retail-to-fast-food change at 1,500 sq ft costs about **$5.8K** (calc).
- **Sales tax:** 7% on prepared food (6% state + 1% county).
- **Town rules:**
  - **Pinecrest:** its US-1 alternative district lists "Restaurants, Without Drive-Through". Outdoor dining rules were rewritten in 2024.
  - **Coral Gables:** sidewalk cafés pay a $250 application fee plus $15 per sq ft.
  - **South Miami:** has an outdoor-seating permit and agreement.
  - Hours rules were not found for any of the three.

### Cited Findings

**State licence (DBPR Division of Hotels and Restaurants)** [SRT; myfloridalicense.com was refused by the proxy in the earlier pass and was not retried]

### Inferences
- **Classification decides the impact fee.** A small order-at-counter burger shop could be classed by the county as "fast-food" ($79–91/sq ft) or as "fast casual" ($17–20/sq ft). For a **retail-to-restaurant change at 1,500 sq ft** (calc; Zone 2 to Zone 4, net of strip-plaza credit, before the 2% fee):
  - **Fast-food:** net ≈ **$109,458–$125,390**.
  - **Fast casual:** net ≈ **$15,989–$18,315**.
  - **Sit-down high-turnover:** ≈ $43.9K–$50.3K (calc).
  - Taking a space that was **already a restaurant** avoids most or all of this. It also avoids the sewer capacity step, unless seats are added.
  - Which context zone covers Coral Gables, South Miami, Pinecrest and Kendall was not determined.
- **Water and sewer, retail to restaurant at 1,500 sq ft** (calc):
  - Fast food: 975 gpd − 150 gpd = 825 gpd × $6.99 ≈ **$5,767**.
  - Take-out or full service: 1,350 gpd × $6.99 ≈ **$9,437**.
  - An existing restaurant of the same type: about $0.
- **On a business purchase, the buyer pays new:** the DBPR licence (about $312 for the first year, calc), the FOG permit (about $169–$200 plus any operator fee), the city business tax receipt and certificate of use (earlier notes), and county sales-tax registration. Plan review is skipped only if nothing in the kitchen changes. Swapping a pizza oven for a griddle and fryers is a change that probably needs DBPR plan review and a hood or suppression review (inference).
- **Drive-up window:** Pinecrest's US-1 alternative district appears to exclude it. In Coral Gables it would at least be a hearing-level conditional use if allowed at all (inference). A walk-up "ventanita" counter needs separate checking.

## 5. Cost and time to fix or add a hood, fire suppression, grease trap, griddle and fryers; permit times

### Takeaway
- **Hood:**
  - **Type I hood with duct, fan and make-up air:** about **$15K–$50K** installed (national guides).
  - **Wet-chemical suppression:** about **$2K–$5.5K**.
- **Grease interceptor:** an in-ground unit of about 750 gal costs about **$15K–$40K+** in Miami. That cost is the main reason to buy a box that already has one.
- **Permit time:** Miami-Dade commercial permit review takes about **4–12 weeks**, and **8–16 weeks** including DBPR kitchen review. Lease signing to opening takes about **4–12 months**. All of these figures come from contractor blogs.
- **Griddle and fryer prices:** not searched in this pass.

### Cited Findings [SRT; mostly contractor and vendor blogs]
- **Hoods and suppression:**
  - Type I hood systems "$15,000–$50,000 installed"; Type I hood alone $3,000–$15,000+; Type II $1,500–$5,000; a 10 ft hood at $10–12K basic or $40–50K turnkey; wet-chemical suppression "$2,000 to $5,500" — [Safe and Sound 2026](https://getsafeandsound.com/blog/fire-suppression-system-cost/); [Restaurant Warehouse](https://therestaurantwarehouse.com/blogs/restaurant-equipment/commercial-kitchen-hood-installation-cost); [CountBricks](https://www.countbricks.com/post/commercial-vent-hood-installation-cost); [Approved Costs](https://approvedcosts.com/fire-suppression-system/).
  - One suppression price is dated March 2021.
- **Grease interceptors:**
  - Miami installers quote $5K–$30K+ and $15K–$40K+ for gravity interceptors; a guide gives $8K–$15K for 500–750 gal; a 750-gal polyethylene tank alone lists at $19,546 — [Caraballo](https://caraballoexpress.com/grease-trap-installation/); [Unclogme](https://unclogme.com/2026/03/19/complete-guide-to-miami-dade-grease-trap-compliance-for-restaurants-in-2026/); [Grease Trap Locator](https://greasetraplocator.com/grease-interceptor-cost/); [Drain-Net](https://drain-net.com/grease-traps/large-capacity-grease-traps/product/grease-interceptor-with-750-gallons-liquid-holding-capacity).
  - Miami-Dade commissioners passed a resolution in **February 2026** to study FOG rules because of cost burdens, with gravity systems that can exceed $40K — per [Grease Trap Locator](https://greasetraplocator.com/grease-interceptor-cost/) (secondary; the resolution itself was not seen).
- **Build-out cost:** Miami restaurant build-outs cost $150–250/sq ft all-in — [Atlantic Commercial Advisors](https://atlanticcommercialadvisors.com/blog/restaurants-for-lease-miami-dade-county-2026). A Miami 2026 build-out guide exists but its figures were not captured — [TM International Group](https://tmgroupdc.com/blog/restaurant-buildout-cost-in-miami-fl-2026-commercial-guide/).
- **Permit times:**
  - Commercial permits take "4 to 12 weeks", longer for restaurants because of fire, ADA and life-safety review — [IG Permit Expeditors 2026](https://igpermitexpeditors.com/building-permit-time-miami-dade-2026/).
  - County plan review takes 4–8 weeks — [Endless Life Design](https://www.endlesslifedesign.com/post/permit-application-timeline-plan-review-miami-dade-broward-palm-beach).
  - Permitting plus DBPR review takes 8–16 weeks; the critical path runs through the hood, suppression, grease interceptor, gas, make-up air and ADA restrooms; each correction cycle adds 1–2 weeks — [Pineland Engineering](https://pinelandengineering.com/blog/restaurant-permit-miami-dade-mep-engineering/); [Breadless franchise guide](https://franchise.eatbreadless.com/blog/miami-restaurant-franchise-investment-2026/).
  - Shell space takes 14–22 weeks from permit application to CO — same result set [SRT].
  - The county sewer capacity letter takes about 3–5 days [R-19] — [Sewer Capacity Certification](https://www.miamidade.gov/global/economy/building/sewer-capacity-certification.page).

### Inferences
- **Converting a box with an existing hood:** adding or upgrading suppression over a new griddle and fryers, re-certifying the hood, cleaning ducts and making minor plumbing changes would plausibly cost **$10K–$30K** and take **2–4 months** with permits (inference from the ranges above).
- **Adding a new hood and interceptor** to a retail bay would cost about **$30K–$90K** for those two items alone, before the impact fees in §4, and take **4–9 months** (inference).
- Together with §4, a first-generation retail bay could cost a burger shop **$50K–$215K** more than a box that is already a restaurant (calc from the low and high ends of the ranges).
