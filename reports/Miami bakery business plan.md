# Miami Bakery Business Plan: One Kitchen, Five Ways to Sell

The business that works in Miami is a roll kitchen, not a bakery shop. It is one small production kitchen with a walk-up window and no seats, selling the same rolls five ways: at the window four mornings a week, at farmers markets, to offices, through school fundraisers and on delivery apps. In the model it leaves you **$129,152 a year on $312,719 of cash at Dadeland Plaza, 19% of sales before your own pay**, $56,000 if sales run 20% low, and about $250,000 (25%) if the one kitchen is filled, which is a target and not an estimate. The same center as a Boston-style shop left $8,641. It starts as a pop-up that costs $83,453 and tests three of the five channels, and the demand for pre-orders, before you sign a lease.

*Prepared November 19, 2026. Model figures come from the repo's pro forma (`db/proforma.yaml`, formats `roll_popup` and `roll_kitchen`, Miami costs) and are reproduced in [proforma_results.md](../research_notes/Miami%20bakery%20business%20plan/proforma_results.md). They are Year-2 figures unless stated; "left for you" has no owner pay in the costs and no loan; low and high are 80% and 120% of estimated sales. Research facts link to their sources; most come from search-result text, because listing and many press sites refuse automated reads, and nothing has been confirmed with a broker, landlord or agency. No Miami bakery publishes its sales, so every volume here is an estimate. Judgment is marked as mine. Earlier work: [Miami roll bakery feasibility](Miami%20roll%20bakery%20feasibility.md) and [Miami roll house design](Miami%20roll%20house%20design.md), which this plan replaces.*

## 1. Why bakery margins are thin, and what fixes them

I took the earlier designs apart line by line. Four things decide the margin, and food isn't one of them.

| Food and packaging | $24.5 | $20.4 | $18.5 | $18.6 |
| Hourly crew, with payroll taxes | $43.3 | $34.0 | $29.2 | $27.7 |
| Rent and NNN | $13.2 | $14.4 | $8.6 | $5.8 |
| Utilities | $2.9 | $3.2 | $1.9 | $1.3 |
| Card fees, repairs, supplies, marketing | $9.1 | $9.1 | $9.6 | $9.6 |

*Model output. The first two columns are the earlier designs in the center's 1,004 sq ft unit; the roll kitchen is in its 830 sq ft unit. Stage 3 is an illustration, not an estimate.*

- **The food is cheap.** Every roll format costs 8–14% of its Miami price in ingredients and packaging. Nothing more can be won there.
- **Labor per roll is set by batch size.** The recipes time a roll at 3.1 hands-on minutes in 36-roll batches. In a 60-quart mixer run of 132, with a sheeter on the bench, it takes about 1.3. That figure is my estimate from the recipe steps and has to be timed in Stage 1; if it fails, a third baker costs $44,571 a year.
- **A shop pays for quiet days.** The Boston-style shop paid its crew and rent six mornings a week and broke even at 96% of its estimated sales. The kitchen breaks even at 65%, because pre-orders set the bake a day ahead and the weekday crew is working on office orders, fundraiser packs and tomorrow's dough.
- **Rent buys a window, and only the window needs it.** 46% of the kitchen's sales don't pass through the window, and 39% (markets, office orders, fundraisers) are the same wherever the kitchen sits, so they add sales without adding rent.

The benchmarks say the same. Nothing Bundt Cakes, the best-earning box bakery in the country, averaged $1,479,065 a bakery with a 22.1% store-level profit in its 2023 figures, selling celebration cakes from strip centers with no seats; one trades at 9487 S Dixie Hwy, inside Dadeland Plaza ([Franchise Chatter](https://www.franchisechatter.com/2024/12/15/fdd-talk-nothing-bundt-cakes-franchise-costs-fees-average-revenues-and-or-profits-2024-review/); [Community Newspapers](https://communitynewspapers.com/?p=51688)). Crumbl shows what novelty is worth: average sales fell from $1.8 million in 2022 to $1.14 million in 2025, the median store's profit to $77,359, and about 61 stores have closed this year ([Restaurant Business, 2024](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/financing/fast-growing-crumbls-unit-volumes-store-profitability-took-hit-last-year); [Restaurant Business](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/financing/crumbl-slows-its-growth-restaurant-volumes-increase); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91631321/crumbl-cookies-closing-stores-2026-list-shuttered-locations-list)). Misha's Cupcakes, Coral Gables' celebration-box brand for 21 years, moved into a 3,333 sq ft flagship on US-1 in 2024, closed it in May 2026 and ended the business on October 21; the space is now offered at $85 a sq ft ([Gables Courier](https://gablescourier.com/articles/misha-s-cupcakes-closes-for-good-after-21-years-in-coral-gables-mvjtdbt0); [LoopNet](https://www.loopnet.com/Listing/1586-S-Dixie-Hwy-Coral-Gables-FL/26136818/)). No reason was given; the lease is my inference.

My reading: a hand-made bakery reaches 20–25% only when one kitchen feeds several selling points and none of them is an expensive lease. A manager in your place would cost about $77,700 with payroll taxes, which leaves 8% at Stage 2 and 17% at Stage 3. I found no honest way to 30% at this size.

**Models I rejected.**

| Model | Why not |
|---|---|
| A bakery-café with seats and lunch | It competes with Bachour, Pura Vida and Paris Baguette on rents of $65–$95 a sq ft, and the Boston version needed over $1 million of sales |
| A roll shop with a window and nothing else | The roll house left $61,712 on an estimate I now think was too high; this plan's kitchen with its window alone loses $35,354 |
| A kitchen hidden in a cheap warehouse | Modeled at Ranchuelo's by The Falls: $50,450, because the window is worth more than the rent saved |
| A ghost kitchen for delivery only | The one priced unit found was $4,500 a month on a five-year term; the operators' kitchens are in Wynwood, Doral and Miami Beach, and apps take 25–30% |
| A mall kiosk or a food-hall stall | One Dadeland kiosk pays $5,000 a month for mall hours; Doral's food hall closed this month |
| Fresh rolls wholesale to cafés | About half the price for a product that is best within a day |
| A franchise | Cinnaholic's average shop sells about $375,100; a Nothing Bundt Cakes bakery costs $585,000–$1.1 million to open and isn't your product |
| Buying a café for its sales | The one with published figures asks $199,000 for $65,000 of claimed cash flow on $165,000 of sales |

**Who buys.** Five groups, with the evidence for their size:

- **Weekend households** in Pinecrest, Palmetto Bay, Coral Gables, South Miami and Kendall, buying a box for Sunday. Crumbl and Nothing Bundt Cakes both trade in Pinecrest and Kendall, which shows the box habit at $20–$50.
- **Hosts and celebrants**, who need a tray or a cake on a date. Misha's exit leaves the mini-dozen, its staple, without its best-known seller.
- **Offices and clinics.** Coral Gables counts more than 10,000 businesses; Baptist Hospital, South Miami Hospital and Doctors Hospital employ roughly 8,400–11,100 between them ([City of Coral Gables](https://www.coralgables.com/department/economic-development); [CauseIQ](https://causeiq.com/directory/hospitals-list/miami-fort-lauderdale-pompano-beach-fl-metro)).
- **Gift givers.** Miami's Realtor association had about 56,000 members before its 2026 merger, and closing gifts usually run $50–$300 ([Florida Realtors](https://floridarealtors.org/news-media/news-articles/2026/04/south-florida-association-merger-sets-record); [Climb the Ladder](https://climbtheladder.com/how-much-do-realtors-spend-on-closing-gifts/)).
- **Students and market-goers**, who buy the grande roll one at a time: the University of Miami has 19,852 students ([University of Miami](https://www.irsa.miami.edu/cds2425.pdf)).

The county is not growing: it lost 10,115 residents in the year to July 2025, while visitors reached a record 28 million ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/local/miami/2026/04/06/miami-population-decline-immigration-slowdown); [Caribbean National Weekly](https://www.caribbeannationalweekly.com/posts/miami-dade-tourism-generates-23-billion-in-visitor-spending)). The plan sells to residents and treats visitors as a bonus at winter markets.

*Costs are the database's at distributor estimates. Seven products are new drafts in `db/products/` and none has been baked: the grande roll, the roll of the week, the mini tray, the roll cake, the two twist loaves and the colada. The guava roll, the six- and twelve-box and the take-and-bake pack were drafted for the roll house.*

**Prices** sit inside Miami's range: under a golfeado and a Coral Gables croissant for the single, level with Keik for the grande, level with The Salty for the dozen, and under two dozen bundtinis for the tray. I kept the roll house's prices so that the gain in this plan comes from the structure, not from a price rise. Prices 5% higher are worth $26,495 a year; Stage 1 should test $5.75 against $5.25 for the single.

**The calendar.** A new roll of the week every Monday (cafecito glaze, key lime, coconut and tres leches, a golfeado-style roll with papelón and salty cheese). Seasonal bakes from the same dough: rosca de Reyes for January 6, heart-shaped boxes in February, Mother's Day boxes on May 9 (also Knaus's last day), graduation trays in late May, pan de jamón in December as a test.

**Left out on purpose:** seats, lunch, croissants and laminated dough, scones and muffins, fresh rolls wholesale to cafés. Each adds labor or rent faster than sales in this market.

**Two cautions.** The classic roll is 4 oz, small beside a Cinnabon or a Keik roll; if single buyers balk, a 6 oz classic costs about 15 cents more to make. And the state's market-stall permit covers only items that don't need refrigeration, so the guava-and-cheese roll needs the regulator's ruling before it goes on the stall ([FDACS](https://www.fdacs.gov/Business-Services/Food/Food-Establishments/Mobile-Food-Establishments)).

## 5. Five ways to sell

| Delivery apps | Students, households | 184 | $264: about 11 orders | $48,182 | 7% | 27% | 38% |

*Model output for the Dadeland Plaza kitchen. Contribution is sales less the channel's own food, variable costs, fees and selling crew; the allocation of the crew is my judgment. The shared costs are the two bakers and cleaning ($111,797), rent and NNN ($57,278), utilities ($12,824) and overheads ($43,466).*

- **Window.** Open 7 am to 1 pm Thursday to Sunday, Friday to Sunday from June to September. I set it at what an average cinnamon-roll franchise shop sells in a year, about a quarter below the roll house's estimate, because nothing in the research supports more. Boxes ordered online are picked up from a shelf, so one person runs it on Thursday and Friday.
- **Markets.** About 235 rolls a stall-day, against Roll & Dough's reported 300. The Pinecrest market runs on Sundays at Pinecrest Gardens with more than 80 vendors, and Coral Gables' on Saturdays from January to March; Key Biscayne and Cutler Bay have Saturday markets, and the University of Miami a Wednesday one run by an outside operator ([Pinecrest Farmers Market](https://pinecrestfarmermarket.com/); [Coral Gables Gazette](https://gables-gazette.com/coral-gables-farmers-market-returns-to-city-hall/); [Miami & Beaches](https://www.miamiandbeaches.com/event/key-biscayne-farmers-market/43270); [UM events](https://events.miami.edu/event/farmers_market_3920)). Fees weren't published; one page gives $100 to apply and $65 a week for a Pinecrest market.
- **Office, gift and party orders.** Your channel to sell. A free tray of 24 minis costs $3.38 to make, so sampling five offices a week costs under $900 a year. Orders go out by courier at a flat fee from $6.99 a drop; a catering marketplace takes 15% plus 2.99%, or 7% through your own ordering page ([Uber](https://merchants.ubereats.com/us/zh/pricing); [ezCater](https://catering.ezcater.com/en/help/what-is-the-commission/-payment-breakdown)).
- **Fundraisers.** The group's families pre-order frozen take-and-bake packs at $24 and loaves at $19; the group keeps 30%, about $1,078 an event, and you make one frozen drop. Butter Braid runs the same model at $15 a pastry, with $3.50–$6.20 to the group and 400 pastries in its example; Krispy Kreme's groups keep half of a $9 dozen ([Butter Braid](https://butterbraid.com/fundraising/); [Krispy Kreme](https://images.krispykreme.com/fundraising/FundraisingPricingGuide.pdf)). Each event also puts your rolls in 100 or more ovens at the group's expense.
- **Delivery apps.** Priced 15% above the window and charged 25–30% ([DoorDash](https://merchants.doordash.com/en-us/pricing); [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/uber-eats-increases-marketplace-fees/814294/)). It earns least and matters least: dropping it costs $22,557.

**Marketing.** 3% of sales, about $20,000 a year, plus $15,000 at the kitchen's opening. The tools, in order of what I'd rely on: the pre-order list and a Monday text announcing the roll of the week; ordering in English and Spanish, by link and by WhatsApp; office sampling; fundraisers as paid-for trial; a free-roll morning at the first market, as The Salty and Keik both did; and local food accounts for the weekly roll. No paid advertising in the base case.

## 6. How it runs

**The week.** Monday closed. Tuesday and Wednesday: dough, fillings, take-and-bake packs, and the office orders. Thursday to Sunday: the baker starts the bake at 5 am from rolls shaped the afternoon before and held overnight in the proofer-retarder; the window opens at 7; on Saturday and Sunday the market load leaves at 6. The production lead works daytime hours, Tuesday to Saturday, which is easier to hire for than a 4 am start.

**Production.** The kitchen uses about 209 kg of dough a working week, about 2,300 classic-roll equivalents, in 17 or 18 mixes of 12 kg. A double-stack convection oven bakes about 240 rolls a load by my count of its racks, so the oven is not the limit; proofer space on a Saturday is, and needs a staggered schedule. The model counts 10.3 hands-on hours on an average working day against 30.6 paid.

| Role | Hours a week | Wage | Covers |
|---|---|---|---|
| You, the owner | About 47 | Not in the costs | Office, gift and fundraiser accounts; the schedule, books and marketing |
| Production lead | 40 | $25.00 | Dough, fillings, shaping; owns quality and the production plan |
| Baker | 40 | $19.50 | The 5 am bake, glazing, market and window loads |
| Order desk and packing | 24 | $18.50 | Pre-orders, boxes, labels, courier drops |
| Window crew | About 49 | $15.50 plus tips | One on Thursday and Friday, two or three at weekends, including the bar |
| Market crew | About 16 | $16.50 plus tips | One or two at a stall |
| Dish and clean | 12 | $15.00 | |

*Model inputs: about 181 paid hours a week at an average $18.96, plus 11% for payroll taxes and workers' compensation. Under federal rules the bakers can share the window's tips because everyone is paid the full minimum ([Ferry](https://useferry.com/tip-laws/florida)).*

**Equipment** is $119,500, bought used where the machine is simple: asking prices this year were $3,290–$7,990 for a double-stack convection oven and $4,350–$8,500 for a 60-quart Hobart mixer ([Janco](https://jancosales.com/product/vulcan-vc4-gd-10-double-stacked-gas-convection-ovens-used-great-pricing/); [Machinio](https://www.machinio.com/cat/mixers-in-miami,-fl)). Refrigeration, the dishwasher and the espresso machine are new.

**Permits.** The state agriculture department licenses bakeries and coffee shops as retail food establishments, so the coffee window doesn't change the regulator, and the permit includes one free market unit ([FDACS](https://www.fdacs.gov/Business-Services/Food/Food-Establishments/Retail-Food-Establishment-Permit)). Home baking under the cottage food law can't be mixed with a permitted bakery and likely excludes the dairy glaze, so Stage 1 uses a permitted commissary ([UF/IFAS](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/FS425/pdf)). Rolls are untaxed; drinks, hot items and catered trays carry 7%, so the register needs separate keys.

## 10. Risks

- **Volume.** Nothing here is measured. The window's estimate rests on a franchise average and the traffic research's ranking; the others on one pop-up's reported Saturday and on programs elsewhere. Stage 1 exists for this reason.
- **Rivals move first.** Roll & Dough has said it plans a shop, and either pop-up could take the gap. Speed in Stage 1 and the office and fundraiser accounts are your defenses; a line at a market is not.
- **Novelty fades.** Crumbl's fall is the warning. The plan leans on habit, dates and accounts, and treats the roll of the week as a reminder, not the business.
- **The production times.** They are my estimate. Time them in the first month.
- **Two bakers.** The kitchen stops if both leave. Write the process down and pay the lead to stay.
- **The lease.** Miami landlords ask first-time operators for a personal guarantee. Cap it at a rolling 12 months.
- **Summer and storms.** A direct hit can cost a week and the frozen stock.
- **Your time.** The plan needs you selling on weekdays and at markets on weekends through Stage 1.
- **Unconfirmed facts.** Every rent, the Dadeland unit's history, commissary prices, market fees, Pinecrest's July 2026 zoning rewrite, the rule on walk-up windows in each town, and the county's fee class for a bakery.
