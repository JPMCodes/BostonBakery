# What $500,000 of EBITDA Takes

No shop gives it. A business with $500,000 of EBITDA is worth about $1.75–2.25 million, so with $500,000 you either build one over about six years at long odds, or borrow about $1.5–2 million and buy one, keeping $185,217–$269,265 a year until the loan is paid. In food it means three or four good shops, not one.

*Prepared November 25, 2026. Model figures are from the repo's pro forma and are in [model_results.md](../research_notes/Miami%20500K%20EBITDA%20paths/model_results.md): Year 2 of each unit, Miami wages, no owner's pay in the costs. "EBITDA" here also pays a general manager $77,700 to do your job. Research notes: [scale_economics.md](../research_notes/Miami%20500K%20EBITDA%20paths/scale_economics.md) and [acquisition_route.md](../research_notes/Miami%20500K%20EBITDA%20paths/acquisition_route.md). Every research figure comes from search-result text, because franchise filings, broker surveys and listing sites refused page reads. Listing figures are sellers' claims. The odds are my judgment.*

- **The best-documented dessert shop in the country earns less than that.** Nothing Bundt Cakes reports average sales of about $1.47 million and four-wall EBITDA of $316,591 a bakery, 21.6% ([Franchise Chatter](https://www.franchisechatter.com/2025/12/17/nothing-bundt-cakes-franchise-review-2025-costs-fees-news-average-revenues-and-or-profits/); [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/nothing-bundt-cakes-roark-kkr/815842/)). Crumbl's median store made $77,359 in 2024 ([QSR Magazine](https://www.qsrmagazine.com/growth/fast-casual/crumbls-sales-and-profit-grow-rapidly-ahead-of-potential-acquisition)).
- **What $500,000 of earnings costs.** Brokers report 3.1 times the owner's earnings for businesses sold at $1–2 million and 4.0 times EBITDA at $2–5 million, against 2.0 times under $500,000 ([Iconic, citing the IBBA Market Pulse for Q2 2026](https://iconic.co/blog/sde-multiples/)). Your $500,000 with no loan buys $185,185–$250,000 of owner's earnings.

*Model output and loan arithmetic: 10 years at 10%, the price plus 6% for closing costs and working capital.*

## 3. Build it

- **A supplied shop**, sketched on the roll kitchen's window: a fitted bay of 450 sq ft, open Tuesday to Sunday, baked for by the hub. It sells $545,504 and leaves $147,047 at the window's design, $68,329 on my odds for a window, and costs $192,188 to open. That margin, 27%, is above Nothing Bundt Cakes' 21.6%, so treat it as a top-quartile shop.

- **The calendar if every stage meets its design:** pop-up 2027, kitchen 2028, first shop 2030, third shop 2032. The $500,000 is never exhausted, and nothing is drawn for your pay on the way.
- **My odds of reaching $500,000 this way are about one in ten:** a 40% chance the window reaches 90% of its design, about half that the kitchen then fills, about half that three shops then average their design.
- **How long it has taken others.** The Salty reached about 22 shops and "over $21 million" of sales in roughly a decade; Night Owl Cookies passed $1.5 million five years in; Zak the Baker stayed on one site and grew to 85 employees ([QSR Magazine](https://www.qsrmagazine.com/story/how-amanda-pizarro-built-the-salty-donut-into-a-21-million-brand/); [Miami New Times](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/restaurants/cookie-craze-in-miami-night-owl-phenomenom-and-cindy-lous-10491824); [Resy](https://blog.resy.com/2023/11/how-zak-the-baker-cultivates-craftsmanship-and-community/)).

## 4. Buy it

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 times | $1,500,000 | $1,190,000 | $188,711 | $311,289 | 2.65 | $211,289 |
| 3.5 times | $1,750,000 | $1,455,000 | $230,735 | $269,265 | 2.17 | $169,265 |
| 4 times | $2,000,000 | $1,720,000 | $272,759 | $227,241 | 1.83 | $127,241 |
| 4.5 times | $2,250,000 | $1,985,000 | $314,783 | $185,217 | 1.59 | $85,217 |

*Loan arithmetic. About 40% of the first year's payments is principal and more each year, so your stake grows; after year 10 the $500,000 is yours.*

- **The loan exists for this and not for a new shop.** An SBA 7(a) purchase loan runs to $5 million over 10 years at up to prime plus 3 points, with prime at 7.00% in November 2026. It needs 10% in equity and, for loans from October 1, 2026, earnings on the seller's tax returns of 1.25 times the payments; forecasts no longer count ([NerdWallet](https://nerdwallet.com/business/loans/learn/sba-loan-rates); [BizBuySell](https://www.bizbuysell.com/learning-center/sba-7a-loan-business-acquisition/); [Starfield & Smith](https://starfieldsmith.com/2026/09/best-practices-change-of-ownership-transactions-under-sop-50-10-8-1-what-sba-lenders-need-to-know/)).
- **You guarantee it personally,** without limit, and if the business's assets fall short the lender takes a lien on real estate in which you hold 25% or more equity.
- **Food is the cheapest place to buy earnings.** Restaurants sold at 2.39 times cash flow in the third quarter of 2026 and bakeries at 2.68 times in 2025; Miami HVAC and plumbing firms claiming $650,000 ask $3.8–4.0 million, about six times ([We Sell Restaurants](https://blog.wesellrestaurants.com/restaurant-sellers-got-a-record-94.2-of-asking-price-in-q3-2026-even-as-deals-slowed); [BizBuySell](https://www.bizbuysell.com/learning-center/valuation-benchmarks/bakery/); [BizBuySell](https://www.bizbuysell.com/florida/miami-dade-county/hvac-businesses-for-sale/)). The low price is the market's view of the risk.
- **Few are for sale.** The search found about four South Florida food businesses claiming $400,000–800,000 at under $3 million, and some claims look inflated: a caterer claims cash flow of 67% of sales.

- **How these purchases turn out.** Search funds, the professionally backed version of this, have returned 33.9% a year in aggregate, but only 58% of them ever bought a company, and 27% of solo searchers in the international study lost capital ([Stanford](https://gsb.stanford.edu/insights/search-funds-keep-offering-proven-path-ownership); [IESE](https://www.iese.edu/insight/articles/search-funds-record-expansion/)). SBA purchase loans default about half as often as start-up loans in one broker's analysis; restaurant loans are charged off at 5–9% ([GoSBALoans](https://gosbaloans.com/guide/sba-loan-default-rates-by-loan-use/); [PeerSense](https://peersense.com/sba-industry/fullservice-restaurants-722511)).
- **Florida's trap:** a buyer inherits the seller's unpaid state taxes unless the seller produces a Department of Revenue certificate ([Fla. Stat. 213.758](https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/213.758)).

## 5. The platform and the roll

The roll kitchen's markets, office orders and fundraisers need a kitchen, not a window. Baked in a bought commissary they sell $257,050 and leave $29,584 at their design after a hired production lead, for $108,681 of equipment and launch. On the dessert brand that makes $340,584 before $121,473 of loan payments. The roll adds little without shop windows; the bought earnings do the work.

## 6. What I would do

1. **Settle which number you mean.** $500,000 of EBITDA can exist in 2027 with a loan. $500,000 in your pocket is a ten-year result on any route.
2. **If you want the number soon, buy, and buy a kitchen you can judge:** a production or multi-site bakery. Hold to verified EBITDA of $450,000 or more on tax returns and sales-tax filings, a price of 3.5–4 times, cover of 1.7 or better, a lease at least as long as the loan, no customer over 20% of sales, and the seller staying a year.
3. **Nothing found passes yet.** First calls: the two-bakery listing for its last three tax returns, the wholesale bakery for the basis of its 49% margin, the dessert brand.
4. **If you will not borrow, build:** pop-up, kitchen, then shops. Expect $130,000–250,000 a year for you if the kitchen meets its design and then its target, and $500,000 only if three shops all work.
5. **Not these:** a Crumbl package, or another single-shop concept.

## 7. What this could not see

- No filing, broker survey or listing page could be opened; the multiples and franchise figures rest on secondary readings.
- Whether Nothing Bundt Cakes' EBITDA is before its royalty, and whether any territory is open in Miami-Dade.
- The earnings, lease and staff of every listing, and whether each is still for sale.
- The supplied shop is a sketch on an unmeasured window; the bigger production kitchen is an allowance.
- Loan fees, the spread a lender would actually charge, and tax.
