# No Miami space found pays the owner $60,000

The client's roll bakery would not work in any Miami space found so far. Four spaces in Coral Gables and Pinecrest had enough facts to model: three lose money and leave the pro forma under the client's standing rule, and the one that stays, a **1,004 sq ft "restaurant / retail" unit at Dadeland Plaza, 9455–9569 S Dixie Hwy, Pinecrest**, leaves the owner **$8,641 in Year 2 (−$43K at low sales, $60K at high), a 2% return on $405,613 of cash**, on a $55 base rent and $12 NNN that are both assumed because the landlord publishes neither. The model says it needs **234 orders a day to pay the owner $60,000 against 195 estimated**, can carry only **$5 a sq ft** of base rent while doing so, and still falls short at $54K with prices 10% higher. All five Boston sites in the same model do better, leaving **$22K–$67K**, led by 1 Brattle Sq in Harvard Square. Miami's lower wages, payroll costs and power are worth **$16K–$19K a year** on an identical shop, but small shop space asks Boston-level rents ($55 NNN at Pinecrest Center, $60 NNN at Downtown Dadeland, $65–$95 on Miracle Mile), and no Miami district has the transit and student crowds behind Harvard Square's 263 orders; the best were rated between Brighton and Jamaica Plain. Miami also buys cinnamon rolls as events, from Knaus Berry Farm's $1.45–$2 rolls sold October to May to Keik's weekend-only $8.99–$9.99 rolls, and Paris Baguette has leased its first South Florida café nearly across US-1 from Dadeland Plaza. The model's right-sized test says a turnkey 930 sq ft café pays the owner $60,000 only at about $40–$55 base rent plus $14–$16 NNN with about 234 orders a day, above Jamaica Plain's 214, or at Jamaica Plain's sales with prices 10% higher; the closed bakery and café spaces at **2300 Ponce de Leon (at Giralda), 2526 Ponce and 256 Giralda** are where that could happen, though none is listed and their sizes and rents are unknown, so the next steps are a market-stall or commissary test of the roll and calls to those landlords, not a lease.

### Miami's cheaper payroll and power are worth $16K–$19K a year

The model's Miami cost block pays a lead baker $23 an hour instead of Boston's $25, a second baker $19 instead of $20, a barista $16 instead of $16.50, counter staff $15.25 instead of $15.50 and peak help and dish $15 instead of $15.50, so the average paid crew wage falls from $18.82 to $17.92 before payroll burden; it also uses an 11% payroll burden instead of 12%, utilities at $15 a sq ft a year instead of $20, and two more days closed a year for storms ([db/proforma.md](../db/proforma.md)). Run on three Boston shops with their rents and sales unchanged, that is worth **$16K–$19K a year** to the owner ([model](../research_notes/Miami%20roll%20bakery%20feasibility/proforma_results.md)):

| Site (model) | Left for the owner, Boston costs | With Miami costs | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Brattle Sq, Cambridge | $67K | $84K | $18K |
| Café for sale, Jamaica Plain center | $29K | $48K | $19K |
| 401-403 Washington St, Brighton | $22K | $38K | $16K |

The saving is modest because Florida's minimum wage reached **$15.00 on September 30, 2026**, the same as Massachusetts', so counter and barista pay converge, and Florida's tip credit is small: its tipped cash wage is $11.98 against a Massachusetts service rate of $6.75 ([LaborLawByState](https://laborlawbystate.com/en/states/florida/); [Mass.gov](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/massachusetts-law-about-minimum-wage)). Skilled baking is where Miami saves, with 2026 Coral Gables ads offering $20–$23 an hour for a pastry baker at Madruga and $18–$20 at Bachour ([Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Job/coral-gables-bakery-jobs-SRCH_IL.0,12_IC1166105_KO13,19.htm)). Payroll charges are lighter: a 2.7% new-employer reemployment tax on the first $7,000 of wages, a residual-market bakery workers' comp rate of $2.778 per $100 of payroll, no state paid-sick-leave law and a 2024 state law preempting local scheduling rules, against Massachusetts' 40 hours a year of earned sick time ([Florida Department of Revenue](https://floridarevenue.com/taxes/taxesfees/Pages/rt_rate.aspx); [FWCJUA](https://www.fwcjua.com/Rates); [Miami-Dade legislative report](https://www.miamidade.gov/commission/library/2024-final-legislative-report.pdf); [Mass.gov, earned sick time](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/earned-sick-time)). Power is cheaper per kWh, with Florida commercial customers averaging 12.84¢ in February 2026 against Eversource's 15.03¢ for small-business supply alone, though FPL's demand tariff adds $12.70 per kW and the air conditioning runs all year ([KilowattLogic](https://kilowattlogic.com/news/florida-commercial-electricity-rates-fpl-duke-energy-2026); [MassPowerChoice](https://www.masspowerchoice.com/newton/pricing); [FPL](https://www.fpl.com/content/dam/fplgp/us/en/rates/pdf/bus-eff-jan-2026.pdf)). Florida also stopped taxing commercial rent for occupancy from October 1, 2025 ([Greenberg Traurig](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2025/6/florida-legislature-repeals-sales-tax-on-commercial-leases)). Storms are the offset the model counts as two lost days: a tropical cyclone affects Miami about 0.3 times a year, and sales in an impact window fall about 39% before recovering within about 10 days ([DisasterAWARE](https://explore.disasteraware.com/risks/miami-fl/); [World Bank](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/developmenttalk/when-wind-speeds-mislead--what-hurricanes-really-do-to-local-eco)). In our judgment the saving is real but already inside every Miami figure above, and it cannot offset rent that runs higher than Brighton's or Jamaica Plain's at similar sales. The model keeps the roll bakery's $6,000 insurance line, which covers the $3,000–$5,000-plus that Miami bakery policies cost, but windstorm premiums reach tenants through NNN, which is assumed everywhere ([Insure Your Bakery](https://www.insureyourbakery.com/blog/bakery-confectionery-insurance-cost-2026); [PKCRE](https://pkcre.com/insights/what-the-worth-avenue-retail-trade-means-for-tenants)).

Money is not Miami's constraint; footfall is. Pinecrest's median household income is $206,417 and Coral Gables' $130,803, against Cambridge's $130,748, while South Miami's is about $88,313 by an aggregator's count and 30.4% of Brighton households earn under $50,000 ([Census, Coral Gables](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/coralgablescityflorida/SBO001222); [CensusDepth](https://censusdepth.com/cities/south-miami-fl); [Cambridge](https://www.cambridgema.gov/CDD/factsandmaps/demographicfaq)). The three towns are about half Hispanic, against 9–22% in the Boston districts, which in our judgment shapes the menu and the competition more than the volume. What Miami lacks is Harvard's walking crowd: Harvard station's riders are four to eight times any Miami candidate station's, Coral Gables is the only one of the three towns with a big daytime workforce, and 94.7% of Pinecrest's working residents commute out of a village whose own study says it "lacks an engaging, main-street style destination" ([Pinecrest market analysis](https://www.pinecrest-fl.gov/files/sharedassets/village/v/2/government/building-and-planning/documents/211006-pinecrest-market-an.pdf)).

Miami's trade arrives by car. US-1 carries 65,500–95,000 vehicles a day past the Pinecrest centers by broker counts, but that is pass-by traffic, and 71.9% of Coral Gables workers drive alone while 4.2% walk ([Coral Gables](https://www.coralgables.com/media/4086)). Parking favors a 7 am shop in Coral Gables, where meters are not enforced before 9 am and garages charge $2.50 an hour, and Suniland's lot in Pinecrest is free, if tight near popular stores ([Coral Gables parking](https://www.coralgables.com/department/parking-department/parking-garages-fees-information); [Tripadvisor, Suniland](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g34559-d7161893-Reviews-Suniland_Plaza_Shopping_Center-Pinecrest_Florida.html)). The week is lopsided: South Miami's own study finds weekends busiest, Saturday boardings at the candidate stations run 32–39% of weekday levels, and Madruga's lines form on weekend mornings ([South Miami](https://southmiamifl.gov/DocumentCenter/View/7889/1-Community-Characteristics?bidId=); [DTPW](https://www.miamidade.gov/resources/transportation_publicworks/documents/rtr/2025-10-monthly-ridership-report.pdf); [Tripadvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Restaurant_Review-g34152-d12202145-Reviews-Madruga_Bakery-Coral_Gables_Florida.html)). In our judgment Pinecrest and South Miami shops would sell disproportionately on Saturday and Sunday mornings, a Coral Gables office-core shop on weekday mornings, and Miracle Mile would be the most balanced.

Seasonality cuts deeper than the model shows. An aggregator's reading of Florida sales-tax data puts Miami restaurant sales at 1.15 times an average month in January and 0.90 times in October 2025; one Miami owner reported summer 2025 sales 40% below April–May, and Axios reported restaurateurs bracing for "another brutal summer slowdown" in 2026 ([MenuSpy](https://menuspy.ai/reports/miami-fl/); [Axios, 2025](https://www.axios.com/local/miami/2025/07/30/miami-restaurants-summer-slowdown-2025); [Axios, 2026](https://www.axios.com/local/miami/2026/05/28/miami-restaurants-summer-slowdown-closures)). Weekday boardings fell 55% from October to June at University station, against 20% at South Miami and 26% at Dadeland North ([DTPW, June 2026](https://www.miamidade.gov/resources/transportation_publicworks/documents/rtr/2026-06-monthly-ridership-report.pdf)). The model has no seasonal curve: each sales index stands for the year's average, and the Miami block subtracts only two storm days. The Boston research did not measure a seasonal swing either, though Harvard Square also empties of students in summer. South Miami adds a near-term drag: Barnes & Noble is closing at Shops at Sunset Place for a redevelopment whose first phase targets 2029 ([The Miami Hurricane](https://themiamihurricane.com/2026/11/18/sunset-place-barnes-noble-closing-after-27-years/); [Lee & Associates](https://www.lee-associates.com/southflorida/2026/03/09/shops-at-sunset-place-scheduled-for-redevelopment-this-year/)).

No bakery or café in the three towns has published sales, so the nearest yardstick is a chain. Cinnaholic, the cinnamon-roll franchise, averaged about $375,100 a store in its 2024 disclosure, with its top half at $759,812 (secondary sources), and Pura Vida's Miami cafés averaged $750,000 in 2025 ([Franzy](https://franzy.com/franchises/cinnaholic); [Restaurant Business](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/emerging-brands/pura-vida-miami-captures-health-minded-diners-fast-casual-hybrid)). The model's $526,430 for Dadeland Plaza is well above the average cinnamon-roll franchise, so in our judgment the 195-order estimate is an upper-half outcome, not a cautious one.

## Same-use bakeries clear permits fast; conversions draw county fees

The state licence costs about what Boston's licences do and arrives in weeks, and a same-use space in Coral Gables or Pinecrest moves fast; the costs and delays come from hearings in South Miami and from county charges on any conversion from retail:

| Step | Coral Gables | South Miami | Pinecrest | Boston and Cambridge |

A few details sharpen the comparison. Coral Gables asks for the Certificate of Use "before signing a lease", and a 2022 amendment makes a walk-up counter serving patrons a conditional use, though the city's own record of its adoption was not found ([Coral Gables Legistar](https://coralgables.legistar.com/View.ashx?GUID=D48DC3ED-07E9-4460-9E40-92F37E960D20&ID=11581497&M=F)). Pinecrest says it is "actively recruiting restaurant concepts" for its US-1 district, offers expedited permitting, and issues building permits in "a couple weeks to 30 days" when plans are complete ([Village of Pinecrest](https://www.pinecrest-fl.gov/Government/Planning/Economic-Development/Open-a-Restaurant-in-Pinecrest); [Pinecrest permits FAQ](https://www.pinecrest-fl.gov/Government/Building/Apply-for-a-Permit/Permits-FAQ)). Every food use needs DERM's grease (FOG) operating permit, $150 a year under 4,000 sq ft, and a $120 sewer capacity certificate when flow rises ([Miami-Dade FOG permit](https://www.miamidade.gov/permits/grease-discharge.asp); [Miami-Dade environmental fees](https://www.miamidade.gov/permits/library/fees/schedule-environmental.pdf)). The cost research's arithmetic puts a 930 sq ft conversion from retail at about $3,600–$5,900 of water and sewer connection charges, because the county rates retail at 10 gallons a day per 100 sq ft against 65–100 for food, plus about $10,000–$12,000 of road fee if classed fast casual but $69,000–$79,000 if classed fast food, assuming credit for the prior retail use; the schedule has no line for a coffee shop without a drive-through, which makes the class the largest single county unknown ([WASD gallonage schedule](https://www.miamidade.gov/resources/water/documents/guidelines/daily-rated-gallonage-schedule.pdf)). The model's startup budgets carry no separate line for these charges, so a raw bay at Dadeland Plaza would add them on top of its $405,613; a space last used as a restaurant should owe little or nothing. Taxes favor a take-home roll shop: bakery goods sold to go are exempt from Florida sales tax while coffee and anything eaten on site are taxable, which requires separate register keys, and equipment above a $25,000 exemption pays an annual tangible property tax ([Fla. Admin. Code 12A-1.011](https://regulations.justia.com/states/florida/12/12a/chapter-12a-1/section-12a-1-011); [Fla. Stat. 196.183](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/196.183)). In the cost research's judgment, a same-use space in Coral Gables or Pinecrest opens 3–6 months after signing, about as fast as an as-of-right Boston site, a conversion from retail takes 5–8 months, and a South Miami special-use hearing pushes it to 7–12 months, much like a Boston ZBA case; Boston's own guide says opening takes "a few months to a year or more", and Mayor Wu's October 27, 2026 plan to drop zoning review for takeout remains a proposal ([Boston restaurant guide](https://www.boston.gov/node/16634896); [Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/10/27/business/boston-restaurant-license-reforms/)).

## A closed Ponce bakery needs Jamaica Plain-level sales plus low rent or higher prices

Four dark food spaces in Coral Gables and one in South Miami are worth a call, because a same-use tenant skips most of the cost and delay above. **2300 Ponce de Leon Blvd** sits "at the corner of Ponce de leon and Giralda", which a reviewer placed two blocks north of Miracle Mile; it housed Granier Bakery and then Massa Bakery, which Yelp and MapQuest show as permanently closed by July 2026, and no lease listing, size or owner was found ([Yelp, Granier](https://www.yelp.com/biz/granier-bakery-coral-gables); [Tripadvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Restaurant_Review-g34438-d818029-Reviews-or15-Granier_Bakery-Miami_Florida.html); [Yelp, Massa](https://www.yelp.com/biz/massa-bakery-coral-gables); [MapQuest](https://www.mapquest.com/us/florida/massa-bakery-425112664)). **2526 Ponce** was L'Artisane Creative Bakery until September 2025, when it moved to online sales; it sits in a 6,096 sq ft building at 2516–2526 Ponce that also held the closed Jugofresh, and the building's sale listing now reads "off market", so a new owner may be re-tenanting it ([The Burn Miami](https://theburnmiami.com/2025/09/lartisane-bakery-bistro-closes-shop-in-the-gables/); [CityFeet](https://www.cityfeet.com/cont/listing/2516-2526-ponce-de-leon-blvd-coral-gables-fl-33134/cs22553781); [Yelp, Jugofresh](https://www.yelp.com/biz/jugofresh-coral-gables-4)). **256 Giralda Ave** was Amore Gelato & Caffe and then Coffee For The Soul, which Yelp showed open in June 2026 and closed by November; its size and landlord were not found, but a 990 sq ft storefront on the same block at 246 Giralda leased in March 2025, rent not captured ([Yelp, June 2026](https://yelp.com/biz/coffee-for-the-soul-coral-gables); [Yelp, closed](https://www.yelp.com/biz/coffee-for-the-soul-coral-gables); [Yelp, Amore](https://www.yelp.com/biz/amore-gelato-and-caffe-coral-gables-2); [Crexi, 246 Giralda](https://www.crexi.com/lease/properties/1156596/florida-coral-gables-giralda-retail-990-sf)). In South Miami, **Einstein Bros. Bagels at 5946 S Dixie Hwy** closed in spring 2026 after 28 years, though Yelp says it will reopen on January 1, 2027, which suggests the chain still holds the lease ([The Burn Miami](https://theburnmiami.com/2026/05/after-28-years-einstein-bros-bagels-closes-in-south-miami/); [Yelp](https://www.yelp.com/biz/einstein-bros-bagels-south-miami)). In our judgment the Ponce end of Giralda suits a 7 am shop better than the plaza itself, since the city's sensor data shows Miracle Mile with slightly more morning pedestrians while Giralda draws mainly at lunch and dinner ([Coral Gables](https://www.coralgables.com/media/4086)).

What size and rent would make one of them work is answered by the model's right-sized test: a hypothetical 930 sq ft second-generation café in Miami with a $50 a sq ft refresh, a $20,000 equipment credit and three months to open, showing what is left for the owner in Year 2 ([model](../research_notes/Miami%20roll%20bakery%20feasibility/proforma_results.md)):

| Base rent + NNN per sq ft | Sales index 0.95 (Brighton, 185 orders) | 1.0 (195) | 1.1 (Jamaica Plain, 214) | 1.2 (234) | Same at 1.1 with prices 10% higher |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $40 + $14 | $14K | $27K | $53K | $79K | $103K |
| $45 + $15 | $9K | $22K | $47K | $73K | $97K |
| $55 + $16 | −$2K | $11K | $37K | $63K | $86K |
| $65 + $18 | −$13K | $0K | $25K | $51K | $75K |

Read across, the table sets the terms. At the 1.0 index the traffic research gave Miracle Mile, no rent in the table pays the owner $60,000; the best is $27K at $40 base plus $14 NNN. At Jamaica Plain's 1.1 the best is $53K. Only at 1.2, or 234 orders a day, do the three cheaper rent rows clear $60,000 ($79K, $73K and $63K), and only with prices 10% higher at 1.1 does every row clear it ($75K to $103K). The space should therefore be about the program's 930 sq ft, where every Miami space modeled was 1,004–1,500 sq ft. On the volume route its rent can be no more than about $55 base plus $16 NNN, Ponce Plaza's or Pinecrest Center's level rather than Giralda's $75 or Miracle Mile's $65–$95; on the price route even $65 plus $18 leaves $75K. Two cautions in our judgment: the table assumes a genuinely turnkey bakery with a $50 a sq ft refresh, whereas Miami contractors put a typical second-generation refresh at $75–$150 a sq ft ([NAR Development](https://nardevelopmentfl.com/restaurant-buildout-costs-for-miami-and-fort-lauderdale/)); and both routes need sales above every rating the traffic research gave a Miami district. A closed bakery on Ponce works only if a test proves the roll can match Jamaica Plain's sales at prices 10% higher, or beat them at a rent near the strip-center level.

## A market stall and a commissary can test the roll before any lease

The cheapest test sits inside the target area. The **Pinecrest Farmers Market** runs every Sunday at Pinecrest Gardens with 80-plus vendors and takes applications through Unite Worldwide's vendor registration; a co-op page for a Pinecrest market lists a $100 application fee and $65 a week, though it may describe a different market ([Pinecrest Farmers Market](https://pinecrestfarmermarket.com/); [Miami Farmers Market](https://miamifarmersmarket.org/farmers-market-near-me-pinecrest/); [Green Market Co-op](http://greenmarketco-op.org/pinecrest-become-a-vendor.html)). The city-run **Coral Gables Farmers Market** runs Saturdays 8 am–2 pm at City Hall, 405 Biltmore Way, from mid-January to late March (January 17 to March 28 in 2026), with 80-plus vendors and a history back to 2002 ([Coral Gables Gazette](https://gables-gazette.com/coral-gables-farmers-market-returns-to-city-hall/); [Miami Tourism](https://www.miamitourism.org/farmers-markets/)). Florida's cottage food law lets a home baker sell rolls and pastries in person, online or by mail up to **$250,000 a year** without an FDACS permit, though not wholesale ([FDACS](https://www.fdacs.gov/Business-Services/Food/Food-Establishments/Cottage-Foods)); FDACS guidance quoted in cottage-food guides bars cream-cheese and butter icings that need refrigeration, so the client's cooked buttermilk glaze points to a licensed commissary instead ([Cottage Food Law Library](https://cottagefoodlawlibrary.org/florida)). Miami-Dade commissaries advertise from $7.99 an hour, and CityChef from $15 an hour or $300 a month, though its own site quotes $30–$120 an hour ([Miami Commissary Kitchen](https://www.miamidadecaterers.com/join-our-commissary); [The Kitchen Door](https://www.thekitchendoor.com/kitchen-rental/citychef-kitchens-and-catering); [CityChef](https://www.citychefcatering.com/membership)). Local precedents for borrowing a counter exist: Roll & Dough's month inside Tata's Yogurt Bar, Babette serving dinner inside Clásica Victoria's café on Sunset Drive, and Keik launching its fall roll with a free day before a weekend-only run ([Miramar FL News](https://www.miramarflnews.com/news/local/article316285946.html); [Marilyn Cromer](https://marilyncromer.com/blog/downtown-south-miamis-new-evenings-are-happening-in-borrowed-rooms); [Keik](https://www.keikrestaurant.com/blogs/news/treat-yourself-cinnamon-roll)).

In our judgment the test should answer what the model cannot: whether Miami buyers return weekly for a $4.75 roll outside a seasonal event, how many rolls leave in boxes, whether higher prices hold, and how deep the summer dip runs. Running it from January through a summer would cover both the Coral Gables market season and the slow months. It also produces what Miami landlords ask first-time operators for alongside a personal guarantee: "proof of concept: financial statements from existing locations, not just a business plan" ([Atlantic Commercial Advisors](https://atlanticcommercialadvisors.com/blog/restaurants-for-lease-miami-dade-county-2026)).

## Conclusion

Miami fails this format on density, not cost. The model already credits Miami's cheaper payroll and power, worth $16K–$19K a year on the same shop, yet the best space found leaves $8,641, because a six-day, walk-in morning shop needs the transit, student and office crowds that put Harvard Square at 263 orders, and Miami's car-borne districts offer Brighton-to-Jamaica Plain traffic at rents no lower than Boston's neighborhoods. Dadeland Plaza belongs in the pro forma only as a non-losing placeholder: with room for just $5 a sq ft of base rent at the owner's target, no rent quote can rescue it, only more orders or higher prices.

The finding that changes the plan is the shape of Miami's demand. Knaus rations rolls at four dozen a buyer and takes pre-orders of 4–12 dozen, Keik sells its roll only on weekends and never all year, and the viral sellers are pop-ups, so the evidence points to a box-and-pre-order, weekend-heavy, cooler-month business rather than a daily single-roll habit. A format built that way is a hypothesis the model has not tested, and it is what a Miami test should probe alongside the storefront menu. That reorders the steps: sell through a market season and a summer, use the record as the proof of concept Miami landlords ask for, and only then take a rent target of about $40–$55 plus NNN to the owners of the dark bakeries on Ponce de Leon and Giralda, the one path where Coral Gables' same-use permitting, the model's right-sized economics and the client's cash limit line up.
