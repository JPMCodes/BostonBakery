# Boston Bakery Site Shortlist: Spaces with Kitchens Worth a Call

*Update, November 10, 2026: the pro forma's food-cost and utilities inputs were raised after this was written, which lowers every rent ceiling below; current figures are in [`db/proforma.md`](../db/proforma.md). The smaller owner-run shop has its own site list in [Boston bakery small-shop sites.md](Boston%20bakery%20small-shop%20sites.md).*

*Checked November 8, 2026. This extends the [site scan](Boston%20bakery%20site%20scan.md) to Newton, Chestnut Hill, Brookline, Wellesley and Hingham, and re-checks the scan's best leads elsewhere. Only spaces with an existing kitchen or a recent food use count; raw retail is out. Sales levels are estimates, rents come from listings, and nothing was confirmed with a broker.*

Eight spaces are worth a call. Two stand out: **145 Charles St on Beacon Hill**, the best street and foot traffic found, and **1191 Centre St in Newton Centre**, in the easiest town to permit. Two more turn on one fact each. **250 Huntington Ave** pays well at its listed $4,000 a month and loses money at the $14,000 its broker's sale listing implies. **A Brookline pizzeria for sale** has a full hot line at about $30–35 a sq ft all-in, but its address is withheld. None of the best spaces publishes a rent, so the table gives the most rent each can carry.

## How to read the rent ceiling

The ceiling is the base rent per sq ft at which Year-2 cash after loan payments falls to zero in the pro forma (`db/proforma.yaml`), at the stated sales and NNN. A good deal sits well below it; the site scan's rule (base rent at or under 8% of sales) is stricter. Smaller spaces with strong traffic score best, because sales drive profit far more than rent: in the model, $360 a day more sales adds about $73K a year of EBITDA, while $10 a sq ft more rent on 2,500 sq ft costs $25K.

### Notes by site

1. **145 Charles St.** One of Boston's handsomest shopping streets, a short walk from the Charles/MGH Red Line stop and Mass General's staff, amid dense housing and on the weekend route to the Esplanade and Public Garden. Competition is real: Tatte at 70 Charles, Flour on Cambridge St, Beacon Hill Books & Cafe, and Brick Street Bagels announced nearby. Risks: rent probably at the top of the band; empty 27 months; short term offered; Boston take-out zoning and Beacon Hill's historic-district review of signs and storefront changes. Ask why it has sat empty and whether a 5-plus-5 lease is possible.
2. **1191 Centre St.** Newton Centre's village core, 0.2 miles from the Green Line with a 126-space town lot. Newton handles a restaurant of up to 50 seats that is closed from 11:30 pm to 6 am administratively, and a November 2025 ordinance (final adoption unconfirmed) drops parking for shops and restaurants in the village-center districts. The full kitchen fits beside about 16 seats. Risks: on the market about 21 months; SALT Patisserie and Blackbird Doughnuts nearby. Take the 1,894 sq ft unit alone: with the unit next door, the space can carry only $24 a sq ft at $2,900 a day.
3. **250 Huntington Ave.** Across from Symphony Hall among Northeastern, Berklee and the Conservatory. At $4,000 a month it clears about $79K a year after loan payments at $3,083 a day; at $14,000 it loses about $56K. Panera, Pavement and Caffè Nero are within a few blocks, and the arts crowds peak after a 3 pm close.
4. **Brookline pizzeria.** At $2,900 a day as a bakery-café it clears about $46K a year after loan payments, at the listed occupancy cost. Everything hangs on the address. If it is Brookline Village or Washington Square, it is a strong lead.
5. **Delfino.** On Adams Park, Roslindale's village green, beside the Saturday farmers market and the commuter-rail stop. At an assumed $30 rent it clears about $24K at $2,720 a day. The owner hoped to find a buyer; a later report said the restaurant was only closed for renovations.
6. **Giroux Building.** By the Battle Green in a handsome historic center with weekend visitors and the Minuteman Bikeway. Four bakery-cafés are within about three blocks: Tatte, Nouvé, Revival, and Commune All Day Cafe coming.
7. **Hingham Shipyard.** A modern waterfront end unit with a patio beside the commuter ferry. Hingham requires a zoning special permit for every restaurant, which likely adds 3–5 months. Tatte is on Derby St.
8. **627 Tremont St.** A corner of an 1899 building in the South End's rowhouse district that sold coffee for 15 years. Needs a hood and roof venting; nearby bakeries include Sip of Joy, Brick Street Bagels and Greystone; South End asking rents run $45–100.

## Checked and dropped

- **Trio, 845 Washington St, Newtonville** (former Clover, 2,725 sq ft): beside the Mass Pike; carries only about $27 a sq ft at $2,900 a day.
- **19 Main St, Hingham Square** (1,477 sq ft at $40 NNN): no hood, and needs about $2,360 a day just to cover the asking rent; Slow Coffee and Nona's are adding pastries nearby.
- **57 Lincoln St, Newton Highlands** ($45, size unknown): same problem at counter scale.
- **Coolidge Corner restaurant for sale** (1,500 sq ft, $185K, about $50 all-in) and **Party Favors, 1356 Beacon St** (about 3,500 sq ft with a basement bakery, not listed): the first needs about $2,400 a day as a counter; the second is too big unless sales beat Cambridge's.
- **Needham restaurant with a hood** (MLS 73519495, address withheld; 1,387 sq ft plus basement, $5,900/mo plus ~$1,200 NNN, $180–200K): works only at about $2,900 a day; find the address first.
- **Wellesley:** every food space vacated in 2025–26 was re-let within months; SALT Patisserie took the old Starbucks.
- **Chestnut Hill** malls and centers: no food vacancy. **Cleveland Circle, 1948 Beacon St** (former Starbucks): no hood, urban strip.
- **Roslindale pizza shop** (BizQuest BW2472318): address still withheld and probably not on the square.
- **Davis Square, 51 Holland St; Jamaica Plain, 349 Centre St; Egleston Square:** dropped earlier (no kitchen, or money losers in the pro forma).

## West Palm Beach, for comparison

Not cheaper for a walkable, attractive location. Downtown asks run about $50–55 a sq ft, and listings quote $11–14 of charges on top; the Palm Beach County average is a record ~$39 NNN with ~4% vacancy. Wages run about 4% below Boston's (Florida's minimum is $15 since September 30, 2026). Traffic is seasonal: a record 10.7 million county visitors in 2025, peaking December to May, with owners calling summer very slow. A 25% slump from June to October costs about $65K a year of profit at Cambridge-level sales, more than the wage savings.

## Before calling brokers

1. **Rent and charges:** base rent, NNN history, escalations, term and options, and any free rent or build-out allowance.
2. **Kitchen:** hood length, fire suppression and last inspection; grease trap size and last pump-out; gas and electric service.
3. **Why it is empty:** 145 Charles (27 months), 1191 Centre (about 21 months), 250 Huntington (since late 2024).
4. **Legal use (Boston addresses):** get a record of legal occupancy before signing; if take-out isn't in it, expect a zoning appeal unless the Wu reform passes first.
5. **For businesses for sale:** the address, landlord consent to assign the lease, years remaining, and what equipment stays.

## Method and caveats

- **Snippets.** Most listing facts come from search-result snippets: LoopNet, Crexi, CityFeet, CommercialCafe, BizQuest, BizBuySell and several town and news sites block automated reading. Boston's city data and listings, and the Wellesley, Hingham and Cambridge documents cited above, were read directly.
- **Sales levels** are judgment calls against the 1105 Mass Ave site, as in the pro forma; the rent ceilings move a lot with them.
- **Nothing was checked with a broker or landlord.** Rents are asking rents; sizes conflict for several spaces.
- **Not in the pro forma yet.** None of the shortlisted spaces publishes a usable rent. Add one to `db/proforma.yaml` once a broker gives terms.
