# Miami Bakery Concept Comparison: Is the Roll Kitchen the Best Bet?

Yes on the product and the format; no on the route. A roll-led box kitchen with no seats is still the best baking business I can find for you in Miami's southern suburbs. Every rival concept that fits $500,000 of cash leaves less, because none matches its two advantages: food and packaging at 18% of sales against about 30%, and rent at a tenth of sales because the bay is small. But the plan as written has three faults this research exposed. It signs a five-year lease on a window nobody has measured, and on my own odds for that window the kitchen is worth an expected $69,783 a year to you, not the plan's $129,152. It would measure demand at the top of a cinnamon-roll craze. And it builds a kitchen when two operating bakeries are for sale inside your budget. The fixes are to measure the window in a host shop before any lease, to keep the bay small and the lease short, and to check the two purchases while the pop-up runs.

Six research threads are in [research_notes/Miami bakery concept comparison](../research_notes/Miami%20bakery%20concept%20comparison/); the model runs are in [model_results.md](../research_notes/Miami%20bakery%20concept%20comparison/model_results.md). Model figures are Year 2, with no owner's pay in the costs and no loan, from the repo's pro forma. Rows marked "benchmarked" are not designs: they put published sales and cost ratios for that kind of bakery through the same arithmetic as the roll kitchen. ## 1. The verdict, question by question

| Question | Answer | The reason in one line |
|---|---|---|
| Is the roll the right product? | Yes, as the lead and not as the whole line | The lowest food cost of the concepts compared, proven local appetite, and no dedicated roll shop in Miami-Dade |
| Is a box kitchen with a weekend window the right format? | Yes | It is the format that is growing (Nothing Bundt Cakes); the one that is dying is the single-product treat shop sold on novelty |
| Is Pinecrest's stretch of US-1 the right place? | Yes | Household income of $206,417 against the county's $71,753, and it is where Paris Baguette, H&H Bagels, Crumbl and Nothing Bundt Cakes all chose to be |
| Is this the right moment? | For the pop-up, yes. For a lease, only after the window is measured | The craze helps a pop-up and inflates what it measures; rents are at a record |
| Is building the right route? | Not proven | A 50-year café-bakery and a wholesale bakery are for sale within budget; neither claim is verified |

**Why the roll wins on structure.** Two lines of the table decide it. Enriched dough sold by the box costs 18% of its price in food and packaging; bread, bagels with their spreads, and Cuban pastry cost about 30%. And a window open four mornings with pre-ordered boxes needs a small bay: rent, NNN and utilities take 10% of sales, against 12–22%.

## 4. What the area lacks

The supply map for Coral Gables, South Miami, Pinecrest and the Dadeland end of Kendall, from [local_supply_gaps.md](../research_notes/Miami%20bakery%20concept%20comparison/local_supply_gaps.md):

- **Missing:** a kosher bakery; American morning baking (scones, muffins, coffee cake, biscuits); a babka or sweet-loaf specialist; Argentine and Brazilian bakeries; a cupcake shop since Misha's closed; a pie shop.
- **Thin:** gluten-free, bread-first sourdough, key lime.
- **Crowded:** Cuban bakeries, croissant cafés, and now bagels, cookies and doughnuts.
- **Where the lines form:** at small makers with scarce weekend bakes, not at chains. A Coral Gables home bakery open on weekends draws a line outside the house; an RV bakery in a Coconut Grove parking lot is "Miami's hottest", with chocolate babka among its sellers; Madruga's babka sells out ([Miami New Times](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/food-drink/coral-gables-moms-home-bakery-becomes-neighborhood-sensation-40554109/); [Miami Herald via AOL](https://www.aol.com/miami-hottest-bakery-parking-lot-083000339.html); [Time Out](https://www.timeout.com/miami/restaurants/bakery-guide-to-miami)).

A gap is not demand. Bread is the gap the press names most, and at benchmark ratios it leaves $27,853. What I would take from the map is four additions that cost the kitchen almost nothing (my judgment):

1. **American morning trays for offices, from your own book.** The sour cream coffee cake and the blueberry muffin are already costed at 12–18% food cost. Nobody in the three towns specializes in them, and the Einstein Bros. on US-1 in South Miami closed this spring after 28 years ([Miami New Times](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/food-drink/beloved-south-miami-breakfast-spot-closes-after-28-years-40548441/)). Sell them by pre-order only, never at the window.
2. **A chocolate twist loaf.** Babka is the missing specialist, and Boston's Bakey opened in Hallandale Beach in July with Aventura and Fort Lauderdale planned, so it is coming south ([Time Out Miami](https://www.timeout.com/miami/news/bostons-cult-favorite-babka-bakery-just-opened-its-first-south-florida-location-072026)).
3. **One savory roll from the same dough**, ham and cheese. Cinnabon's shops paired with a pretzel brand sell $1,208,741 against $665,401 alone, and Shipley has signed for 20 South Florida shops selling kolaches ([Franchise Chatter](https://www.franchisechatter.com/2026/11/09/cinnabon-franchise-review-2026-costs-fees-news-average-revenues-and-or-profits/); [Shipley](https://shipleydonuts.com/franchise/news/shipley-do-nuts-expansion-new-mexico-and-south-florida)).
4. **Minis as the default tray.** Misha's sold mini dozens to this area's parties for 21 years and stopped on November 1 ([What Now Miami](https://whatnow.com/miami/restaurants/a-miami-bakery-that-started-in-a-home-kitchen-is-closing-after-21-years/)).

## 5. Where the plan is weak

### The window is a bet, and the plan prices it as a fact

The design gives the window 139 orders a morning on 184 mornings: an average Cinnaholic's whole year in half the days. It carries 54% of sales and cannot be seen before the lease.

| Window against the design | Left for you, Year 1 | Left for you, Year 2 |
|---|---|---|
| 30% | −$66,620 | −$33,822 |
| 50% | −$29,145 | $12,742 |
| 70% | $8,330 | $59,306 |
| 90% | $45,804 | $105,870 |
| 100% (the plan) | $64,541 | $129,152 |
| 120% | $102,016 | $175,715 |

*Model output, Dadeland Plaza, other channels at design.*

My odds for a new window in a strip center, as judgment: 30% of the design with probability 10%, 50% with 20%, 70% with 30%, 90% with 25%, 120% with 15%. That averages 74% of the design. On those odds:

- Signing the lease is worth an expected **$69,783** a year, not $129,152. Four times in ten you clear the $60,000 target with room; three times in ten you earn $12,742 or lose money.
- If the window comes in at half and you close after 18 months, about **$242,837** is gone; at 30%, about $303,593.
- Measuring first, and signing only at 70% or better, lifts the expected result if you sign to **$100,881** and avoids an expected loss of $78,927.

### The trend can fade

Crumbl's average store fell 37% in three years. The same fall at your window leaves $44,299 in Year 3; the same fall in every channel loses $36,462. The design's volumes are already below craze levels: Roll & Dough baked 700–800 rolls a day at its Miramar pop-up, and the kitchen's design averages 174 classic rolls a working day. The risk is in the lease, not in the design.

### Your rivals changed in 2026

- **Knaus Berry Farm was sold** in August 2025 to Miami investors, one of them from The Genuine Hospitality Group. It moved to 16790 SW 177th Ave, and for its 70th season it takes cards, added a register, doubled its parking and announced online orders of 4–12 dozen for pickup by car, not yet live in November; one January review mentions frozen rolls ([Local 10](https://www.local10.com/news/local/2025/08/22/knaus-berry-farm-to-reopen-in-miami-under-new-ownership-with-longtime-family-involvement); [WLRN](https://www.wlrn.org/business/2026-10-01/knaus-berry-farms-cinnamon-rolls); [CBS Miami](https://www.cbsnews.com/miami/news/knaus-berry-farm-online-ordering-cinnamon-rolls/)). The plan sold "no line" against Knaus. Under restaurant people the line gets shorter, and a pickup point nearer the suburbs is no longer far-fetched (my inference).
- **Roll & Dough proved the demand and broke on production.** It baked 700–800 large rolls a day, Thursday to Sunday, from a counter inside a Miramar patisserie, closed in the spring to hire at least three more bakers, ran a month inside a Coconut Grove yogurt shop, and went back to pre-orders baked in the owners' apartment. Reviews run 3.0–3.4 stars; the Miami-Dade shop it announced has not opened ([Miramar FL News](https://www.miramarflnews.com/lifestyle-entertainment/restaurants/article315653243.html); [Miramar FL News](https://www.miramarflnews.com/news/local/article316285946.html); [Yelp](https://www.yelp.com/biz/roll-and-dough-miramar)). The buyers exist. What was missing is what you bring: a baker's production plan.
- **Chains are arriving on your stretch.** Paris Baguette is "coming soon" at 9600 S Dixie Hwy, Levain is due in Coconut Grove, and H&H is open at Suniland ([Paris Baguette](https://parisbaguette.com/location-list/fl/miami/); [Miami New Times](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/food-drink/famed-nyc-bakery-levain-to-open-in-coconut-grove-this-fall-40528832/)).
- **No dedicated roll shop opened in Miami-Dade this year.** The slot is still empty.

## 6. The changes

**1. Measure the window before the lease.** Run a morning residency in the pop-up's season: Thursday to Sunday for 14 weeks at a counter that is dark in the morning (an ice-cream or frozen-yogurt shop, a pizzeria) on US-1 in Pinecrest or South Miami, with the host keeping about 15%. Roll & Dough's two host counters show that hosts say yes.

| Residency sales against the kitchen window's design | A residency morning | Stage 1, left in Year 1 | Cash to start |
|---|---|---|---|
| No residency (the plan) | | $15,195 | $83,453 |
| 30% | $461 | $15,321 | $90,679 |
| 50% | $768 | $23,218 | $90,679 |
| 70% | $1,075 | $31,115 | $90,679 |

*Model output: the window's rolls, boxes, loaves and brewed coffee with no espresso bar, two more crew for 6.5 hours a residency day, $4,000 of kit.* It roughly pays for itself; its value is the measurement. The gate I would set, as judgment: **$1,000 or more a morning, about 75 orders, averaged over the last six weeks, and no lower than the first six.** Ask the state how a counter inside another licensed shop is permitted.

**2. Keep the bay small and the lease short.** In a fitted 2,000 sq ft bay at $60 and $15 NNN, the kitchen leaves $13,853 at the design, and needs the window at 150% to match Dadeland Plaza's 830 sq ft. Misha's last move was into a 3,333 sq ft flagship. Ask for three years with options, a right to leave if sales stay under a stated level after month 18, and a guarantee capped at 12 months.

**3. Take the craze out of the gates.** Count repeat buyers and the last weeks against the first, not the best Saturday. The plan's gates on repeat pre-orders and on June and July stay; add the residency gate above.

**4. Hedge the product, not the brand.** Roll first, classic first, fewer flavors; then the four additions in section 4, tested through pre-orders in Stage 1.

**5. Check two purchases while the pop-up runs** (section 7).

**6. Call Roll & Dough.** They have buyers and can't bake enough; you can bake and have no buyers yet. Baking for them under contract, hiring the bakers they trained, or bringing them in are all worth one conversation. This is my idea and nothing says they'd agree.

**7. Start in February.** The craze, Knaus's season to May 9 and cheap butter all favor the first quarter of 2027.

## 7. Buying instead of building

Two listings are worth a call. Both sets of figures are unverified claims read from search results.

|---|---|---|---|
| The claim holds | $49,811 | $70,340 | $120,151 |
| Cash flow is half the claim | −$7,594 | $70,340 | $62,745 |
| The wholesale side only covers its own rent and crew | $0 | $70,340 | $70,340 |

*My arithmetic. Cash in is about $204,996: the price, the pop-up's set-up and $20,000 for the transfer. The pop-up alone costs $83,453 and leaves $39,440.* It answers three of Stage 1's open questions at once: oven time before dawn, trained bakers in a scarce year, and a permitted kitchen. A price of 1.1 times claimed earnings says the claim needs proof.

**The rule I would hold:** buy only on tax returns and bank deposits, at no more than 2.5 times earnings after a lead baker's pay. Florida food permits don't pass to a buyer, and a buyer can be liable for the seller's unpaid sales tax up to the price paid ([FDACS](https://www.fdacs.gov/Business-Services/Food-Establishments/Mobile-Food-Vendors); [Florida Statutes 213.758](https://m.flsenate.gov/Statutes/213.758)). The same business appears at several prices on different sites, so ask for the price history.

**Other leads, thinner:** a 30-year family bakery in Kendall at perhaps $300,000; a Pinecrest pizzeria with an equipped kitchen at 8237 SW 124th St for $150,000; the closed Einstein Bros. at 5946 S Dixie Hwy, whose size and rent aren't published and which is probably too big unless it can be split ([BizBuySell](https://www.bizbuysell.com/florida/miami/bakeries-for-sale/); [listing](https://property.miamibestproperty.com/idx/details/listing/d016/A12050150)). No Crumbl has closed in Miami-Dade, so there is no fitted cookie bay to take over.

## 8. What would change my mind

- **Against the roll:** Knaus opens a pickup point north of Kendall Drive or sells all year; a funded roll chain signs on US-1; the residency's last six weeks run under its first six.
- **Against building:** either purchase proves its cash flow at a price of $450,000 or less for the café, or $129,000 for the wholesale bakery.
- **Against Miami now:** the pop-up misses its gates in a craze year. If rolls don't sell in 2027, they won't in 2029.
- **For a bigger bet:** the residency clears $1,500 a morning for six weeks, which is the design at full strength.

*Model output at usual café cost shares: food and packaging 29%, rent of $70–$75 a sq ft with $15 NNN. Maman's cafés average about $1.8 million by one estimate.* A café pays its owners only when they work in it or when it sells well over $2 million, and it costs more than your limit either way.

**Are they doing poorly?** No Miami owner publishes sales or pay, so nobody outside knows. The signals split by structure. Owner-baker starts are expanding (Honey Veil, Ophelia, Façade, Caracas Bakery), and Madruga has lasted nine years without growing. What closed was big leases and single-product hype (Misha's, Chip City) and Bachour's two restaurant spin-offs. Long-running places close when the lease resets: one landlord says that when old leases expire, "landlords double the rent" ([CoStar](https://www.costar.com/article/123573420/five-years-after-pandemic-boom-miamis-restaurant-scene-may-have-bitten-off-more-than-it-can-chew)). A rough count from press coverage gives six or seven bakery and dessert openings against seven or eight closures in these towns in 2024–26; that is not a census.

**What it means.** The idea is not dead. The version with a hired production lead is the thin one, and it is the one structure with no local survivor. The path that works here is this plan's Stage 1 with the founder baking: Honey Veil went from a stall to a shop in 14 months. Baking through the pop-up and the first year, then hiring the lead as Madruga's owner did, is worth about $47,000 a year while it lasts, and it keeps production, where Roll & Dough failed, in your hands.

## 11. What this research could not see

- No page was read; every fact is from search-result text, and listing snippets often mix figures from neighboring listings.
- No Miami source measures sales by neighborhood, bookings for event carts, or what weight-loss drugs do to bakery buying here.
- No independent roll shop publishes its sales. Roll & Dough's 700–800 a day is the founders' own figure.
- The benchmarked rows use published averages for chains and my sizing of an independent's crew; treat them as order of magnitude.
- My odds for the window are judgment. The residency replaces them with a number.
- The model and the Miami page still show the plan as it was. The residency, the added products and the purchases are not in them.
