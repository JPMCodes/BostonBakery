# Miami Burger-and-Bakery Hybrid: Model Run and Verdict

Not as a cold start; worth a cheap test, and it needs changes. A counter burger shop that bakes its own buns and the cinnamon roll leaves you $77,054 in Year 2 at the best-fitting site if it sells $894,106, and $48,576 on my odds, for $487,765 of cash and about 50 hours a week on the floor. The roll kitchen leaves $129,152 at its design and $69,783 on the same kind of odds, for $312,719. The fresh-baked idea is right, though: the bakery lines earn what the shop makes, and the burgers alone break even.

*Prepared November 25, 2026. The shop is built into the database as the `burger_bakery` format in [`db/proforma.yaml`](../db/proforma.yaml), with its recipes in [`db/products`](../db/products/), and run through the same pro forma as the roll kitchen: Year 2, Miami wages, no owner's pay in the costs, no loan. Research notes: [burger_economics.md](../research_notes/Miami%20burger%20bakery%20hybrid/burger_economics.md), [local_burger_market.md](../research_notes/Miami%20burger%20bakery%20hybrid/local_burger_market.md), [sites_rules.md](../research_notes/Miami%20burger%20bakery%20hybrid/sites_rules.md); every case below is in [model_results.md](../research_notes/Miami%20burger%20bakery%20hybrid/model_results.md). The research facts come from search-result text, because listing and news sites refused page reads. Site figures are sellers' and brokers' claims. The sales levels are my judgment.*

*Model output. "My odds" for the burger shop's sales against its design: 60% of design with a 20% chance, 75% of design with a 30% chance, 90% of design with a 30% chance, 105% of design with a 15% chance, 125% of design with a 5% chance; mean 84%. The design's burger trade alone is $717,335 a year, between the median Miami restaurant for sale ($590,000) and the one burger counter listed with figures ($800,000); breakfast and the rolls are unproven on top, and five burger shops opened nearby in a year (section 6). The roll kitchen's odds are the concept comparison's.*

- **The bakery earns the money.** A plain burger shop in the same kitchen, with bought buns and no rolls or breakfast, leaves −$6,536 on $645,602 of sales.
- **It needs a franchise shop's sales.** You get nothing below $725,850, $60,000 at $856,866 and $100,000 at $944,210. The one Miami burger counter for sale with figures reports $800,000 of sales and $100,000 of cash flow, and the median Miami restaurant for sale $590,000; the median franchised MOOYAH shop sold $1,016,595 in 2025, by a third-party summary of its disclosure ([LoopNet](https://loopnet.ca/biz/Business-Opportunity/beautiful-texas-burgers-franchise-positioned-in-a-privileged-location/2041062); [BizBuySell](https://www.bizbuysell.com/florida/miami-dade-county/american-restaurants-for-sale/); [Zeereport](https://zeereport.com/reports/mooyah)).
- **Labor is the weight.** The crew costs $323,113, 36% of sales, with you unpaid on the floor. A hired general manager in your place turns $77,054 into −$44,842.
- **It takes all your cash.** $487,765 of your $500,000. If sales run at 60% of the design and it closes after 18 months, the loss is $438,248 with the kitchen resold at half its price and $512,748 with nothing recovered.
- **Changes in your hands lift it to $121,960** at the base and $91,360 on my odds (section 4).
- **The upside is the reason to test it.** At 125% of the design it leaves $230,402, and a burger that hits can be repeated: Skinny Louie has grown from one Wynwood shop to ten and plans about 30 ([QSR Magazine](https://www.qsrmagazine.com/growth/smashburger-concept-skinny-louie-grows-footprint-while-doubling-down-on-simplicity/)).

- **Crew:** 45 paid hours a day at an average $18.27: a baker from 5 am (7.5 hours), grill cooks (20.75), an evening shift lead (7.2), a cashier (5.5) and prep (4). You run the breakfast counter and the lunch pass and do not bake or cook.
- **The kitchen** needs about 1,400 sq ft for 20 seats and $117,500 of equipment before what a bought kitchen already has.

- **Keep breakfast, delivery and dinner.** Each carries a share of the rent the others cannot. Breakfast pays for its cook from about 12 sandwiches a day.
- **Bake the buns for the claim, not the saving.** The house bun costs $0.16 against $0.48–1.00 bought ([WebstaurantStore](https://www.webstaurantstore.com/martins-famous-3-1-2-sandwich-potato-rolls-case/876MAR6500520.html); [WebstaurantStore](https://www.webstaurantstore.com/turano-4-sliced-brioche-bun-case/876TUR8317.html)), and the baker's time takes most of that back. Hopdoddy employed three bakers a store for about 1,100 buns a day, by an older report ([QSR Magazine](https://www.qsrmagazine.com/food/menu-innovations/hopdoddy-burger-bar-2/)).
- **Sell the roll as a second line, not a dessert:** at the order point, in boxes, by pre-order, at breakfast. Every $100 a day more of rolls is worth about $18,494 a year.
- **Stay on the floor.** The shop does not carry a manager's salary at these sales.

## 7. Rules that change the cost

- **Take a space that is already a restaurant of the same kind.** The county's road impact fee is $79–91 a sq ft for "fast-food without drive-thru", $17–20 for "fast casual", $36–41 for a sit-down restaurant and $6–7 for strip retail, charged on the increase at a change of use ([Miami-Dade rate schedule](https://www.miamidade.gov/resources/economy/zoning/documents/impact-fee-rates.pdf)). A retail bay of 1,500 sq ft turned into a burger counter could owe $109,000–125,000. Which class the county gives a counter burger shop is not known: get it in writing before signing, at Palmetto Bay above all, where a sit-down restaurant would become a counter one.
- **The grease permit does not transfer.** A new owner applies again ([Miami-Dade](https://www.miamidade.gov/global/permit.page)). The state licence is $262 a year plus $50, with no plan review if the kitchen is unchanged ([DBPR](https://www2.myfloridalicense.com/hotels-restaurants/licensing/food-fees/)); a griddle and fryers in place of a pizza oven is a change.
- **A hood and a grease interceptor are why these kitchens cost $85,000–250,000.** New, a hood system runs $15,000–50,000 and a grease interceptor $15,000–40,000 in Miami, by contractors' guides ([Safe and Sound](https://getsafeandsound.com/blog/fire-suppression-system-cost/); [Caraballo](https://caraballoexpress.com/grease-trap-installation/)).

## 8. What I would do

1. **Do not buy a kitchen for this yet.** $48,576 on my odds for $487,765 and seven days a week is a worse trade than the roll kitchen's.
2. **Add the burger to the pop-up stage.** A flat-top griddle at a brewery or market residency, the buns and rolls from the commissary. A griddle puts it under the state's restaurant licence for mobile and temporary units, not the agriculture department's permit that covers pastry. Measure burgers an hour at the peak, the share of orders that add a roll, and whether $11.50–12.50 holds.
3. **Test the bun first.** The roll is approved; the pressed bun has not been baked or held under two patties for ten minutes in a wrap.
4. **If the burger sells out week after week,** buy a kitchen that is already a restaurant with five or more years on its lease, open with kiosks, and price above the smash-burger pack.
5. **If it only sells steadily,** the roll kitchen is the better business and the burger stays a pop-up.

## 9. What this could not see

- No local shop's sales. The sales indexes and my odds are judgments.
- No measured lift from baking buns in the shop, anywhere.
- In-store prices for most local burger shops; several anchors are delivery-app prices, which run about 30% over the counter's.
- Lease terms, sizes and equipment lists for the three sites, and the county's impact-fee class.
- Fryer and refrigeration prices are estimates; the crew plan is mine and untested.
