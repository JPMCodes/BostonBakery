# Boston Bakery Site Scan: Three Former Food Spaces to Call First

*Listings, closures and Boston license records checked on November 7, 2026. All 40 candidate records (39 sites) are in [Boston bakery site scan candidates.csv](Boston%20bakery%20site%20scan%20candidates.csv); the criteria come from the [market report](Boston%20bakery%20market%20matrix.md).*

Of 39 sites checked, 23 still look available (7 confirmed live on November 7, 16 likely live), and 15 of those score 8 or more out of 10: 10 leases and 5 businesses for sale. Call three first. **3089-3091 Washington St, Egleston Square**: live, 1,490 sq ft at $29 a sq ft (about $43,000 a year, so about $540,000 of sales keeps rent at 8%), with a hood and a grease trap on the city's inspection record. **349 Centre St, Jamaica Plain**: live, a 1,200 sq ft section at $3,950 a month on a busy corner the listing says is zoned for restaurants, though the old kitchen was probably removed. **1105 Mass Ave, Cambridge**, at the edge of Harvard Square, for a bakery-cafe: a 2,500 sq ft former diner with a very large hood and two walk-in cold rooms, apparently empty since June, but its only known rent ($35 a sq ft) is from 2024. No business for sale is a clear buy; the best, a nearly new Lexington Center cafe at $165,000, shows no sales figures, and its rent needs nearly $1 million a year in sales. Biggest caveat: nothing has been confirmed with a broker. Many details come from search snippets, rent is missing or contradictory for several of the best kitchens, and no lease space has a food license confirmed to transfer.

## What we looked for

A space of 900-3,000 sq ft: about 1,200 sq ft for a counter bakery that needs roughly $0.6-0.8 million a year in sales, or about 2,500 sq ft for a bakery-cafe with seats and lunch that needs about $1.2-1.7 million. Rent of about $30-55 per sq ft a year that stays at or under 8% of sales; multiply a site's yearly rent by 12.5 to get the sales it needs. Newbury St, prime Back Bay and the Seaport ($85 to more than $400 a sq ft) are out for a first shop unless the rent is unusually low. The best spaces are former bakeries, cafes or restaurants that already have a vented cooking hood, a grease trap, gas and enough electric power, a food license that can transfer, and zoning that already allows a bakery with takeout; such a space can open in 3-6 months, against 6-12 for raw space plus 3-6 more for a zoning appeal. The places to look are Boston's neighborhoods, the Cambridge and Somerville squares, Brookline, and inner-suburb town and shopping centers, near weekend or destination crowds rather than weekday office commuters alone. A business purchase should be a bakery, cafe, coffee shop or small restaurant with a hood, stated sales and earnings, a license that transfers and at least 5 years left on the lease, counting renewal options.

**Terms.** *NNN*: the tenant also pays its share of property tax, insurance and upkeep on top of base rent. *SDE* (seller's discretionary earnings): the owner's yearly cash flow before paying themselves. *Second-generation*: already built out for food. *Common Victualler license*: the local license to serve food. *Walk-in*: a walk-in cold room. *BRT*: Boston Restaurant Talk, a blog that tracks restaurant openings and closings. *Fit*: a score out of 10 (size 2, rent or price 2, existing food space 3, location 2, confirmed available 1). *(snippet)*: seen only in a search-result snippet, not on the listing page itself.

## Before you call a broker

| Ask or verify | What this scan found |
|---|---|
| **Hood and make-up air.** Is there a vented (Type 1) hood with fire suppression over the cooking line? How long is it, will your ovens fit under it, is make-up air (fresh air fed back in to replace what the hood pulls out) installed, and when was it last cleaned and inspected? | City inspections document hoods at 3089 Washington St, 250 Huntington Ave and 34 Harvard Ave, and the 1105 Mass Ave listing calls its hood "excessively large"; no hood is stated for the Lexington, Roslindale or Needham sites. A new hood with ducting runs about $6,000-25,000, and full systems up to about $60,000 (rough guides cited in the market report). |
| **Grease trap.** Size, location, last pump-out and last inspection result. | Inspectors found the trap at 3089 Washington St covered with plastic film and cardboard in Feb 2026. |
| **Gas and electric.** Gas line and meter size for ovens; electric service for ovens, mixers and coolers; who pays for upgrades. | Not documented for any site. |
| **Food license (Common Victualler).** Does the address hold an active license and food permit, and can they transfer to you? | None of the Boston spaces in the lease table has one to transfer; Cambridge and suburban licenses were not checked. A new Boston license costs $100 plus $1 a seat ($210 takeout-only), takes 2-6 weeks and needs a public hearing if the address has none; the health-permit plan review takes several months. |
| **Zoning.** Is a bakery with takeout already allowed at this address without a zoning appeal? | Of the eight best leases, only 349 Centre St's listing mentions zoning (it says restaurant use is allowed). An appeal adds 3-6 months. Mayor Wu's October 27, 2026 proposals would drop zoning review for takeout, but they are not rules yet. |
| **NNN charges.** Is the rent gross or NNN? Ask for last year's charges per sq ft. | Six of the eight best leases do not say. Each $10 a sq ft of NNN on 1,200 sq ft adds about $12,000 a year, or about $150,000 of extra sales at 8%. |
| **Tenant-improvement allowance.** Will the landlord pay toward the build-out, or give free rent while you build? | No listing seen mentions one. |
| **Term and options.** At least 5 years plus renewal options; size of yearly increases. | Few listings state a term: Roslindale (5 + 5 years, 3% a year), the Medford sale (~5 + 5 years) and the waterfront cafe (6-7 years left). 1191 Centre St, 1326 Mass Ave and 34 Harvard Ave mention only a renewal option, and the Davis Square cafe's lease may end in Oct 2029. |
| **Signage.** What sign is allowed on the front? | Rarely stated. |
| **Hours.** Any lease or license limits on early-morning baking, deliveries or late hours? | Not stated in any listing seen. |
| **History.** Why did the last tenant leave, and how long has the space been empty? | 1191 Centre St: ~591 days on market. 250 Huntington Ave: dark since late 2024. 1105 Mass Ave: two operators closed in two years. |
| **For a business purchase.** Tax returns or bank records that back up sales and SDE; a copy of the lease and the landlord's consent to transfer it; a list of the equipment that stays. | Only 3 of the 14 businesses show both sales and owner earnings. |

**Checking a Boston address on data.boston.gov** (Boston addresses only; Cambridge, Somerville, Brookline and the suburbs keep their own records):

1. **Past and present food businesses.** Open [Food Establishment Inspections](https://data.boston.gov/dataset/food-establishment-inspections) and search the street, trying a few numbers either side (The Draft's 34 Harvard Ave space is filed under number 32). Each record shows the business name, license number and status, and inspectors' comments; search the comments for "hood", "grease", "exhaust", "fryer" and "walk-in".
2. **Who holds a food permit now.** Check the [Active Food Establishment Licenses](https://data.boston.gov/api/3/action/datastore_search?resource_id=f1e13724-284d-478c-b8bc-ef042aa5b70b) list.
3. **Common Victualler license.** Open [Licensing Board Licenses](https://data.boston.gov/dataset/licensing-board-licenses). Each license describes the premises (square footage, seats, where the kitchen is) and carries board notes such as "temporarily closed".
4. **Read with care.** A shop that closed this year can still show Active until year-end renewal; Inactive can also mean a new owner under a new license number; and a license description shows the space as it was when licensed, not as it is today.

## Caveats

- **Coverage.** The four sweeps turned up 261 distinct leads (lease listings, businesses for sale and vacated food spaces); only the 40 scoring highest on size, rent, kitchen evidence, location and recency were checked in depth. The other 221 are listed with links in the [sweep notes](../research_notes/Boston%20bakery%20site%20scan/) (`sweep_*.md`).
- **Snippets.** LoopNet, Crexi, CityFeet, Showcase, CommercialCafe, BizQuest and BizBuySell block automated reading, so details from those sites come from search-result snippets, marked "(snippet)". The search tool's summaries sometimes attach a figure to the wrong listing; conflicting figures are shown side by side. Only boston.com listings and articles, Boston Restaurant Talk (BRT) posts and Boston's city data were read directly.
- **Listings change daily.** Several are undated or months old, and four business listings (Medford, Watertown, Winchester and the Harvard Square-area cafe) could not be found again. Confirm availability first.
- **Nothing checked with a broker.** No broker or landlord was contacted, and no hood, grease trap, license transfer or lease document was inspected. Rents are asking rents; NNN charges often come on top.
- **License history covers Boston only.** Licenses in Cambridge, Somerville, Brookline and the suburbs were not checked, and one local source (uphamscorner.org, for 750 Dudley St) could not be reached.
- **Benchmarks are national** and mostly asking prices; the sales targets are estimates from the market report, since no independent Boston bakery publishes its sales.
- **CSV.** Blank numbers mean "not stated". The Lexington Center cafe appears twice (two records of the same listing).
